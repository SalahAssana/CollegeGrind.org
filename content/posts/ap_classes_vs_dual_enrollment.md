If you are a high school student looking to get a head start on college, you have probably heard a lot about Advanced Placement (AP) classes and Dual Enrollment (DE). Both programs let you tackle college-level work while you are still in high school. They can save you thousands of dollars in tuition, help you graduate early, and make your college applications stand out.

Choosing between AP and Dual Enrollment can feel overwhelming. Should you take an AP class that finishes with a big exam in May? Or should you take a [Dual Enrollment class](https://www.flvs.net/curriculum/accelerated-programs/dual-enrollment) through a local college?

The short answer is that neither option is automatically better for every student. Dual Enrollment has a huge practical advantage because you earn college credit by passing the class, not by taking one high-stakes test. However, AP classes are often recognized more consistently by out-of-state and [selective private universities](https://admissions.dartmouth.edu/glossary-question/does-dartmouth-grant-credit-ap-level-ib-or-clep-examinations). Understanding how both programs work will help you choose the best fit for your goals.

## **The Basics: How Each Program Works**

Before comparing the two, it helps to understand how each program awards college credit.

```
                  +---------------------------------------------------+  
                  |            HIGH SCHOOL CREDIT OPTIONS             |  
                  +-------------------------+-------------------------+  
                                            |  
                   +------------------------+------------------------+  
                   |                                                 |  
                   v                                                 v  
    +------------------------------+                  +------------------------------+  
    |    ADVANCED PLACEMENT (AP)   |                  |     DUAL ENROLLMENT (DE)     |  
    +------------------------------+                  +------------------------------+  
    | High school course           |                  | Actual college course        |  
    | Standardized nationwide      |                  | College or high school setting|  
    | Single end-of-year AP exam   |                  | Continuous term grading      |  
    | Score 3, 4, or 5 for credit  |                  | Grade 'C' or higher for credit|  
    +------------------------------+                  +------------------------------+

```

### **Advanced Placement (AP)**

AP classes are high school courses created by the [College Board](https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/exam-policies-guidelines/exam-fees). They follow a standardized curriculum taught by your high school teachers. At the end of the school year in May, you take a standardized AP exam scored from 1 to 5.

Your grade in the high school class goes on your high school transcript. However, that class grade does not give you college credit. To earn college credit, you must earn a qualifying score on the AP exam. Most colleges award credit for [scores of 3, 4, or 5](https://catalog.ufl.edu/UGRD/academic-advising/exam-credit/), depending on their [individual credit policies](https://ufonline.ufl.edu/apply/transfer-credit/).

### **Dual Enrollment (DE)**

[Dual Enrollment](https://www.flvs.net/curriculum/accelerated-programs/dual-enrollment) is a partnership between your high school and a local college or university. You enroll in an actual college course taught by a college professor or an approved high school teacher. Classes may take place on your high school campus, online, or directly on the college campus.

When you finish the course with a passing grade—usually a C or better—you earn both high school credit toward graduation and real college credit. That credit is recorded directly on an [official college transcript](https://bulletin.stanford.edu/academic-polices/transfer-test-credit/undergraduate).

## **Comparing AP and Dual Enrollment Side-by-Side**

To see how these programs compare, look at how they grade your work, what they cost, and how easily credits transfer.

| Feature | Advanced Placement (AP) | Dual Enrollment (DE) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **How You Earn Credit** | Earn a qualifying score (3, 4, or 5) on a single [end-of-year exam](https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/exam-policies-guidelines/exam-fees). | Pass the college course with a qualifying grade (usually a [C or higher](https://bulletin.stanford.edu/academic-polices/transfer-test-credit/undergraduate)). |
| **Grading Style** | High-stakes test on a single day in May. | Continuous grading over a full semester (tests, papers, homework). |
| **Curriculum Standard** | Same standardized curriculum across the nation. | Set by the local college or community college syllabus via [participating programs](https://www.flvs.net/curriculum/accelerated-programs/dual-enrollment). |
| **Standard Cost** | [$99 per exam](https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/exam-policies-guidelines/exam-fees) in the U.S. and Canada. | Often free or low-cost through public high school agreements. |
| **Financial Assistance** | [Reduced fee of $53](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/exam-administration-ordering-scores/ordering-fees/exam-fees/reductions) per exam for eligible low-income students. | Often fully funded by state or district grants for public school students. |
| **College Recognition** | Widely recognized across public, private, and [selective colleges](https://catalog.ufl.edu/UGRD/academic-advising/exam-credit/). | Highly accepted by in-state public universities; policies vary at selective private colleges. |

## **The Practical Advantage of Dual Enrollment: No High-Stakes Test**

The biggest advantage of Dual Enrollment is how credit is awarded.

In an AP class, your ability to earn college credit comes down to a single test on a single day. You could earn an "A" in your AP class all year long, but if you get sick, freeze up, or have a bad day during the [May exam](https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/exam-policies-guidelines/exam-fees), you might score a 2. If that happens, you get no college credit for a full year of hard work.

Dual Enrollment removes that single point of failure. You earn college credit the same way actual college students do: by doing the work over an entire semester. Your grade is based on a mix of homework, quizzes, essays, midterms, and final projects. If you study hard and stay on top of your assignments, you can comfortably secure a passing grade and guarantee your [transferable college credit](https://bulletin.stanford.edu/academic-polices/transfer-test-credit/undergraduate) before the semester even ends.

## **Costs and Fee Reductions**

Cost is another key factor for families. Both programs are much cheaper than paying full college tuition, but their fee structures differ.

### **AP Exam Fees**

Taking an AP class in school is free, but taking the AP exam costs money.

| AP Fee Category | Details & Cost |
| --- | --- |
| **Standard Exam Fee** | [$99 per exam](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fapstudents.collegeboard.org%2Fexam-policies-guidelines%2Fexam-fees) in the U.S., U.S. territories, Canada, and DoDEA schools. |
| **International Exam Fee** | [$129 per exam](https://www.savemyexams.com/learning-hub/exam-guides/cost-of-ap-exams/) outside the U.S. and Canada. |
| **College Board Fee Reduction** | [$37 discount per exam](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fapcentral.collegeboard.org%2Fexam-administration-ordering-scores%2Fordering-fees%2Fexam-fees%2Freductions) for eligible low-income students. |
| **Net Low-Income Cost** | [$53 per exam](https://scholarships360.org/college-admissions/how-much-are-ap-tests/) (schools waive their $9 rebate). |
| **State Subsidies** | Many states offer [state-level fee assistance](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/exam-administration-ordering-scores/ordering-fees/exam-fees/state-assistance) to cover the remaining $53, making exams $0 for qualifying students. |
| **Late or Canceled Exam Fee** | [$40 extra fee](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/exam-administration-ordering-scores/ordering-fees/exam-fees) for ordering late or canceling an exam after November 14. |

Low-income eligibility for AP fee reductions is based on federal income guidelines or participation in programs like free or reduced-price school lunch, SNAP, or TANF, as outlined in the [College Board Fee Reduction Eligibility Form](https://d16k74nzx9emoe.cloudfront.net/f3361592-d61a-41ef-a25c-46c422b4d140/25-26%20Fee%20Reduction%20Eligibility%20Form.pdf).

### **Dual Enrollment Costs**

In many states, [Dual Enrollment](https://www.flvs.net/curriculum/accelerated-programs/dual-enrollment) is completely free for public high school students. State programs often cover tuition, college fees, and even textbooks. In places where parents pay tuition out-of-pocket, community colleges typically charge discounted per-credit rates, making it an affordable way to earn college credits.

## **How Colleges Treat Your Credits: In-State vs. Out-of-State**

How colleges accept your credits depends heavily on the type of college you want to attend.

### **Public In-State Universities**

If you plan to attend a public university in your home state, Dual Enrollment is hard to beat. Most states have articulation agreements—laws that guarantee credits earned at local community colleges transfer directly to state universities.

For example, at the [University of Florida (UF)](https://ufonline.ufl.edu/apply/transfer-credit/), students who earn an Associate of Arts (A.A.) degree through Dual Enrollment can transfer up to 60 credits directly toward their bachelor's degree. UF also accepts AP scores of 3 or higher for college credit, up to a maximum cap of 45 test credits based on their [exam credit chart](https://catalog.ufl.edu/UGRD/academic-advising/exam-credit/).

### **Out-of-State and Selective Private Colleges**

If you plan to apply to selective private universities or out-of-state colleges, AP classes often have a clear advantage. Selective colleges know exactly what an AP class covers because the curriculum is identical nationwide.

Private universities often have strict policies regarding Dual Enrollment transfer credits:

| Institution | AP Credit Policy | Dual Enrollment Credit Policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **University of Florida** | Accepts scores of 3, 4, or 5; awards credit up to a [45-credit test limit](https://catalog.ufl.edu/UGRD/academic-advising/exam-credit/). | Accepts up to 60 transfer credits from [in-state public colleges](https://ufonline.ufl.edu/apply/transfer-credit/). |
| **Stanford University** | Accepts select AP scores of 4 or 5; max combined limit of 45 quarter units according to [Stanford Student Services](https://studentservices.stanford.edu/my-academics/earn-my-degree/undergraduate-degree-progress/test-transfer-credit/external-test-0). | Evaluates courses individually per the [Stanford Bulletin](https://bulletin.stanford.edu/academic-polices/transfer-test-credit/undergraduate); **will not award credit** if the course satisfied high school diploma requirements. Requires C- or better. |
| **Dartmouth College** | Awards "credit on entrance" for qualifying scores to allow course exemptions based on [Dartmouth regulations](https://dartmouth.smartcatalogiq.com/en/2022/orc/regulations/undergraduate-study/requirements-for-the-degree-of-bachelor-of-arts/credits-and-proficiencies-on-entrance). | Examines credits strictly; pre-college credits **do not reduce** the 35 credits needed to graduate per [Dartmouth Admissions](https://admissions.dartmouth.edu/glossary-question/does-dartmouth-grant-credit-ap-level-ib-or-clep-examinations). |

[Stanford University](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/transfer/credit.html), for instance, caps transfer credits at 45 quarter units. Furthermore, Stanford will not grant transfer credit for a Dual Enrollment course if that course was used to satisfy any high school graduation requirement according to their [undergraduate credit policies](https://bulletin.stanford.edu/academic-polices/transfer-test-credit/undergraduate). The class must be an extra college class taken above and beyond your secondary diploma requirements.

Dartmouth College grants course exemptions for strong AP scores, letting you skip introductory classes. However, Dartmouth's policy specifies that pre-college credits [do not reduce the 35 total course credits](https://dartmouth.smartcatalogiq.com/en/2022/orc/regulations/undergraduate-study/requirements-for-the-degree-of-bachelor-of-arts/credits-and-proficiencies-on-entrance) required to graduate.

## **How to Choose: Which Option Fits You Best?**

To decide whether AP or Dual Enrollment makes more sense, evaluate your goals, learning style, and college preferences.

### **Dual Enrollment may be better for you if:**

* You plan to attend an in-state public college or community college where [credits transfer smoothly](https://ufonline.ufl.edu/apply/transfer-credit/).
* You get anxious during big standardized tests and prefer being graded on continuous semester work per standard [college credit policies](https://bulletin.stanford.edu/academic-polices/transfer-test-credit/undergraduate).
* You want to take specialized classes that your high school does not offer, such as Psychology, Sociology, Criminal Justice, or Advanced Math through local [accelerated learning options](https://www.flvs.net/curriculum/accelerated-programs/dual-enrollment).
* You want to experience real college workloads, learn how to navigate college portals, and interact with college professors.

### **AP may be better for you if:**

* You are aiming for selective private universities or top out-of-state colleges known for generous [AP credit policies](https://www.cuemath.com/blog/top-colleges-that-give-credit-for-ap-exam-scores/).
* You need the course to fulfill high school graduation requirements, and your target college rejects dual enrollment classes that count toward high school diplomas under their [transfer credit guidelines](https://bulletin.stanford.edu/academic-polices/transfer-test-credit/undergraduate).
* You are a strong test-taker who performs well on [standardized exams](https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/exam-policies-guidelines/exam-fees).
* You prefer staying on your high school campus without worrying about transportation, commuting, or fitting college schedules around high school sports.

## **Practical Action Steps for Students and Parents**

If you are trying to make a final decision, follow these simple steps:

1. **Check Your Target Colleges' Policies**: Look up the transfer credit rules on the admissions or registrar websites for three or four colleges you might like to attend, such as reviewing an official [UF Transfer Credit guide](https://ufonline.ufl.edu/apply/transfer-credit/). Search for their "AP Credit Chart" and "Transfer Credit Policy."
2. **Talk to Your High School Counselor**: Ask how [Dual Enrollment options](https://www.flvs.net/curriculum/accelerated-programs/dual-enrollment) work in your district. Find out if your state pays for tuition and books, and ask which local colleges partner with your school.
3. **Be Honest About Your Learning Style**: Think about how you handle tests. If taking a single cumulative exam in May sounds stressful, Dual Enrollment offers a lower-risk path to college credit compared to standard [AP exam scoring](https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/exam-policies-guidelines/exam-fees).
4. **Consider Mixing Both**: You do not have to choose just one pathway. Many successful students participate in discussions on [college planning forums](https://talk.collegeconfidential.com/t/ap-and-dual-enrollment-credits/490933) about taking AP for core high school subjects like English or U.S. History, while taking Dual Enrollment for electives or advanced math courses.

## **Which Path Fits Your College Strategy?**

Both AP and Dual Enrollment offer effective paths to college credit while you are still in high school. Dual Enrollment provides a practical advantage by letting you earn credit through classroom grades over a full semester, avoiding the pressure of a single test day based on standard [transfer credit evaluation policies](https://bulletin.stanford.edu/academic-polices/transfer-test-credit/undergraduate). It is an excellent choice for students staying in-state for college with established [articulation pathways](https://ufonline.ufl.edu/apply/transfer-credit/). On the other hand, AP classes offer a nationally standardized curriculum that carries wide recognition at [selective private universities](https://www.cuemath.com/blog/top-colleges-that-give-credit-for-ap-exam-scores/) across the country. By matching each program's strengths to your personal learning style and college plans, you can build a high school schedule that saves both time and money.
