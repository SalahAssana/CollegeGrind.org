## Key Points

* **The Transition from EFC to SAI:** Many programs are currently updating their eligibility metrics to align with the new Student Aid Index (SAI) established by the FAFSA Simplification Act, which replaces the former Expected Family Contribution (EFC).  
* **The Last-Dollar Mechanism:** The vast majority of these programs operate as [last-dollar scholarships](https://www.tbr.edu/initiatives/tn-promise). This means they apply only after federal Pell Grants and state grants are credited to the student’s account; the university then covers the remaining gap up to the cost of tuition.  
* **Income vs. Assets:** Eligibility is rarely determined by income alone. Most programs, particularly at elite private institutions, specify that families must have "typical assets" for their income level, meaning significant home equity or liquid savings can disqualify students even if their income falls below the threshold.  
* **Residency and Enrollment Strictness:** [Public university guarantees](https://www.utsystem.edu/promise-plus) are almost universally restricted to in-state residents and often require full-time enrollment (at least 12 to 15 credits per semester) and the maintenance of a specific GPA.  
* **The Middle-Class Expansion:** There is a [visible trend among top-tier private universities](https://www.road2college.com/free-tuition-colleges/) to expand their "free tuition" thresholds to include families earning up to $200,000 or even $250,000, recognizing the "middle-class squeeze" where families earn too much for traditional need-based aid bUniversity of Texas too little to afford a $90,000 annual cost of attendance.  
* **Post-Graduation Strings:** Some state programs, most notably [New York’s Excelsior Scholarship](https://www.brockport.edu/scholarships-aid/excelsior/), require recipients to live and work within the state for a period of time equal to the duration of the scholarship, effectively converting the grant into a loan if the student leaves the state after graduation.  
* **Work and Service as Currency:** Several mission-driven colleges ([Work Colleges](https://www.sofi.com/learn/content/colleges-with-free-college-tuition/)) and federal service academies require students to pay for their education through labor or military service commitments rather than financial capital.  
* **Fixed Tuition vs. Free Tuition:** Some states, like North Carolina, use an [NC Promise](https://www.northcarolina.edu/impact/affordability-efficiency/nc-promise/) model that drastically lowers the cost (e.g., $500 per semester) so that it is essentially covered by standard federal aid even if not explicitly labeled as "free."

The cost of going to college in the United States keeps rising, and it has become harder for families to afford a four‑year degree. Tuition has increased faster than inflation for decades, contributing to a national student debt crisis that affects several generations. In response, many colleges and states have created “free tuition” programs. These programs vary widely: some are large state‑funded Promise programs, while others are offered by private universities with large endowments.  

This report provides a straightforward list to these programs at four‑year universities. It explains how they work and organizes them in one place to help students, families, and counselors understand their options. Community colleges are not included so the focus stays on four‑year institutions.

### The Sustainability of the Free Tuition Model

The trend of "Tuition Guarantees" is accelerating. Since 2019, dozens of flagship universities have [established or significantly raised their income thresholds](https://www.road2college.com/free-tuition-colleges/). This is driven by two factors: the competitive need to attract top students from diverse backgrounds and the legislative recognition that high-tuition models are politically untenable.

However, the long-term sustainability of these programs depends on two different mechanisms. For private elite schools, it is the performance of their endowments. For public universities, it is the continued support of state legislatures. As [seen in Berea College](https://www.berea.edu/news/npr-features-berea-colleges-no-tuition-policy-and-labor-program), mission-driven funding models require constant stewardship. Similarly, as observed in [Maine's community college system](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/article/maine-community-colleges-optimistic-about-tuition-free-future-after-cost-cuts-surging), programs that begin as "pilots" must constantly prove their popularity and economic impact to be made permanent. For students currently in the pipeline, these programs represent a unique, time-sensitive opportunity to obtain a world-class education without the lifelong burden of student debt.

### Understanding Limitations and Maintenance

Accessing these programs is only the first step; maintaining eligibility requires strict adherence to academic and administrative rules.

1. **The Credit Pace:** Almost every public guarantee (SUNY, U-Michigan, UT System) requires students to complete 30 credits per academic year. This is to ensure [on-time graduation in four years](https://www.brockport.edu/scholarships-aid/excelsior/). Failing to meet this pace typically results in the permanent loss of the guarantee.
2. **The FAFSA Renewal:** Because eligibility is re-evaluated annually based on income, students must [file the FAFSA every single year](https://www.uvm.edu/studentfinancialservices/uvm-promise). A significant jump in family income (e.g., a parent returning to the workforce) can disqualify a student for the following year.
3. **GPA Requirements:** While admission is the primary hurdle, maintaining a specific GPA (usually 2.0 to 2.5 for public schools, or 3.0 to 3.5 for honors programs like Macaulay) is a [mandatory condition for renewal](https://www.uvm.edu/studentfinancialservices/uvm-promise).
4. **Age and First-Degree Caps:** Most "[Promise](https://www.citizensbank.com/student/articles/states-that-offer-programs-for-free-or-reduced-cost-community-college.aspx)" programs are restricted to students under the age of 24 who are pursuing their first bachelor's degree. Adult learners or those seeking a second degree generally do not qualify.
5. **Summer and Winter Exclusions:** The vast majority of these scholarships apply [only to the Fall and Spring semesters](https://www.uvm.edu/studentfinancialservices/uvm-promise). Taking extra classes in the summer usually results in an out-of-pocket tuition bill.

### How To Use This List

To effectively utilize this list, one must understand that "free tuition" is a specific financial term that does not equate to a "free college experience." The total Cost of Attendance (COA) includes tuition, mandatory fees, housing, food (meal plans), books, supplies, and personal expenses. Most programs listed here cover only the "tuition" or "tuition and mandatory fees" portion.6

The gap between "free tuition" and a "full ride" can be substantial, often ranging from $15,000 to $25,000 per year for room and board. Students should prioritize schools where the income threshold for "no parent contribution" or "full COA coverage" matches their family's financial profile. Furthermore, the application process for these programs is rarely a separate scholarship application; in most cases, simply filing the FAFSA and the CSS Profile by the university's priority deadline is the mechanism for consideration.

### The State University of New York (SUNY) and City University of New York (CUNY)

New York’s [Excelsior Scholarship](https://suny.oneonta.edu/cost-aid/types-aid/scholarships/excelsior-scholarship) is a pioneer in large-scale state tuition coverage. It is a "last-dollar" program designed to bridge the gap between state TAP (Tuition Assistance Program) grants, federal Pell grants, and the total cost of tuition.

* **University Name:** All SUNY and CUNY 4-Year Colleges (e.g., Stony Brook, University at Buffalo, Baruch College, Hunter College, CCNY)  
* **Program Name:** Excelsior Scholarship  
* **Program Type:** Statewide Last-Dollar Guarantee  
* **What It Covers:** Full tuition (up to $7,070 for SUNY or $6,930 for CUNY)  
* **Income Threshold:** $125,000 or less (Combined Federal AGI)  
* **Eligibility Requirements:** [New York State resident](https://www.brockport.edu/scholarships-aid/excelsior/) (12 consecutive months); U.S. citizen or eligible non-citizen; earn 30 credits per year successively; maintain continuous enrollment  
* **Where It Applies:** Any SUNY or CUNY two- or four-year institution  
* **Key Limitations / Notes:** [Post-graduation residency requirement](https://www.ccny.cuny.edu/financialaid/excelsior-scholarship)—recipients must live and work in NY for the same number of years they received the award. If they leave early, the award converts to a no-interest loan  
* **Application Process:** Complete FAFSA, then the NYS TAP application, then the [specific Excelsior Scholarship application](https://www.suny.edu/smarttrack/types-of-financial-aid/scholarships/excelsior/) on the HESC website

* **Income Threshold:** None (based on merit)
* **Eligibility Requirements:** High school GPA; outstanding standardized test scores (though some campuses are test-optional); [must maintain a 3.5 GPA](https://www.collegeadvisor.com/resources/macaulay-honors-college/) while in college
* **Key Limitations / Notes:** Only available to [New York State residents](https://www.brooklyn.edu/honors-academy/macaulay/); students must meet residency requirements for in-state tuition to receive the full scholarship
