## Key Points

- State financial aid can add thousands of dollars on top of federal Pell Grants and school-based aid, often making in‑state options dramatically cheaper.
- Most state programs require that you be a legal resident of the state and attend an in‑state public or participating private college.
- Many high‑value programs are “need‑based,” using your FAFSA (or state aid form) to decide eligibility and award size.
- Merit‑based and lottery‑funded programs reward GPA and test scores, sometimes covering most or all tuition for strong students (for example, Cal Grants, HOPE, Bright Futures, APS).
- Deadlines can be early (often February–March of senior year) or “ASAP until funds run out,” so filing the FAFSA and any state forms early is critical.
- Many programs are “last‑dollar,” meaning they cover remaining tuition after Pell and other grants, which is especially powerful for low‑income students (e.g., Arizona Promise, Arkansas Future, Delaware SEED, HOPE Career Grant add‑ons).
- Awards are often renewable if you maintain a minimum college GPA, complete a certain number of credits each year, and refile the FAFSA or state application on time.
- Some states (Colorado’s COF, Georgia’s GTEG and HOPE Career Grant) offer modest but easy‑to‑access stipends or grants that too many students simply never claim.

***

State financial aid is money from your state government that helps pay for college costs such as tuition and fees, usually without needing to be repaid if you meet program rules.

Unlike federal aid, which is based on national rules and always starts with the FAFSA, state programs are created by each state, have their own eligibility criteria, and often target residents attending in‑state schools.

Many students miss out on state aid because they are unaware these programs exist, assume the FAFSA is only for federal aid, or miss priority deadlines that come months before their college choice is final.

Need‑based state grants can significantly increase college access and persistence, especially for lower‑income students, but only if students apply correctly and on time.

This article is part 1 of a 50‑state guide and covers Alabama through Georgia in a consistent format so you can quickly scan your state and compare it to others.

## How to Use This Guide

- Find your state in the alphabetical list
- In each state section, read the “Overview” first to see whether aid is mainly need‑based, merit‑based, or mixed.
- Under “Major State Grant Programs,” focus on:
    - Residency requirement
    - Income/need or GPA/test requirements
    - Award ranges
    - Whether FAFSA or a separate state form is required
    - Credit‑hour and GPA rules for renewal
- Under “Notable or Unique Programs,” look for:
    - “Free tuition” or “tuition‑free” language
    - “Last‑dollar” programs that fill in gaps after other aid
    - Special pathways like technical or high‑demand career programs
- Always confirm details (deadlines, award amounts, rules) on the official state or college websites, because amounts and deadlines can change every year.

## Alabama

### Overview

Alabama’s state aid system is moderate, with a core need-based grant, a non-need grant for private colleges, and a few [targeted scholarships](https://www.collegescholarships.org/states/alabama.htm). Aid leans slightly toward [need-based support](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/list/by-state/alabama/) at a wide range of institutions, but there is no statewide “free tuition” or last-dollar Promise program.

### Major State Aid Programs

**Alabama Student Assistance Program (ASAP)**
Type: [Need-based grant](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/list/by-state/alabama/).
Award Amount: Approximately 300–5,000 dollars per year, depending on need and funding.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Alabama resident.
- Undergraduate student at an [eligible Alabama institution](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/list/by-state/alabama/).
- Demonstrated financial need (based on FAFSA Student Aid Index).
- Typically must be enrolled at least half-time.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Eligible in-state public universities.
- Community colleges.
- Participating [private institutions in Alabama](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/list/by-state/alabama/).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Funding is limited and awards vary each year.
- Awards are made by participating institutions based on available state allocation.
- Renewal depends on continued financial need and satisfactory academic progress.

Application Process:

- [FAFSA required](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/list/by-state/alabama/); no separate state ASAP application.
- Student lists an eligible Alabama institution on the FAFSA; the school packages ASAP if the student qualifies.

Deadline:

- Follow federal/state FAFSA timeline; applying as early as possible increases chances due to limited funds.

***

**Alabama Student Grant Program**
Type: Non-need-based [state grant for private colleges](https://www.scholarships.com/financial-aid/college-scholarships/scholarships-by-state/alabama-scholarships).
Award Amount: Up to 3,000 dollars per academic year; actual amount depends on annual appropriation.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Alabama resident.
- Undergraduate student at an eligible [independent (private) Alabama college](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/list/by-state/alabama/) or university.
- Not based on financial need.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Only at eligible independent (private, nonprofit) Alabama institutions.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Limited to participating private colleges.
- Funding is limited and can change year to year.
- Must meet institutional satisfactory academic progress standards.

Application Process:

- Application is typically handled through the [financial aid office](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/list/by-state/alabama/) at the student’s private Alabama college.
- FAFSA is usually required by the college to coordinate aid, even though the grant itself is non-need-based.

Deadline:

- Set by term and by the institution; students should check their college’s financial aid office for current dates.

**CollegeCounts Scholarship (State Treasurer)**
Type: Need-based / merit-informed scholarship (hybrid).
Award Amount: Varies by year and funding; provides significant assistance toward [tuition and fees at Alabama colleges](https://treasury.alabama.gov/programs/collegecounts-scholarship/).

Eligibility Requirements:

- Alabama resident.
- High school senior or first-time college freshman beginning in the specified fall term.
- Planning to enroll in an [eligible Alabama educational institution](https://treasury.alabama.gov/programs/collegecounts-scholarship/).
- Demonstrated financial need.
- Minimum GPA 2.75.
- ACT score of 29 or below (ACT not required for students attending two-year schools).

Where It Can Be Used:

- Eligible in-state public universities.
- Community colleges.
- Some private institutions within Alabama that meet program criteria.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Focused on first-time freshmen.
- Award renewal typically requires continued enrollment and academic progress.

Application Process:

- Separate online scholarship application through the [Office of the Alabama State Treasurer](https://treasury.alabama.gov/programs/collegecounts-scholarship/).
- FAFSA required to document financial need.

Deadline:

- Annual cycle; deadlines set by the Treasurer’s office (generally late winter or early spring—check current application year).

### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

- Numerous state-connected but privately administered scholarships (e.g., [Smith Scholarship Foundation](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/alabama/), Alabama GI Dependents’ Scholarship) provide significant aid to specific groups but are more niche in scope.

### Key Insights for This State

- ASAP is the primary lever for low-income students; [FAFSA completion early in the cycle](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/list/by-state/alabama/) is critical because funds are limited and campus-allocated.
- Alabama Student Grant can be valuable “extra” money for residents at private colleges because it is not need-based and stacks with other aid.
- The state lacks a broad last-dollar Promise program, so students must combine state, federal, and institutional aid creatively.
- Deadlines vary across programs and are often [handled by institutions](https://treasury.alabama.gov/programs/collegecounts-scholarship/), so missing a college’s internal priority date can cost students state aid eligibility.

## Alaska

### Overview

Alaska’s aid system is relatively generous for residents who stay in-state, combining a [robust need-based grant](https://acpe.alaska.gov/Funding-Solutions/Alaska-Education-Grant) with a [high-value merit scholarship](https://acpe.alaska.gov/Funding-Solutions/Alaska-Performance-Scholarship). Aid is split between the core Alaska Education Grant (AEG) and the Alaska Performance Scholarship (APS), which rewards strong academics with [up to 7,000 dollars per year](https://acpe.alaska.gov/portals/0/APS/Pubs/2025_APS_OutcomesReport.pdf).

### Major State Aid Programs

**Alaska Education Grant (AEG)**
Type: [Need-based grant](https://www.scholarships.com/scholarships/alaskadvantage-education-grant).
Award Amount: [500–4,000 dollars](https://acpe.alaska.gov/Funding-Solutions/Alaska-Education-Grant) per academic year.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Alaska resident for at least 365 days before filing the FAFSA.
- U.S. citizen or permanent resident.
- High school diploma or GED.
- Enrolled at least half-time in an [eligible Alaska postsecondary institution](https://acpe.alaska.gov/Funding-Solutions/Alaska-Education-Grant).
- Undergraduate student (no prior bachelor’s degree).
- Demonstrated financial need based on Student Aid Index (SAI) from FAFSA.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [Participating Alaska institutions](https://bigfuture.collegeboard.org/scholarships/alaska-education-grant), including University of Alaska campuses and eligible private or vocational schools in-state.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Awards prioritized by prior-year recipients, FAFSA submission date, and enrollment level.
- Grants are awarded until funds are exhausted; not all eligible students receive funding.
- Receipt in one year does not guarantee awards in future years.

Application Process:

- [FAFSA is the sole application](https://www.scholarships.com/scholarships/alaskadvantage-education-grant); student must list at least one qualifying Alaska institution.
- An AEG intent form may be required after initial eligibility screening.

Deadline:

- Official deadline typically [June 30](https://www.scholarships.com/scholarships/alaskadvantage-education-grant) for the relevant aid year, but early FAFSA submission is strongly encouraged for prioritization.

***

**Alaska Performance Scholarship (APS)**
Type: Merit-based, [tiered scholarship](https://acpe.alaska.gov/Funding-Solutions/Alaska-Performance-Scholarship).
Award Amount: Three levels, currently up to [7,000, 5,250, or 3,500 dollars](https://www.alaska.edu/news/did-you-know/2024-did-you-know-UA-scholarships-HB148.php) per year depending on GPA and curriculum/test level; total up to 28,000 dollars over multiple years.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Alaska high school graduate meeting a [rigorous curriculum](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/get_documents.asp?docid=15460).
- Minimum GPA thresholds (e.g., roughly 2.5–3.5+) tied to three award levels.
- Qualifying standardized test score or higher GPA, depending on level.
- Must enroll in an eligible Alaska postsecondary institution.

Where It Can Be Used:

- In-state public universities, community campuses, and some eligible career/technical schools in Alaska.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Awards are limited to a set number of years and total dollar maximum (around 28,000 dollars).
- Award levels were [raised in 2024](https://www.alaska.edu/news/did-you-know/2024-did-you-know-UA-scholarships-HB148.php) to provide more substantial support for top-tier students.
- Recent law changes added a [“step-up” provision](https://www.alaska.edu/news/did-you-know/2024-did-you-know-UA-scholarships-HB148.php) allowing students to move to a higher award level if they raise their college GPA.
- Time to use the scholarship has been extended (e.g., from six to eight years after high school) to accommodate nontraditional paths.

Application Process:

- FAFSA completion is required.
- High school and the state track curriculum, GPA, and test scores; eligibility notifications are provided as early as the [end of junior year](https://www.alaska.edu/news/did-you-know/2024-did-you-know-UA-scholarships-HB148.php).

Deadline:

- Follow Alaska Department of Education/ACPE timelines; students should complete FAFSA and ensure high school records are submitted by state-published dates.

### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

- University of Alaska institutional scholarships (such as [Nanook Pledge](https://www.uaf.edu/finaid/akresident.php)) offer multi-year institutional aid to residents but are campus-based, not statewide programs.

### Key Insights for This State

- Alaska is strong for in-state stayers: combining APS and AEG can [significantly reduce net tuition](https://acpe.alaska.gov/portals/0/APS/Pubs/2025_APS_OutcomesReport.pdf) and fees at Alaska institutions.
- Both main programs require staying in-state; students who leave Alaska for college forfeit substantial state support.
- APS is highly GPA- and curriculum-sensitive, so course selection and grades in high school directly affect award level.
- AEG funds are first-come, need-prioritized; missing FAFSA timelines or listing out-of-state schools can reduce [chances of funding](https://acpe.alaska.gov/Funding-Solutions/Alaska-Education-Grant).


## Arizona

### Overview

Arizona’s state aid system is centered on a major [last-dollar Promise scholarship](https://www.azregents.edu/programs/arizona-promise-program) for low-income students at public universities, with relatively limited broad state grants outside that space. Aid is primarily [need-based and last-dollar](https://www.azjlbc.gov/25baseline/unibor.pdf) at the flagship level, with some campus-specific implementations like the Arizona Promise Grant at the University of Arizona.

### Major State Aid Programs

**Arizona Promise Program**
Type: [Last-dollar scholarship](https://www.azregents.edu/programs/arizona-promise-program) / tuition guarantee.
Award Amount: Covers remaining in-state tuition and mandatory fees at Arizona’s public universities after federal and other gift aid; typical annual value can be [up to roughly 12,700 dollars](https://scholaroo.nationaltoday.com/scholarship/arizona-promise-program/), depending on campus and aid package.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Arizona resident.
- Recent Arizona high school graduate (timing requirements apply).
- Minimum 2.5 high school GPA.
- Qualify for in-state tuition.
- [Financial-need criteria](https://www.azregents.edu/programs/arizona-promise-program) (e.g., Pell-eligible or meeting state low-income thresholds).

Where It Can Be Used:

- Arizona’s three public universities: Arizona State University, Northern Arizona University, University of Arizona.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Last-dollar: applied after Pell Grants and other scholarships; does not cover housing, meals, books, or non-mandatory costs.
- Designed for full-time undergraduates; must meet [satisfactory academic progress](https://www.azjlbc.gov/25baseline/unibor.pdf) to renew.
- Funding levels depend on legislative appropriations.

Application Process:

- Complete FAFSA by the state/university priority date and list an Arizona public university.
- Apply for admission and meet university financial aid processes; eligible students are awarded automatically if criteria are met.

Deadline:

- Priority FAFSA/aid deadlines are typically in [early spring](https://financialaid.arizona.edu/types-of-aid/undergraduate-grants/arizona-promise-grant); check the Arizona Board of Regents and individual university timelines.

***

**Arizona Promise Grant (University of Arizona implementation)**
Type: Last-dollar state grant implemented at [UA campus level](https://financialaid.arizona.edu/types-of-aid/undergraduate-grants/arizona-promise-grant).
Award Amount: Fills the gap between tuition/mandatory fees and all other gift aid for eligible first-year Arizona resident students at UA’s main campus.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Arizona resident.
- Pell-eligible based on FAFSA.
- First-year undergraduate at [University of Arizona main campus](https://financialaid.arizona.edu/types-of-aid/undergraduate-grants/arizona-promise-grant).
- Minimum 2.5 unweighted core GPA from an Arizona high school.
- Full-time enrollment (12+ units).

Where It Can Be Used:

- University of Arizona main campus only.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Does not cover housing, meals, books, parking, or other non-fee costs.
- [Renewable for up to 4 academic years](https://financialaid.arizona.edu/types-of-aid/undergraduate-grants/arizona-promise-grant) (8 consecutive fall/spring semesters) with continued Pell eligibility and academic progress.

Application Process:

- FAFSA completion and UA admission; no separate application for the grant.

Deadline:

- FAFSA filing date set each year by UA (often early spring for full consideration).

### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

- Additional campus-specific need- and merit-based scholarships exist at each public university, but these are institutional rather than statewide and follow separate criteria.

### Key Insights for This State

- Arizona’s main statewide aid is [Promise-style](https://www.azregents.edu/programs/arizona-promise-program) and focused on public universities, leaving community college and private-sector aid more reliant on institutional or local programs.
- Low-income, Pell-eligible students who meet GPA thresholds can effectively have [tuition and mandatory fees covered](https://financialaid.arizona.edu/types-of-aid/undergraduate-grants/arizona-promise-grant) at the three public universities, but they must budget separately for living costs.
- Aid is strongly FAFSA-dependent and generally first come within [funding limits](https://www.azjlbc.gov/25baseline/unibor.pdf), so early FAFSA filing is critical.
- Overall generosity is moderate: generous for qualifying low-income university students but relatively limited for others.


## Arkansas

### Overview

Arkansas relies heavily on [lottery-funded scholarships](https://www.myarkansaslottery.com/press-release/asl-raises-1113m-scholarships-fiscal-year-2025-reaches-third-highest-contribution-2009), with the Arkansas Academic Challenge Scholarship as the flagship and additional programs for workforce training and concurrent enrollment. Aid is largely merit/participation-based rather than strictly need-based, though award structures now reflect [student aid index tiers](https://sams.adhe.edu/Scholarship/Details/ACST).

### Major State Aid Programs

**Arkansas Academic Challenge Scholarship**
Type: Primarily merit-based, with some hybrid elements via [Student Aid Index tiers](https://sams.adhe.edu/Scholarship/Details/ACST).
Award Amount (typical schedule at 4-year institutions): [1,000 dollars freshman year](https://sams.adhe.edu/Scholarship/Page/ACST/traditional-recipient-info-sheet), 4,000 dollars sophomore year, 4,000 dollars junior year, 5,000 dollars senior year. At 2-year institutions: 1,000 dollars for the first year and 3,000 dollars for the second year.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Arkansas resident.
- Must attend an [approved Arkansas college](https://studentscholarships.org/scholarship/11056/arkansas-academic-challenge-scholarships) or university.
- Traditional students generally need at least a [19 ACT composite](https://sams.adhe.edu/Scholarship/Page/ACST/traditional-recipient-info-sheet) (or equivalent) or meet academic performance standards after one year of college.
- Full-time enrollment (typically 12+ credit hours first term, 15+ per term thereafter).

Where It Can Be Used:

- Arkansas public universities and community colleges.
- Eligible [private institutions](https://www.myarkansaslottery.com/scholarships) within Arkansas.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Funded in large part by the [Arkansas Scholarship Lottery](https://senate.arkansas.gov/senate-news/posts/2025/october/arkansas-scholarship-lottery-revenue/); total funding exceeds 1.5 billion dollars since 2009.
- GPA (about 2.5) and credit-completion requirements apply for renewal.
- Lifetime hour limits apply; award amounts may be [tiered based on Student Aid Index](https://sams.adhe.edu/Scholarship/Details/ACST).

Application Process:

- Apply through the state’s online [YOUniversal system](https://portal.arkansas.gov/service/ar-academic-challenge-scholarship/) operated by the Arkansas Department of Higher Education (ADHE).
- FAFSA strongly recommended/required to coordinate aid and determine SAI.

Deadline:

- Annual deadline [July 1](https://www.myarkansaslottery.com/scholarships) for the upcoming academic year.

***

**Arkansas Workforce Challenge Scholarship**
Type: Need-leaning workforce scholarship for [high-demand certificate programs](https://www.myarkansaslottery.com/scholarships).
Award Amount: Covers part of tuition and fees for approved workforce training and certificate programs.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Arkansas resident.
- Enrolling in eligible certificate or short-term training in high-demand fields (e.g., healthcare, IT, industrial maintenance).
- Cannot be receiving the [Arkansas Academic Challenge Scholarship](https://www.myarkansaslottery.com/scholarships) simultaneously.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Approved Arkansas institutions offering qualifying workforce/certificate programs.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Designed for workforce programs rather than degree-seeking four-year paths.
- Application must precede program start by [at least 30 days](https://www.myarkansaslottery.com/scholarships).

Application Process:

- Separate [Workforce Challenge application](https://www.myarkansaslottery.com/scholarships) through the Arkansas Scholarship Lottery/ADHE systems.

***

**ACCESS to Acceleration Program (replacing Concurrent Challenge)**
Type: Scholarship for concurrent enrollment / acceleration, primarily for high school students taking college coursework.
Award Amount: State funds to support [concurrent college courses](https://www.myarkansaslottery.com/press-release/asl-raises-1113m-scholarships-fiscal-year-2025-reaches-third-highest-contribution-2009).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Replaces the prior Concurrent Challenge Scholarship; implementation phased in from [2025 onward](https://senate.arkansas.gov/senate-news/posts/2025/october/arkansas-scholarship-lottery-revenue/).

### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

- Additional lottery-funded or state-supported awards target particular populations or fields and can [supplement Academic Challenge](https://www.myarkansaslottery.com/scholarships) awards.

### Key Insights for This State

- Arkansas offers strong scholarship coverage for in-state students through the Academic Challenge, especially in [later years](https://sams.adhe.edu/Scholarship/Page/ACST/traditional-recipient-info-sheet) of a four-year degree.
- Aid is merit-centric; students with ACT scores below the threshold have fewer statewide options.
- Workforce Challenge is critical for short-term training but is [mutually exclusive](https://www.myarkansaslottery.com/scholarships) with Academic Challenge.
- Deadlines (especially the [July 1 date](https://www.myarkansaslottery.com/scholarships)) are hard cutoffs; missing them can mean losing a year of eligibility.


## California

### Overview

California is one of the most generous states for college aid, combining large [need-based Cal Grants](https://www.gradetograd.com/blog/cal-grant-2025-guide-qualify-apply), a middle-income scholarship, and [community-college fee waivers](https://www.ccsf.edu/california-college-promise-grant-ccpg). Aid is heavily need-based but also supports middle-income families through the [Middle Class Scholarship](https://www.calstate.edu/apply/paying-for-college/financial-aid/types/Pages/middle-class-scholarship.aspx), with strict priority deadlines and complex coordination across programs.

### Major State Aid Programs

**Cal Grant A, B, and C (Ortiz-Pacheco-Poochigian-Vasconcellos Cal Grant Program)**
Type: Primarily need-based grants with [GPA thresholds](https://www.chabotcollege.edu/finaid/cal-grants.php); some variants function as tuition guarantees.

Award Amount (typical recent ranges):

- **Cal Grant A:** Up to about 9,358–9,708 dollars per year for UC and private nonprofits; around 5,742 dollars for CSU, covering systemwide tuition and fees.
- **Cal Grant B:** First year living allowance (around 1,648 dollars), then up to full tuition plus allowance in later years at UC/CSU.
- **Cal Grant C:** Supports tuition and training costs in [vocational and technical programs](https://collegeaidguide.com/cal-grant/); award amounts vary but can include several thousand dollars plus supplies.

Eligibility Requirements:

- California resident or [eligible AB540 student](https://www.gradetograd.com/blog/cal-grant-2025-guide-qualify-apply).
- High school GPA minimums (e.g., 3.0 for Cal Grant A, 2.0 for Cal Grant B).
- Income and asset ceilings based on household size.
- Demonstrated financial need via FAFSA or California Dream Act Application (CADAA).

Where It Can Be Used:

- UC and CSU campuses.
- California Community Colleges (Cal Grant B/C; Cal Grant A is often held in reserve until transfer).
- Eligible [private nonprofit](https://www.chabotcollege.edu/finaid/cal-grants.php) and some for-profit institutions in California.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Funding duration usually up to four academic years.
- For community-college students, some awards can be held in [Community College Reserve](https://www.cerritos.edu/financial-aid/programs/cal-grant.inc) until transfer.
- Awards and ceilings change with [legislation](https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/ca_202520260ab402) and per-capita income adjustments.

Application Process:

- FAFSA or CADAA plus GPA verification must be submitted.
- GPA verification deadlines are generally [March 2 and September 2](https://www.cerritos.edu/financial-aid/programs/cal-grant.inc) (for certain community college students).

Deadline:

- Priority deadline **March 2** for most students.

***

**Middle Class Scholarship (MCS 2.0)**
Type: Need-informed middle-income scholarship; hybrid formula grant.
Award Amount: Varies by student; covers a percentage of remaining costs after [other gift aid](http://www.finaid.ucsb.edu/scholarships/middle-class-scholarship) and a self-help contribution.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Undergraduate or teaching-credential student at [UC or CSU](https://fas.ucsd.edu/types/scholarships/middle-class-scholarship.html).
- Family income and asset ceilings (e.g., in the [roughly 217,000–234,000 dollars range](https://www.ofas.uci.edu/paying-for-uci/grants/middle-class-scholarship.php)).

Where It Can Be Used:

- University of California.
- California State University.
- Some California Community College bachelor’s programs.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Award amounts fluctuate with state funding; recent [budget pressures](https://ticas.org/california/ca-emergency-financial-aid-statement/) can lead to adjustments.

***

**California College Promise Grant (CCPG)**
Type: Need-based [fee waiver](https://citruscollegerequests.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/6400264358548-California-College-Promise-Grant-CCPG) for community college enrollment fees.
Award Amount: Waives the per-unit community college enrollment fee (currently [46 dollars per unit](https://www.mjc.edu/financialaid/ccpg.html)); does not cover non-enrollment fees.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Enrollment at a [California Community College](https://www.peralta.edu/financial-aid/california-promise).
- Financial need or specific means-tested benefit/income criteria.

Application Process:

- FAFSA or CADAA automatically counts as a [CCPG application](https://www.cccco.edu/-/media/CCCCO-Website/docs/application/2025-26-english-ccpg-application-a11y.pdf) at most community colleges.

### Key Insights for This State

- California is highly favorable for low- and middle-income residents, with Cal Grants covering [full systemwide tuition](https://www.gradetograd.com/blog/cal-grant-2025-guide-qualify-apply) at UC/CSU.
- The state is deadline-driven: missing the [March 2 GPA/FAFSA deadline](https://www.cerritos.edu/financial-aid/programs/cal-grant.inc) can eliminate access to major grants for an entire year.
- Stacking Cal Grant, CCPG, and local promise initiatives can make community college [effectively tuition-free](https://www.ccsf.edu/california-college-promise-grant-ccpg) for many students.


## Colorado

### Overview

Colorado’s statewide aid is moderate and largely need-based, delivered through the [Colorado Student Grant](https://www.ucdenver.edu/student-finances/financial-aid/types/grants) and institutional grant packages, with [CASFA](https://coloradomtn.edu/financial-aid/casfa/) extending access to undocumented students. There is no single statewide Promise program, but [state grants](https://www.colorado.edu/financialaid/types-aid/undergraduate-grants) can meaningfully reduce costs for low-income residents at public institutions.

### Major State Aid Programs

**Colorado Student Grant (CSG)**
Type: [Need-based state grant](https://finaid.uccs.edu/types-of-aid/grants).
Award Amount: Up to about 5,000 dollars per year, depending on Student Aid Index, enrollment, and institutional policy.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Colorado resident for tuition purposes.
- First-time degree-seeking undergraduate (no prior bachelor’s degree).
- Enrolled at least [half-time](https://www.fortlewis.edu/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=VaAGO7X9LOY%3D&portalid=37) (e.g., 6+ credits at many institutions).
- Demonstrated financial need within state-set SAI ranges (for example, SAI roughly between -1,500 and about 12,000 for recent years).
- Meeting institutional satisfactory academic progress standards.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [Participating Colorado public institutions](https://www.ucdenver.edu/student-finances/financial-aid/types/grants) and some private institutions that administer state aid.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Funding is limited; institutions must prioritize the neediest students within SAI ranges.
- Award levels and SAI cutoffs may vary by institution and year depending on state allocation.

Application Process:

- FAFSA for citizens and eligible non-citizens.
- [CASFA (Colorado Application for State Financial Aid)](https://denverscholarship.org/resources/casfa/) for students not eligible for federal aid but who meet [Colorado ASSET](https://otero.edu/pay-for-college/financial-aid/colorado-application-for-state-financial-aid/) residency criteria.

Deadline:

- No single statewide date, but students should submit FAFSA or CASFA [as close to opening](https://ed.cde.state.co.us/postsecondary/financialaidfafsa) (around October) as possible; many campuses award CSG on a first-come, first-served basis.

### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

- Institutional “Colorado Promise” or tuition guarantee programs at some campuses (e.g., [CSU](https://thehub.colostate.edu/financial-aid/eligibility/), CU Boulder) use a mix of state and institutional funds to cover tuition for Pell-eligible or low-income residents, but these are campus-specific rather than uniform statewide programs.

### Key Insights for This State

- Colorado’s primary need-based tool is the Colorado Student Grant; eligibility hinges heavily on SAI ranges and [half-time enrollment](https://finaid.uccs.edu/types-of-aid/grants).
- [CASFA is crucial](https://denverscholarship.org/resources/casfa/) for undocumented and other non-FAFSA-eligible residents, effectively opening state aid to ASSET students on similar timelines.
- Because funding is limited and institutionally allocated, applying early and enrolling at least half-time is essential to secure CSG.
- Overall generosity is moderate and more focused on need than merit; high-achieving students do not receive a separate statewide merit award.


## Connecticut

### Overview

Connecticut offers a strong [hybrid of merit-plus-need scholarships](https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/sde/performance/fafsa/frequently-asked-questions--roberta-b-willis-need-merit-scholarship.pdf) and last-dollar free community college, making it relatively generous for in-state students who meet GPA and eligibility criteria. The Roberta B. Willis programs target four-year and two-year students, while [PACT (now the Mary Ann Handley Award)](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/article/ct-community-college-free-everyone-what-know-about-pact-program) functions as a last-dollar community-college program.

### Major State Aid Programs

**Roberta B. Willis Need-Merit Scholarship Program**
Type: [Hybrid merit/need-based scholarship](https://connecticut.teach.org/aid/roberta-b-willis-scholarship-need-and-merit-based).
Award Amount: Up to **5,250 dollars** per year for four-year programs and up to **4,650 dollars** per year for two-year programs, renewable for up to six consecutive years.

Eligibility Requirements:

* Connecticut resident and high school senior/graduate.
* Academic requirement: **top 20% of class** and/or [SAT around 1200+ or ACT 25+](https://connecticut.teach.org/aid/roberta-b-willis-scholarship-need-and-merit-based).
* Must attend a Connecticut public or non-profit private college (Yale and Wesleyan excluded).
* Must meet a [Student Aid Index (SAI) cutoff](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/connecticut/) set annually.

Where It Can Be Used:

* Connecticut public universities and community colleges.
* Eligible private non-profit institutions in-state.

Application Process:

* [FAFSA required](https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/sde/performance/fafsa/frequently-asked-questions--roberta-b-willis-need-merit-scholarship.pdf).
* Separate state application, often submitted via a high school counselor, must be filed by the program deadline.

Deadline:

* Typically **mid-February** (e.g., February 15), which is much earlier than federal deadlines.

***

**Roberta B. Willis Need-Based Grant Program**
Type: [Need-based state grant](https://bigfuture.collegeboard.org/scholarships/roberta-b-willis-need-based-grant-program).
Award Amount: Up to about **4,500 dollars** per year for eligible students.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

* Awards depend on [annual appropriations](https://bigfuture.collegeboard.org/scholarships/roberta-b-willis-need-based-grant-program) and institutional allocations; not all eligible students may receive the maximum.

***

**Pledge to Advance Connecticut (PACT) / Mary Ann Handley Award – Debt-Free Community College**
Type: [Last-dollar community-college tuition](https://www.ct.edu/admission/free) and mandatory-fee program.
Award Amount: Covers remaining tuition and mandatory fees at Connecticut community colleges after all other grant aid; often results in [zero tuition-related debt](https://www.collegefairguide.com/single-post/connecticut-now-has-free-community-college).

Eligibility Requirements:

* Connecticut resident and high school graduate (including GED/home-schooled).
* Must complete FAFSA and [accept all grant aid](https://www.housedems.ct.gov/node/18639).
* Enrolled in a degree or credit-bearing certificate program.

Where It Can Be Used:

* All 12 Connecticut community colleges (consolidated as **CT State Community College**).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

* [Last-dollar](https://www.ct.edu/newsroom/following-pact-priority-deadline-potential-students-can-still-apply-for-fre): does not cover books, transportation, or living expenses.
* Funding is finite and awards are [first-come, first-served](https://www.housedems.ct.gov/node/18639) after a priority deadline.

### Key Insights for This State

* Connecticut is relatively strong for in-state students, especially [high achievers with financial need](https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/sde/performance/fafsa/frequently-asked-questions--roberta-b-willis-need-merit-scholarship.pdf) and community-college students.
* The Willis Need-Merit Scholarship has an early **mid-February deadline** and requires [counselor involvement](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/connecticut/); missing this is a common pitfall.
* PACT is strictly [first-come, first-served](https://www.ct.edu/newsroom/following-pact-priority-deadline-potential-students-can-still-apply-for-fre), so early FAFSA completion is critical for securing funds.


## Delaware

### Overview

Delaware’s aid system is particularly strong for associate-degree and early bachelor’s pathways via the [SEED (Student Excellence Equals Degree)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_Student_Excellence_Equals_Degree_Program_Scholarship) last-dollar scholarship. Aid is primarily merit-based with last-dollar design, covering [tuition at Delaware Tech](https://studentscholarships.org/scholarship/34674/student-excellence-equals-degree-seed-scholarship) and the University of Delaware’s associate programs for qualifying residents.

### Major State Aid Programs

**Delaware SEED / SEED+ Scholarship**
Type: Merit-based, last-dollar [tuition scholarship](https://www.ahead-penn.org/creating-knowledge/college-promise/programs/delaware-seed-student-excellence-equals-degree).
Award Amount: Covers tuition (not fees, books, or living costs) for up to 10 semesters at eligible institutions; effectively [free tuition for an associate degree](https://studentscholarships.org/scholarship/34674/student-excellence-equals-degree-seed-scholarship) plus additional semesters toward a bachelor’s degree.

Eligibility Requirements:

* Delaware resident.
* High school graduate from a Delaware high school.
* Minimum high school [GPA of about 2.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_Student_Excellence_Equals_Degree_Program_Scholarship).
* Full-time enrollment initially, though [SEED+](https://studentscholarships.org/scholarship/34674/student-excellence-equals-degree-seed-scholarship) allows part-time attendance after the first semester.
* Must not have been convicted of a felony.
* Must [file FAFSA](https://www.udel.edu/home/pathways/seed-scholarship/) and apply for other financial aid.

Where It Can Be Used:

* **Delaware Technical Community College (Delaware Tech)** for associate degrees, diplomas, credit certificates, and workforce training.
* [University of Delaware’s Associate in Arts Program](https://www.udel.edu/home/pathways/seed-scholarship/) / Flex Pathway.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

* Last-dollar: covers tuition only; does not cover books, fees, or other expenses.
* Expanded SEED+ [removes age caps](https://studentscholarships.org/scholarship/34674/student-excellence-equals-degree-seed-scholarship) and allows up to 10 semesters, including short-term workforce certificates.
* Recipients must maintain at least a [2.5 college GPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_Student_Excellence_Equals_Degree_Program_Scholarship) and meet credit-completion and continuous-enrollment rules.

Application Process:

* Apply for admission to Delaware Tech or UD Associate/Flex Pathway.
* File FAFSA and meet all institutional and SEED deadlines.

Deadline:

* Specific [institutional deadlines](https://scholarships.delawarestudentsuccess.org/scholarships-listed-by-deadline-april/entry/2716/) (e.g., mid-January) apply; students must meet admission, FAFSA, and any SEED form deadlines.

### Key Insights for This State

* Delaware is particularly strong for students starting at Delaware Tech or UD’s associate programs, providing [up to 10 semesters of tuition](https://studentscholarships.org/scholarship/34674/student-excellence-equals-degree-seed-scholarship) for many residents.
* SEED is merit-based (GPA and conduct) rather than purely need-based, but it still [requires FAFSA](https://www.udel.edu/home/pathways/seed-scholarship/) and coordination with other aid.
* Because SEED is last-dollar and tuition-only, students still need to plan for fees, books, and living costs.
* Continuous enrollment and GPA requirements can trip up students who reduce course loads or struggle academically.


## Florida

### Overview

Florida’s aid system is robust and highly merit-driven, led by the [Bright Futures program](https://www-bhs.stjohns.k12.fl.us/guidance/florida-bright-futures/), with additional need-based grants (FSAG) and a grant for private colleges (EASE). Generosity is strong for [academically high-achieving residents](https://scholarships360.org/scholarships/bright-futures-scholarship-requirements/) and moderate for need-based students, with strict state deadlines and first-come funding caps.

### Major State Aid Programs

**Florida Bright Futures Scholarship Program (FAS, FMS, GSV)**
Type: [Merit-based scholarship](https://studentfinance.fsu.edu/current-students/financial-aid/florida-bright-futures) with multiple award tiers.
Award Amount:

- **Florida Academic Scholars (FAS):** Typically [covers 100% of tuition](https://www.floridastudentfinancialaidsg.org/pdf/fas-fms.pdf) and certain fees at in-state public institutions.
- **Florida Medallion Scholars (FMS):** Generally covers about [75% of tuition](https://scholarships360.org/scholarships/bright-futures-scholarship-requirements/) at public institutions.
- **Gold Seal Vocational/Gold Seal CAPE (GSV):** Supports [career and technical programs](https://www-bhs.stjohns.k12.fl.us/guidance/florida-bright-futures/) with per-credit awards.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Florida resident and U.S. citizen or eligible non-citizen.
- Meet specific [high school GPA](https://www.floridastudentfinancialaidsg.org/pdf/fas-fms.pdf), required coursework, standardized test scores, and community service/work hours.
- Enroll in an eligible Florida postsecondary institution.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Florida public universities, state colleges, and some [eligible private institutions](https://studentfinance.fsu.edu/current-students/financial-aid/florida-bright-futures/).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Students must maintain [minimum college GPA](https://www.floridastudentfinancialaidsg.org/pdf/fas-fms.pdf) and credit-hour completion to renew.

Application Process:

- Complete the [Florida Financial Aid Application (FFAA)](https://floridabrightfutures.gov) during senior year of high school.
- FFAA must be submitted no later than **August 31** after high school graduation; missing this window [permanently forfeits eligibility](https://www-bhs.stjohns.k12.fl.us/guidance/florida-bright-futures/).

***

**Florida Student Assistance Grant (FSAG)**
Type: [Need-based state grant](https://www.scf.edu/paying-for-college/financial-aid/grants/florida-student-assistance-grant-fsag/).
Award Amount: Varies by year and institution; [funding is limited](https://www.spcollege.edu/financial-aid/types-of-financial-aid/grants/florida-student-assistance-grant) and determined by state appropriations.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Florida resident for tuition purposes for at least one year.
- Degree-seeking undergraduate [enrolled at least half-time](https://www.floridastudentfinancialaidsg.org/PDF/factsheets/FSAG.pdf) (6+ credit hours).
- Pell-eligible or SAI below state cutoff (e.g., [SAI around 9,984 or less](https://ut.smartcatalogiq.com/en/current/catalog/undergraduate-catalog/financial-aid/types-of-assistance-available/scholarships-and-grants/florida-residents/florida-student-assistance-grant-fsag)).

Where It Can Be Used:

- [Participating Florida public colleges](https://www.spcollege.edu/financial-aid/types-of-financial-aid/grants/florida-student-assistance-grant) and universities.

Application Process:

- **FAFSA required**; institution determines eligibility based on FAFSA data and enrollment.

***

**Effective Access to Student Education (EASE) Grant**
Type: Non-need state grant for [private, nonprofit institutions](https://www.pba.edu/financial-aid/types-of-aid/scholarships/ease-grant/).
Award Amount: Set by the legislature; for example, about [2,000 dollars per student](https://ut.smartcatalogiq.com/en/current/catalog/undergraduate-catalog/financial-aid/types-of-assistance-available/scholarships-and-grants/florida-residents/florida-effective-access-to-student-education-grant).

Eligibility Requirements:

- Florida resident for at least one year.
- Full-time undergraduate student (12+ credit hours) at an [eligible private Florida college](https://www.south.edu/effective-access-to-student-education-program-ease/).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- [No financial-need requirement](https://floridataxwatch.org/Top-Issues/Education/the-importance-of-effective-access-to-student-education-ease-grants); functions as a tuition-equalization grant to narrow public-private price gaps.

Application Process:

- One-time [EASE application](https://www.south.edu/effective-access-to-student-education-program-ease/) via institution’s financial aid office.

### Key Insights for This State

- **Bright Futures** makes Florida very attractive for [high-achieving in-state students](https://scholarships360.org/scholarships/bright-futures-scholarship-requirements/), often covering most tuition.
- Need-based support through **FSAG** is meaningful but limited; [early FAFSA filing](https://www.scf.edu/paying-for-college/financial-aid/grants/florida-student-assistance-grant-fsag/) is essential to compete for funds.
- **EASE** significantly reduces costs for residents choosing [private nonprofits](https://erau.edu/leadership/consumer-information/florida-ease-accountability-report), narrowing the public–private price divide.
- The Bright Futures FFAA deadline (**August 31 after graduation**) is a [hard, one-time window](https://floridabrightfutures.gov); missing it permanently forfeits eligibility.


## Georgia

### Overview

Georgia’s state aid system is highly merit-focused and considered one of the most generous for academically strong residents through its [HOPE and Zell Miller programs](https://gsfc.georgia.gov/programs-and-regulations/hope-programs). Aid consists mainly of tuition scholarships and grants funded by the [Georgia Lottery](https://gsfc.georgia.gov/hope), with additional grants for private-college students and technical programs.

### Major State Aid Programs

**HOPE Scholarship**
Type: [Merit-based tuition scholarship](https://osfa.uga.edu/types-of-aid/undergraduate/scholarships/hope-and-zell-miller-scholarships/).
Award Amount: Covers a substantial portion of tuition at eligible public institutions and a set dollar amount at [eligible private institutions](https://truett.edu/admissions/financial-aid/state-grants-loans/).

Eligibility Requirements:

* Georgia resident.
* High school graduate with at least a **3.0 HOPE GPA** (as calculated by GSFC).
* Must maintain at least a [3.0 cumulative postsecondary GPA](https://osfa.uga.edu/types-of-aid/undergraduate/scholarships/hope-and-zell-miller-scholarships/) at checkpoints (30, 60, 90 hours and each spring) to renew.

Where It Can Be Used:

* Eligible Georgia public universities and colleges.
* Public technical colleges (for associate-level degrees).
* Eligible private institutions in Georgia.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

* Funded by [Georgia Lottery proceeds](https://gsfc.georgia.gov/hope); over 16 billion dollars awarded since 1993.
* Length-of-eligibility caps: generally **127 semester hours** and certain time limits from first receipt.

Application Process:

* [FAFSA or Georgia state aid application](https://gsfc.georgia.gov/programs-and-regulations/hope-programs) (GAFutures/GSFAPPS) can be used to apply.

***

**Zell Miller Scholarship**
Type: [High-merit scholarship](https://osfa.uga.edu/types-of-aid/undergraduate/scholarships/hope-and-zell-miller-scholarships/) (enhanced HOPE).
Award Amount: Typically **covers full standard tuition** at eligible public institutions; provides a [higher private-institution award](https://truett.edu/admissions/financial-aid/state-grants-loans/) than HOPE.

Eligibility Requirements:

* High school HOPE GPA of at least **3.7**.
* Minimum [SAT score of 1,200](https://www.gafutures.org/hope-state-aid-programs/hope-zell-miller-scholarships/zell-miller-scholarship/) or ACT composite around 25 in a single test administration.
* Must maintain at least a **3.3 cumulative college GPA** to retain Zell Miller; students may drop to HOPE if they fall below but remain above 3.0.

***

**HOPE Grant and Zell Miller Grant (Technical Colleges)**
Type: Merit-based grants for certificate and diploma programs.
Award Amount:

* **HOPE Grant:** Provides substantial tuition assistance for certificate/diploma programs at [technical colleges](https://gsfc.georgia.gov/hope).
* **Zell Miller Grant:** Provides [full standard tuition assistance](https://gsfc.georgia.gov/programs-and-regulations/hope-programs) for qualifying students in these programs.

Eligibility Requirements:

* Enrolled in a certificate or diploma program at an eligible technical college.
* Zell Miller Grant requires maintaining about a **3.50 GPA**.

***

**Georgia Tuition Equalization Grant (GTEG)**
Type: Non-need grant for [private-college students](https://truett.edu/admissions/financial-aid/state-grants-loans/).
Award Amount: About **1,100 dollars per year** for full-time Georgia residents at eligible private institutions.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

* Non-need-based; [stacks with HOPE/Zell Miller](https://truett.edu/admissions/financial-aid/state-grants-loans/) at private institutions.

### Key Insights for This State

* Georgia is very strong for academically strong residents: HOPE and especially Zell Miller can [cover most or all tuition](https://gsfc.georgia.gov/programs-and-regulations/hope-programs) at public institutions.
* The system is merit-heavy, offering comparatively less pure need-based state aid than many other states.
* [Hour and time limits](https://osfa.uga.edu/types-of-aid/undergraduate/scholarships/hope-and-zell-miller-scholarships/) plus GPA checkpoints are common pitfalls; losing HOPE or Zell mid-college can [significantly increase costs](https://direct.mit.edu/edfp/article/18/4/597/112925/Gaining-Losing-and-Regaining-Merit-based).
* **GTEG plus HOPE/Zell** can make private institutions more affordable, though award amounts are smaller than public-institution tuition coverage.

## Hawaii

### Overview

Hawaii offers a moderately generous set of state-funded programs with a strong emphasis on need-based aid and “last-dollar” support at community colleges. Aid is somewhat concentrated within the University of Hawaiʻi system, with flagship programs like [Hawaiʻi Promise](https://uhcc.hawaii.edu/admissions/hawaii-promise.php/enroll.php) for community colleges and the [B Plus Scholarship](https://catalog.windward.hawaii.edu/state-financial-aid-programs) for public high school graduates.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Hawaiʻi Promise Scholarship (UH Community Colleges)

Type:
Need-based, [last-dollar community college tuition](https://mauinow.com/2024/06/18/the-2024-25-hawaii-promise-scholarship-fund-for-uh-community-colleges-is-largest-yet/) and direct-cost scholarship.

Award Amount:
Covers remaining direct education costs (tuition, fees, books, and a transportation/books allowance) after other grants and scholarships; typical annual awards average around [low–$2,000s per student](https://manoa.hawaii.edu/news/article.php?aId=13304), with total program funding of about $5 million in 2024–25.

Eligibility Requirements:

* Hawaiʻi resident who qualifies for [in-state tuition](https://uhcc.hawaii.edu/admissions/hawaii-promise.php/enroll.php).
* Enrolled in a financial-aid-eligible program at a [UH Community College](https://uhcc.hawaii.edu/admissions/hawaii-promise.php/enroll.php).
* At least half-time (typically 6+ credits) and maintaining satisfactory academic progress (SAP).
* Must [file FAFSA](https://accessscholarships.com/scholarship/hawaii-promise-program/) and accept other federal/state grants and scholarships first.

Where It Can Be Used:

* Any of the [seven University of Hawaiʻi Community Colleges](https://manoa.hawaii.edu/news/article.php?aId=13304) (e.g., Honolulu, Kapiʻolani, Leeward, Maui, Windward, Hawaiʻi CC, Kauaʻi CC).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

* Last-dollar: pays only the gap after other gift aid; does not generally cover room/board.
* Funding is [limited by annual state appropriations](https://mauinow.com/2024/06/18/the-2024-25-hawaii-promise-scholarship-fund-for-uh-community-colleges-is-largest-yet/); awards may be reduced if demand exceeds funding.
* Must maintain SAP and required credit load each term for renewal.

Application Process:

* Submit FAFSA by the UH community colleges’ recommended date (commonly by July 1 for fall).
* [No separate scholarship application](https://accessscholarships.com/scholarship/hawaii-promise-program/); students are automatically considered if enrolled and FAFSA-complete.

Deadline:

* Priority: complete FAFSA by July 1 for fall enrollment; later applicants considered only while funds remain.

#### State of Hawaiʻi B Plus (B+) Scholarship

Type:
Hybrid merit–need scholarship for recent [public high school graduates](https://catalog.windward.hawaii.edu/state-financial-aid-programs) attending UH; GPA- and curriculum-based with financial-need requirement.

Award Amount:
Up to about [$2,000 per year at UH Mānoa](https://manoa.hawaii.edu/fas/types-of-aid/scholarships/financial-aid-scholarships/) (amounts may vary by campus and funding).

Eligibility Requirements:

* Hawaiʻi resident and graduate of a Hawaiʻi public high school.
* Minimum cumulative [high school GPA of 3.0](https://www.collegescholarships.org/states/hawaii.htm) (“B+”) and completion of a “rigorous” high school curriculum.
* Demonstrated financial need based on FAFSA.
* Enroll at an eligible University of Hawaiʻi campus, usually full-time (12+ credits) for maximum award.

Where It Can Be Used:

* University of Hawaiʻi [four-year campuses](https://manoa.hawaii.edu/fas/types-of-aid/scholarships/financial-aid-scholarships/) (e.g., UH Mānoa) and often other UH campuses, subject to campus rules.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

* Funding is limited; renewal contingent on SAP and continued need, up to about four years.
* Need-based: high-income students with 3.0+ GPAs [may not qualify](https://www.collegescholarships.org/states/hawaii.htm) if they lack financial need.

Application Process:

* File FAFSA by UH’s priority date; some campuses require a short institutional scholarship application.
* [High school transcript and curriculum verification](https://accessscholarships.com/scholarship/hawaii-b-scholarship/) used to confirm GPA and course rigor.

Deadline:

* FAFSA and any campus scholarship application typically due by February–March.

#### UH Opportunity Grant (UH System)

Type:
Need-based institutional/state-supported grant.

Award Amount:
Varies by campus and financial need; [amounts are limited](https://catalog.windward.hawaii.edu/state-financial-aid-programs) and depend on campus allocations.

Eligibility Requirements:

* Hawaiʻi resident (priority) enrolled at least half-time in a UH campus.
* Demonstrated financial need via FAFSA.

Where It Can Be Used:

* UH Mānoa, UH Hilo, UH West Oʻahu, and [UH community colleges](https://catalog.windward.hawaii.edu/state-financial-aid-programs).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

* Funds are limited and may run out early.
* Typically tuition-focused; may not cover full cost of attendance.

Application Process:

* Submit FAFSA by campus priority deadline; no separate application for most students.

Deadline:

* Campus financial aid priority deadlines (often around February–March for fall).

### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs (Hawaii)

* **Native Hawaiian Tuition Waiver & Second Century Scholars Grant** – [Need-based awards](https://catalog.windward.hawaii.edu/state-financial-aid-programs) tied to Native Hawaiian ancestry, financial need, and at least half-time enrollment at UH; funding is limited, and FAFSA is required.

### Key Insights for This State

* Hawaiʻi is relatively strong for community college affordability thanks to [Hawaiʻi Promise’s last-dollar design](https://uhcc.hawaii.edu/admissions/hawaii-promise.php/enroll.php), but four-year aid is more limited and selective.
* Filing [FAFSA early is critical](https://mauinow.com/2024/06/18/the-2024-25-hawaii-promise-scholarship-fund-for-uh-community-colleges-is-largest-yet/) because both Hawaiʻi Promise and UH grants are limited and can be reduced once funds are exhausted.
* Public high school students with strong GPAs should target the [B Plus Scholarship](https://www.collegescholarships.org/states/hawaii.htm) plus campus merit to reduce UH tuition substantially.
* Native Hawaiian students should explore [state-funded waivers and grants](https://catalog.windward.hawaii.edu/state-financial-aid-programs) in addition to federal and private Native Hawaiian aid.

## Idaho

### Overview

Idaho’s state aid is moderate, anchored by the [Idaho Opportunity Scholarship](https://nextsteps.idaho.gov/scholarship-idaho/idaho-opportunity-scholarship) and newer workforce-focused programs like Idaho LAUNCH. The state also offers selective merit/leadership awards like the [Governor’s Cup Scholarship](https://nextsteps.idaho.gov/scholarship-idaho/idaho-governors-cup-or-academic), making the system a mix of need-based and [targeted merit aid](https://boardofed.idaho.gov/scholarships/).

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Idaho Opportunity Scholarship

Type:
Need-based, with a modest merit component in ranking.

Award Amount:
Up to [$3,500 per year](https://www.byui.edu/financial-aid/idaho-opportunity-scholarship), renewable for up to 4 years (maximum about $14,000 over four years).

Eligibility Requirements:

* Idaho resident and graduate (or upcoming graduate) of an Idaho high school, Idaho homeschool, or [equivalent GED/HSE from Idaho](https://www.collegescholarships.com/scholarships/detail/126888).
* Minimum [unweighted cumulative GPA of 2.7](https://accessscholarships.com/scholarship/idaho-opportunity-scholarship/).
* Pursuing first certificate, associate, or bachelor’s degree at an [eligible Idaho college](https://www.byui.edu/financial-aid/explore-idaho-state-aid/idaho-opportunity-scholarship), university, or technical school.
* Demonstrated financial need via FAFSA (Student Aid Index heavily weighted in selection).
* Must maintain SAP and credit-completion benchmarks for renewal.

Where It Can Be Used:

* Approved [Idaho public universities and colleges](https://nextsteps.idaho.gov/scholarship-idaho/idaho-opportunity-scholarship) (e.g., Boise State, Idaho State, University of Idaho) and approved private/technical institutions.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

* Competitive: students are ranked using a [combined 70% need / 30% GPA score](https://nextsteps.idaho.gov/scholarship-idaho/idaho-opportunity-scholarship); awards limited by annual funding.
* First-come within the priority window; many years not all eligible students are funded.

Application Process:

* Create an account and apply through the [Scholarship Idaho/Next Steps Idaho portal](https://nextsteps.idaho.gov/scholarship-idaho/idaho-opportunity-scholarship).
* Submit FAFSA and list an eligible Idaho institution.

Deadline:

* Application and FAFSA generally open October 1 and are [due by March 1](https://accessscholarships.com/scholarship/idaho-opportunity-scholarship/) for the following academic year.

#### Idaho LAUNCH Grant

Type:
Workforce-focused, last-dollar style tuition grant for in-demand programs.

Award Amount:
Total of $8,000, with up to $4,000 available in the first academic year; typically covers [80% of tuition](https://www.byui.edu/financial-aid/explore-idaho-state-aid) for approved in‑demand programs.

Eligibility Requirements:

* Idaho resident and recent Idaho high school graduate.
* Enrolled in an [in-demand degree or certificate program](https://www.byui.edu/financial-aid/explore-idaho-state-aid) designated by the state.

Where It Can Be Used:

* Eligible Idaho colleges and universities offering state-designated in-demand programs (e.g., certain health, tech, and trade fields).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

* Last-dollar: fills remaining tuition after other grants and scholarships.
* Limited to specific high‑demand programs; not a general-purpose scholarship.

Application Process:

* Apply through the [Idaho Board of Education / Scholarship Idaho portal](https://boardofed.idaho.gov/scholarships/) following program-specific instructions.

Deadline:

* Deadlines set by the Office of the State Board of Education; dates vary but are published each cycle.

#### Idaho Governor’s Cup Scholarship

Type:
Merit-based with strong emphasis on leadership and public service; [academic and CTE tracks](https://nextsteps.idaho.gov/scholarship-idaho/idaho-governors-cup-or-academic).

Award Amount:

* Academic track: [up to $5,000 per year](https://www.sallie.com/scholarships/scholly/idaho-governor-s-cup-scholarship).
* CTE track: [up to $3,000 per year](https://nextsteps.idaho.gov/scholarship-idaho/idaho-governors-cup-or-cte).
* Renewable for up to four years (academic) or three years (career‑technical), subject to continued eligibility.

Eligibility Requirements:

* Idaho resident and graduating senior from an Idaho high school or homeschool (or first‑year CTE student).
* Minimum [2.8 unweighted GPA](https://nextsteps.idaho.gov/scholarship-idaho/idaho-governors-cup-or-academic).
* Full-time enrollment at an eligible Idaho college or university.
* Demonstrated [commitment to public service](https://www.sallie.com/scholarships/scholly/idaho-governor-s-cup-scholarship), documented volunteer and leadership activities, and two letters of recommendation.

Where It Can Be Used:

* Eligible Idaho public and private colleges and universities listed by the scholarship program.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

* Highly competitive, with only about [25 awards statewide per year](https://www.sallie.com/scholarships/scholly/idaho-governor-s-cup-scholarship), subject to funding.
* Renewal requires continued full-time enrollment and academic progress.

Application Process:

* Apply through Scholarship Idaho; submit essay, volunteer documentation, and recommendation letters.

Deadline:

* Academic track applications generally due March 1; [CTE track around April 1](https://nextsteps.idaho.gov/scholarship-idaho/idaho-governors-cup-or-cte).

### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs (Idaho)

* **GEAR UP Idaho Scholarship (cohort-specific)** – Provides [additional funding](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/idaho/) for students who participated in Idaho GEAR UP; deadlines and awards are published by the Idaho State Board of Education for each cohort.

### Key Insights for This State

* Idaho’s Opportunity Scholarship is the core statewide aid program; students with GPAs above 2.7 and clear need should prioritize this [application by March 1](https://nextsteps.idaho.gov/scholarship-idaho/idaho-opportunity-scholarship).
* Workforce-focused aid ([LAUNCH](https://www.byui.edu/financial-aid/explore-idaho-state-aid) and [CTE Governor’s Cup](https://nextsteps.idaho.gov/scholarship-idaho/idaho-governors-cup-or-cte)) makes Idaho relatively strong for residents pursuing in-demand technical and career programs.
* High achievers with strong service records should treat the [Governor’s Cup](https://www.sallie.com/scholarships/scholly/idaho-governor-s-cup-scholarship) as a prestigious, high-value scholarship but be aware of its highly competitive nature.
* Filing FAFSA early and using [Scholarship Idaho](https://boardofed.idaho.gov/scholarships/) is central to accessing most state funds.

## Illinois

### Overview

Illinois has a relatively generous but heavily oversubscribed need-based system centered on the [Monetary Award Program (MAP)](https://osfa.illinois.edu/types-of-aid/grants/monetary-award-program-map-grant/), plus institution-specific [AIM HIGH awards](https://www.osfa.illinois.edu/types-of-aid/grants/aim-high-program/) that blend merit and need. Funding often runs out early, so timing of the FAFSA is critical.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Monetary Award Program (MAP) Grant

Type:
Need-based state grant for undergraduates attending Illinois colleges.

Award Amount:

- Awards typically range from about $300 up to [roughly $8,064–$8,400 per year](https://financialaid.uic.edu/types-of-aid/grants/state-grants/), depending on school, enrollment level, and financial need.
- Restricted to [tuition and mandatory fees only](https://www.luc.edu/finaid/grants/illinois-map-grants/).

Eligibility Requirements:

- Illinois resident (student and generally parents) attending or planning to attend an [approved Illinois institution](https://osfa.illinois.edu/types-of-aid/grants/monetary-award-program-map-grant/).
- Undergraduate who has not earned a bachelor’s or professional degree.
- Student Aid Index (SAI) [below program limits](https://www.gradetograd.com/blog/illinois-map-grant-2025-how-to-qualify).
- Enrolled at least half-time (6+ credits) at a participating Illinois college or university.
- Meeting institutional SAP standards.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Illinois public universities, community colleges, and participating private/nonprofit institutions.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- **First-come-first-served:** ISAC routinely [suspends awards](https://www.gradetograd.com/blog/illinois-map-grant-2025-how-to-qualify) for students filing after an annual cutoff once funds are depleted.
- Limited lifetime eligibility (up to [135 MAP eligibility units](https://osfa.illinois.edu/types-of-aid/grants/monetary-award-program-map-grant/), roughly equivalent to 4.5 years full-time).

Application Process:

- Submit FAFSA or, for eligible undocumented students, the [Alternative Application for Illinois Financial Aid](https://financialaid.uic.edu/types-of-aid/grants/state-grants/).
- No separate MAP application; colleges automatically package MAP for eligible students.

Deadline:

- FAFSA/Alternative Application should be submitted as soon as possible after opening; awards may be suspended for applications received after an [ISAC-announced date](https://financialaid.uic.edu/types-of-aid/grants/state-grants/).

#### AIM HIGH Grants

Type:
Hybrid merit/need state grant delivered through public universities, with [institutional matching funds](https://www.osfa.illinois.edu/types-of-aid/grants/aim-high-program/).

Award Amount:

- Varies by campus; examples include [$5,000 per year](https://financialaid.illinoisstate.edu/scholarships/aim-high/) (up to $20,000 over 4 years) at certain universities for top in‑state freshmen.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Illinois resident; U.S. citizen, eligible noncitizen, or qualifying [undocumented student under the RISE Act](https://financialaid.illinoisstate.edu/scholarships/aim-high/).
- First‑time undergraduate (usually new freshmen) at an Illinois public university.
- Full‑time enrollment in a baccalaureate program.
- Household income typically [not more than 8× federal poverty guidelines](https://financialaid.illinoisstate.edu/downloads/aimhigh/2024-2025%20AIM%20HIGH%20Scholarship.pdf); campuses may prioritize higher GPA applicants.
- FAFSA or RISE application required.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Participating [Illinois public universities](https://www.osfa.illinois.edu/types-of-aid/grants/aim-high-program/) (UIUC, Illinois State, UIC, etc.).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Renewable up to four years with full-time enrollment and maintaining [specific GPA requirements](https://financialaid.illinoisstate.edu/scholarships/aim-high/) (often ≥2.75).
- Campus-specific criteria and award amounts vary; students must review their university’s AIM HIGH page.

Application Process:

- Complete FAFSA or Alternative Application by the campus’s preferred filing date.
- [No separate state-level AIM HIGH application](https://www.osfa.illinois.edu/types-of-aid/grants/aim-high-program/); students are considered based on admission and aid data.

Deadline:

- Preference typically given to those who file by mid‑March for the upcoming academic year.

### Key Insights for This State

- Illinois is generous on paper through MAP, but [first‑come allocations](https://www.gradetograd.com/blog/illinois-map-grant-2025-how-to-qualify) make early FAFSA filing essential.
- Students with moderate incomes should check [AIM HIGH criteria](https://financialaid.illinoisstate.edu/scholarships/aim-high/) at each public university, since awards and income caps differ by campus.
- Because MAP covers tuition/fees only, students should plan separately for [living expenses](https://www.luc.edu/finaid/grants/illinois-map-grants/) via institutional scholarships or work‑study.
- Out-of-state enrollment sacrifices access to both MAP and AIM HIGH, significantly reducing state-level support.

## Indiana

### Overview

Indiana offers a robust, multi-tiered aid system that is relatively strong for low-income students, highlighted by [21st Century Scholars](https://learnmoreindiana.org/scholars/) (early promise) and the [Frank O’Bannon](https://indianastate.edu/costs-aid/financial-aid/scholarships-and-grants/frank-obannon-grant) need-based grant. [Workforce Ready Grants](https://www.in.gov/dwd/nextleveljobs/workforce-ready-grant/) add a separate, tuition-free pathway for adults seeking high-value certificates.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### 21st Century Scholars

Type:
Early-commitment, [need-based college promise scholarship](https://21centuryscholars.indiana.edu/index.html) covering up to 100% tuition at public colleges.

Award Amount:

- Pays up to [100% of tuition](https://www.ivytech.edu/admissions/ivy-tech-for-21st-century-scholars/) at eligible Indiana public colleges and universities; partial awards at participating private institutions.
- Does not cover books, housing, or other non-tuition fees.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Indiana resident; U.S. citizen or eligible noncitizen.
- Students are now [automatically enrolled](https://www.chalkbeat.org/indiana/2025/10/06/21st-century-scholars-program-grows-as-college-going-rate-falls/) in 7th–8th grade if they are financially eligible for free/reduced-price lunch; others must apply by 8th grade in ScholarTrack.
- Family income must meet state guidelines at enrollment; students must maintain a [Scholar Success Program checklist](https://learnmoreindiana.org/scholars/) and meet high school completion requirements.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Eligible Indiana public two‑ and four‑year institutions (tuition up to 100%).
- Approved [Indiana private institutions](https://financialservices.indianatech.edu/aid-programs/state-aid-programs/21stcentury/) (partial tuition coverage).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Students must maintain a minimum high school GPA (typically around 2.5) and complete required “Scholar Success” activities.
- College renewal requires full-time enrollment, SAP, and [completion of at least 30 credits](https://www.ivytech.edu/admissions/ivy-tech-for-21st-century-scholars/) per academic year at some institutions.

Application Process:

- Automatic enrollment for income-eligible 7th–8th graders; others apply via [ScholarTrack](https://learnmoreindiana.org/scholars/) in middle school.
- FAFSA must be filed each year of college to receive the award.

Deadline:

- Enrollment: by the end of 8th grade; FAFSA: by [April 15](https://indianapolis.iu.edu/cost-aid/grants/frank-obannon.html) each year for on‑time consideration.


#### Frank O’Bannon Grant (Higher Education Award & Freedom of Choice Award)

Type:
Primary statewide [need-based grant](https://indianastate.edu/costs-aid/financial-aid/scholarships-and-grants/frank-obannon-grant) for undergraduates.

Award Amount:

- Award amounts vary by institution type and SAI; current cycles show ranges up to about [$5,300 annually](https://www.in.gov/che/files/2025-2026-Frank-OBannon-Grant-Final.pdf) toward tuition and required fees.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Indiana resident by December 31 the year before applying.
- U.S. citizen or eligible noncitizen.
- High school graduate or GED holder, pursuing [first associate or bachelor’s degree](https://www.south.edu/frank-o-bannon-grant/).
- Enrolled [full-time (12+ credits)](https://indianapolis.iu.edu/cost-aid/grants/frank-obannon.html) at an eligible Indiana institution.
- Demonstrated financial need via FAFSA; must meet SAP.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Indiana [public universities](https://www.in.gov/che/files/2024-2025-Frank-OBannon-Grant-Finalized.pdf) (Higher Education Award) and eligible private and proprietary institutions (Freedom of Choice Award).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Limited to [8 semesters](https://indianastate.edu/costs-aid/financial-aid/scholarships-and-grants/frank-obannon-grant) of full-time funding, generally to be used within 8 years of initial award.
- [“Fast Track” incentives](https://www.in.gov/che/files/2025-2026-Frank-OBannon-Grant-Final.pdf) can increase awards for students completing 30+ credits in the year.

Application Process:

- Submit FAFSA by the state deadline, listing an eligible Indiana institution.
- No separate application; the [Commission for Higher Education](https://www.in.gov/che/files/2024-2025-Frank-OBannon-Grant-Finalized.pdf) evaluates eligibility.

Deadline:

- FAFSA must be filed by [April 15](https://indianapolis.iu.edu/cost-aid/grants/frank-obannon.html) for full consideration.


#### Workforce Ready Grant (Next Level Jobs)

Type:
Adult-focused, [last-dollar tuition](https://www.in.gov/che/state-financial-aid/state-financial-aid-by-program/workforce-ready-grant/) and mandatory-fee grant for high-value certificate programs.

Award Amount:

- Covers tuition and mandatory fees for approved high‑value certificate programs; does not cover program-specific equipment.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Indiana resident and U.S. citizen or eligible noncitizen.
- High school diploma (or equivalent) but [less than a college degree](https://www.in.gov/dwd/nextleveljobs/workforce-ready-grant/).
- Enrolled in an [approved high‑value certificate program](https://www.in.gov/che/state-financial-aid/state-financial-aid-by-program/workforce-ready-grant/) in fields like advanced manufacturing, IT, or health sciences.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Ivy Tech, Vincennes University, [Indiana Tech](https://www.indianatech.edu/news/indiana-tech-adds-to-opportunities-available-through-the-workforce-ready-grant/), and other approved training providers.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Available for up to two years, covering only the credits required by the qualifying program.
- [Not income-limited](https://www.in.gov/dwd/nextleveljobs/workforce-ready-grant/), making it accessible to a wide range of adult learners.

Application Process:

- Start via [NextLevelJobs.org](https://www.in.gov/dwd/nextleveljobs/workforce-ready-grant/) survey and connect to an eligible training provider.

Deadline:

- [No April 15 deadline](https://www.in.gov/che/state-financial-aid/state-financial-aid-by-program/workforce-ready-grant/); students may apply year-round.


### Key Insights for This State

- Indiana is strong for low-income students who engage early: [21st Century Scholars](https://learnmoreindiana.org/scholars/) plus Frank O’Bannon can nearly eliminate tuition at public institutions.
- Meeting [credit-completion milestones](https://indianapolis.iu.edu/cost-aid/grants/frank-obannon.html) (often 30 credits/year) materially increases state grant amounts and keeps students “on-time.”
- Adults without degrees should look closely at [Workforce Ready Grants](https://indianapublicmedia.org/news/state-grant-pays-for-adult-skills-training-certifications-in-high-demand-fields.php) as a tuition-free reskilling pathway.
- Missing the **April 15 FAFSA deadline** sharply limits access to [Frank O’Bannon](https://www.in.gov/che/files/2024-2025-Frank-OBannon-Grant-Finalized.pdf) and can reduce total aid.

## Iowa

### Overview

Iowa’s aid system is relatively robust and diversified, with separate grants for private colleges, community college CTE, and statewide scholarships like the [All Iowa Opportunity Scholarship](https://educate.iowa.gov/higher-ed/financial-aid/scholarships-grants/aios). Many programs require both the FAFSA and a [state financial aid application](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/11649/download?inline) by early deadlines.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### All Iowa Opportunity Scholarship (AIOS)

Type:
Need-based scholarship with additional priorities (e.g., foster youth, TRIO, GEAR UP) for [recent high school graduates](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/10253/download?inline).

Award Amount:

- Maximum awards range from roughly [$5,198 to $5,334 per year](https://educate.iowa.gov/higher-ed/financial-aid/scholarships-grants/aios) in recent cycles.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Iowa resident graduating high school within the [last two years](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/10253/download?inline).
- Demonstrated financial need (SAI at or below [roughly $10,400–$10,669](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/11649/download?inline), depending on the year).
- Enrolled in at least 3 semester hours at an [eligible Iowa college](https://financialaid.iastate.edu/types-of-aid/scholarships/state-of-iowa-awards/) or university.
- Priority to students from [foster care](https://educate.iowa.gov/higher-ed/financial-aid/scholarships-grants/aios), children of deceased public safety workers, TRIO participants, and GEAR UP students.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Eligible Iowa [public and private](https://financialaid.iastate.edu/types-of-aid/scholarships/state-of-iowa-awards/) colleges and universities.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Limited funding; awards prioritized to high-need and priority groups before other applicants.

Application Process:

- Complete the FAFSA plus the [Iowa Financial Aid Application](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/10253/download?inline).

Deadline:

- Both applications are [due by April 1](https://educate.iowa.gov/higher-ed/financial-aid/scholarships-grants/aios).


#### Iowa Tuition Grant – Not-for-Profit

Type:
Need-based grant for Iowa residents attending [private nonprofit colleges](https://www.iasfaa.com/assets/docs/Conference-ppt/S2025%20FA%20101%20Iowa%20Financial%20Aid%20Programs%20%20-%20Read-Only.pdf).

Award Amount:

- Maximum around [$7,500 per year](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/11649/download?inline) for eligible students.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Iowa resident enrolled in at least 3 semester hours in an associate or bachelor’s program at an [eligible nonprofit private college](https://www.iasfaa.com/assets/docs/Conference-ppt/S2025%20FA%20101%20Iowa%20Financial%20Aid%20Programs%20%20-%20Read-Only.pdf).
- SAI at or [below about 16,000](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/10253/download?inline) (current limit).

Where It Can Be Used:

- Participating [Iowa nonprofit private](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/11649/download?inline) institutions.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Sliding scale based on SAI; higher-need students receive larger awards.

Application Process:

- Submit FAFSA by state deadline and list an eligible Iowa nonprofit institution.

Deadline:

- FAFSA [due by July 1](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/11649/download?inline) for the upcoming academic year.


#### Iowa Vocational‑Technical Tuition Grant

Type:
Need-based grant for community college [career and technical education (CTE)](https://www.nicc.edu/financial-aid/scholarships/iowa-kibbie-grant/) programs.

Award Amount:

- Maximum around [$1,000–$1,200 per year](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/11649/download?inline); awards on a sliding scale based on SAI.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Iowa resident enrolled in an [eligible CTE program](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/10253/download?inline) at an Iowa community college.
- At least 3 semester hours of enrollment.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [Iowa community colleges](https://www.nicc.edu/financial-aid/scholarships/iowa-kibbie-grant/) offering approved vocational-technical programs.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Need-based; awards may be modest but can [stack with other grants](https://www.nicc.edu/financial-aid/scholarships/iowa-kibbie-grant/).

Application Process:

- FAFSA only; [no separate state application](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/11649/download?inline) required.

Deadline:

- FAFSA [due by July 1](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/10253/download?inline).


#### Kibbie Grant (Iowa Skilled Workforce Shortage Tuition Grant)

Type:
Need-based grant for [shortage-area CTE programs](https://educate.iowa.gov/higher-ed/financial-aid/scholarships-grants/kibbie-grant) at community colleges.

Award Amount:

- Statutory maximum of half the average community college tuition and fees; [maximum 2025–26 award](https://educate.iowa.gov/higher-ed/financial-aid/scholarships-grants/kibbie-grant) is around $3,000.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Iowa resident enrolled in a [specified shortage-area program](https://www.nicc.edu/financial-aid/scholarships/iowa-kibbie-grant/).
- At least 3 credit hours in an eligible program of at least 15 weeks.
- SAI at or [below about 6,000](https://educate.iowa.gov/higher-ed/financial-aid/scholarships-grants/kibbie-grant) for the 2025–26 cycle.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [Iowa community colleges](https://educate.iowa.gov/higher-ed/financial-aid/scholarships-grants/kibbie-grant) and specifically approved programs like nursing, welding, and IT.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Award amount depends on [funding and recipient count](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/11649/download?inline) and may be less than the statutory maximum.

Application Process:

- FAFSA required; no separate application, but students must [enroll in an eligible program](https://educate.iowa.gov/higher-ed/financial-aid/scholarships-grants/kibbie-grant).

Deadline:

- FAFSA by [July 1](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/10253/download?inline) for the following academic year.


### Key Insights for This State

- Iowa is relatively strong for residents at private nonprofits through the [Iowa Tuition Grant](https://www.iasfaa.com/assets/docs/Conference-ppt/S2025%20FA%20101%20Iowa%20Financial%20Aid%20Programs%20%20-%20Read-Only.pdf), provided they meet the July 1 FAFSA deadline.
- The [All Iowa Opportunity Scholarship](https://educate.iowa.gov/higher-ed/financial-aid/scholarships-grants/aios) is high value but targeted; priority groups should ensure they hit the April 1 cutoff.
- Community college students in shortage fields can often [stack Voc‑Tech and Kibbie grants](https://www.nicc.edu/financial-aid/scholarships/iowa-kibbie-grant/), significantly lowering costs.
- Timely filing is mandatory for almost every major program; [late filers risk losing aid](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/11649/download?inline) regardless of financial need.

## Kansas

### Overview

Kansas offers a mix of traditional need-based grants and newer [last-dollar workforce programs](https://sccc.edu/admissions-and-aid/financial-aid/types-of-aid/scholarships/kansas-promise/) like the Kansas Promise Scholarship. Aid is moderate overall but particularly favorable for [in-demand community and technical programs](https://www.washburntech.edu/admissions/paying-for-college/Kansas-Promise.html).

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Kansas Promise Scholarship

Type:
Workforce-oriented, last-dollar [service scholarship](https://www.jccc.edu/admissions/financial-aid/aid-types/scholarships/kansas-promise/) for specified programs at community and technical colleges.

Award Amount:

- Covers remaining tuition, required fees, books, and materials after other gift aid, up to specified [limits and credit hour caps](https://wsutech.edu/kansaspromise/).

Eligibility Requirements:

- U.S. citizen and [Kansas resident](https://www.washburntech.edu/admissions/paying-for-college/Kansas-Promise.html).
- Enrolled at least half-time in an [approved Promise-eligible program](https://www.butlercc.edu/getting-started/cost-financial-aid/scholarships/kansas-promise-scholarships).
- Meet residency criteria, such as being a recent Kansas high school graduate or a [3-year resident](https://www.jccc.edu/admissions/financial-aid/aid-types/scholarships/kansas-promise/).
- Must meet [income limits](https://www.jccc.edu/admissions/financial-aid/aid-types/scholarships/kansas-promise/) set by the Kansas Board of Regents.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [Kansas community and technical colleges](https://sccc.edu/admissions-and-aid/financial-aid/types-of-aid/scholarships/kansas-promise/), and some other institutions offering approved programs.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- **Last-dollar:** covers only the [gap remaining](https://wsutech.edu/kansaspromise/) after other grants and scholarships.
- **Service obligation:** recipients must [live and work in Kansas](https://sccc.edu/admissions-and-aid/financial-aid/types-of-aid/scholarships/kansas-promise/) for at least two consecutive years after completion, or the grant converts to a loan.
- Funding is limited and awarded [first-come, first-served](https://www.butlercc.edu/getting-started/cost-financial-aid/scholarships/kansas-promise-scholarships).

Application Process:

- Apply through the [Kansas Board of Regents (KBOR)](https://www.washburntech.edu/admissions/paying-for-college/Kansas-Promise.html) application and complete the FAFSA.

Deadline:

- Typical filing deadlines: [July 1 (summer), October 1 (fall), and March 1 (spring)](https://sccc.edu/admissions-and-aid/financial-aid/types-of-aid/scholarships/kansas-promise/).


#### Kansas Comprehensive Grant

Where It Can Be Used:

### Key Insights for This State

- Kansas is particularly generous for students in high-demand fields through the [Promise Scholarship](https://wsutech.edu/kansaspromise/), often making programs tuition-free in exchange for a service commitment.

- Early FAFSA and [KBOR application submission](https://sccc.edu/admissions-and-aid/financial-aid/types-of-aid/scholarships/kansas-promise/) significantly improves your chances of receiving limited state funds.
- Students should carefully consider the [repayment penalty](https://www.jccc.edu/admissions/financial-aid/aid-types/scholarships/kansas-promise/) if they cannot meet the two-year residency and work requirement.

## Kentucky

### Overview

Kentucky offers a solid combination of [merit-based awards](https://www.kheaa.com/pdf/pubs/KEES_Brochure.pdf), [need-based grants](https://www.petersons.com/scholarship/kheaa-college-access-program-grant-111_150093.aspx), and [workforce scholarships](https://southcentral.kctcs.edu/news/2025/work-ready-scholarship-closing.aspx), making it relatively strong for in-state students who stay on top of requirements. Many programs are not income-tested but still require timely applications.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Kentucky Educational Excellence Scholarship (KEES)

Type:
Merit-based scholarship earned annually from high school GPA and test scores, with some [CTP eligibility](https://www.kheaa.com/web/scholarships-grants.faces).

Award Amount:

- Annual “base” KEES award per year of high school ranges from about **$125** (for 2.5 GPA) to **$500** (for 4.0 GPA); a student with a 4.0 all four years can [accumulate about $2,000 total](https://www.kheaa.com/pdf/pubs/KEES_Brochure.pdf).
- Additional [“bonus” awards](https://www.kheaa.com/web/scholarships-grants.faces) are available for high ACT/SAT and AP/IB/CAI scores.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Attend and graduate from a certified Kentucky high school; [homeschool and GED students](https://www.kheaa.com/web/scholarships-grants.faces) can qualify via test scores.
- Earn at least a **2.5 GPA** each high school year while meeting [KEES curriculum requirements](https://www.kheaa.com/pdf/pubs/KEES_Brochure.pdf).
- Not be a convicted felon.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Participating Kentucky colleges and universities, including some [comprehensive transition and postsecondary (CTP)](https://www.kheaa.com/web/scholarships-grants.faces) programs for students with intellectual disabilities.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- CTP students receive flat KEES CTP awards ($500 for 6+ credits; $250 for less than 6 credits per term).
- College renewal requires at least half-time enrollment and [satisfactory academic progress](https://www.kheaa.com/pdf/pubs/KEES_Brochure.pdf).

Application Process:

- **No separate application;** KEES is automatically calculated from high school records and applied when students enroll at eligible institutions.

Deadline:

- No explicit student deadline; maintaining your GPA during high school triggers the award.


#### Kentucky College Access Program (CAP) Grant

Type:
Need-based state grant for [low-income undergraduates](https://www.bellarmine.edu/financial-aid/scholarships-funding/kentucky-state-assistance.php).

Award Amount:

- For 2025–26, awards can reach [up to $5,300 per year](https://www.petersons.com/scholarship/kheaa-college-access-program-grant-111_150093.aspx) at some institutions.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Kentucky resident undergraduate at an [eligible public or private college](https://www.petersons.com/scholarship/kheaa-college-access-program-grant-111_150093.aspx), university, or technical school.
- Must not currently be in high school.
- Demonstrated financial need; often restricted to [Pell-eligible students](https://www.bellarmine.edu/financial-aid/scholarships-funding/kentucky-state-assistance.php) with unmet need.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Participating Kentucky public and private colleges, universities, and technical institutions.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Funding is limited and typically awarded on a **first-come, first-served** basis until appropriations are exhausted.

Application Process:

- Submit the FAFSA listing a Kentucky institution; CAP is awarded automatically to eligible students.

Deadline:

- No fixed date, but students are [strongly advised to file the FAFSA](https://www.petersons.com/scholarship/kheaa-college-access-program-grant-111_150093.aspx) as soon as possible after it opens.


#### Work Ready Kentucky Scholarship (WRKS)

Type:
[Last-dollar workforce scholarship](https://www.kentucky.gov/Pages/Activity-stream.aspx?n=EducationCabinet&prId=242) for high-demand certificate, diploma, and associate programs.

Award Amount:

- Covers remaining tuition and some mandatory fees (capped around $400 per year) after Pell, CAP, KEES, and other tuition-specific aid; coverage is [capped at in-state KCTCS rates](https://hopkinsville.kctcs.edu/affording-college/paying-for-college/work-ready-kentucky.aspx).

Eligibility Requirements:

- Kentucky resident with a high school diploma or working toward a GED.
- No associate degree or higher already; targeting [first-time postsecondary credentials](https://southcentral.kctcs.edu/news/2025/work-ready-scholarship-closing.aspx).
- Enrolled at least half-time in an [approved high-demand program](https://www.kentucky.gov/Pages/Activity-stream.aspx?n=EducationCabinet&prId=242) (e.g., healthcare, manufacturing, IT).

Where It Can Be Used:

- Primarily [KCTCS institutions](https://hopkinsville.kctcs.edu/affording-college/paying-for-college/work-ready-kentucky.aspx) and other eligible schools offering WRKS-approved programs.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- **Not based on income or GPA;** open to a wide range of adults.
- Approval is program-specific; changing majors may require [verifying continued eligibility](https://southcentral.kctcs.edu/news/2025/work-ready-scholarship-closing.aspx).

Application Process:

- Apply via the [Work Ready Kentucky Scholarship portal](https://kwib.ky.gov/Documents/WRKS_Presentation_KWIB_PSL_050422.pdf) and complete the FAFSA.

Deadline:

- For many schools, applications are due by **December 1** for upcoming terms; earlier application [increases chances of funding](https://southcentral.kctcs.edu/news/2025/work-ready-scholarship-closing.aspx).


### Key Insights for This State

- Kentucky is strong for in-state students: [KEES](https://www.kheaa.com/pdf/pubs/KEES_Brochure.pdf) can significantly reduce tuition based on high school performance.
- CAP Grant funds [often run out early](https://www.petersons.com/scholarship/kheaa-college-access-program-grant-111_150093.aspx); file your FAFSA as soon as possible.
- Adults seeking short-term credentials can often attend community college [tuition-free through Work Ready](https://hopkinsville.kctcs.edu/affording-college/paying-for-college/work-ready-kentucky.aspx), regardless of income.
- Stacking KEES, CAP, and WRKS can [dramatically reduce](https://hopkinsville.kctcs.edu/affording-college/paying-for-college/work-ready-kentucky.aspx) out-of-pocket costs at community/technical colleges.

## Louisiana

### Overview

Louisiana provides substantial merit support through [TOPS](https://mylosfa.la.gov/students-parents/scholarships-grants/tops/) but relatively modest need-based aid via the [GO Grant](https://mylosfa.la.gov/students-parents/scholarships-grants/go-grant/), making it strong for in-state students who meet academic criteria and file the FAFSA. Aid is significantly more limited for students who miss merit thresholds or enroll part-time.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Taylor Opportunity Program for Students (TOPS)

Type:
Merit-based state scholarship with multiple award tiers; tuition-focused.

Award Amount:

- Covers the [tuition portion of institutional charges](https://www.rpcc.edu/financial-aid/tops-scholarships) at eligible Louisiana public colleges and universities.
- Offers several [award tracks](https://mylosfa.la.gov/students-parents/scholarships-grants/tops/): Opportunity, Performance, Honors, and Tech, each with different GPA and ACT/SAT requirements.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Louisiana resident and U.S. citizen or eligible permanent resident.
- Graduate from an [eligible Louisiana high school](https://www.collegescholarships.com/scholarships/detail/131699) with required TOPS core curriculum units and a minimum core GPA (typically 2.50+).
- Meet minimum [ACT composite scores](https://mylosfa.la.gov/students-parents/scholarships-grants/tops/) based on the state average.
- Enroll [full-time](https://www.susla.edu/page/louisiana-tops-program) (12+ credits) at an eligible Louisiana institution within one year of graduation.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Louisiana public colleges and universities, [community and technical colleges](https://mylosfa.la.gov/students-parents/scholarships-grants/tops/), and participating independent colleges.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Covers [tuition only](https://www.susla.edu/page/louisiana-tops-program); does not pay for books, fees, or living expenses.
- Renewal requires completion of [24 credit hours per academic year](https://www.rpcc.edu/financial-aid/tops-scholarships) and maintaining a minimum cumulative GPA.
- Funding is subject to legislative appropriations; in lean years, benefits may be reduced.

Application Process:

- File the FAFSA (recommended) or, if not applying for federal aid, a TOPS Online Application.

Deadline:

- Students are [strongly encouraged to apply by July 1](https://www.rpcc.edu/financial-aid/tops-scholarships) after high school graduation to avoid losing eligibility.


#### Louisiana GO Grant

Type:
Need-based state grant that [supplements the Pell Grant](https://mylosfa.la.gov/students-parents/scholarships-grants/go-grant/) for low- to moderate-income students.

Award Amount:

- Annual awards range from a [minimum of $300 to a maximum of $3,000](https://www.careerexplorerswla.com/resources/louisiana-go-grant/), with actual amounts determined by institutional packaging policies.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Louisiana resident and [Federal Pell Grant recipient](https://www.susla.edu/page/louisiana-go-grant).
- Have [remaining financial need](https://studentscholarships.org/scholarship/10108/louisiana-go-grant-program) after subtracting Student Aid Index (SAI) and all other gift aid from the cost of attendance.
- Enrolled at least [half-time (6+ credit hours)](https://mylosfa.la.gov/students-parents/scholarships-grants/go-grant/) at an eligible Louisiana institution.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Eligible [public and private colleges and universities](https://www.nicholls.edu/financial-aid/types-of-financial-aid/louisiana-go-grant/) in Louisiana.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Awards [cannot be used for summer terms](https://www.careerexplorerswla.com/resources/louisiana-go-grant/).
- Lifetime limits are [tied to Pell Grant lifetime limits](https://studentscholarships.org/scholarship/10108/louisiana-go-grant-program).

Application Process:

- File the FAFSA; institutions [automatically determine eligibility](https://mylosfa.la.gov/students-parents/scholarships-grants/go-grant/) and award amounts based on state allocations.

Deadline:

- No explicit state deadline beyond FAFSA timing, but early filing is advised as [funds may run out](https://mylosfa.la.gov/students-parents/scholarships-grants/go-grant/) at the institutional level.


### Key Insights for This State

- Louisiana is relatively generous for academically strong in‑state students via TOPS, especially at public universities where [tuition may be fully covered](https://mylosfa.la.gov/students-parents/scholarships-grants/tops/).
- Knowing the [TOPS core requirements](https://www.collegescholarships.com/scholarships/detail/131699) early in high school is critical, as students just below thresholds may receive little state tuition help.
- The [GO Grant](https://www.careerexplorerswla.com/resources/louisiana-go-grant/) provides essential support for Pell-eligible students but is capped and restricted to fall/spring terms.
- Filing the FAFSA by [July 1](https://www.rpcc.edu/financial-aid/tops-scholarships) ensures TOPS consideration and opens the door for the GO Grant and federal aid.

### Overview

Maine’s primary statewide aid vehicle is the **Maine State Grant Program (MSGP)**, a need-based grant administered by FAME. #### Maine State Grant Program (MSGP)

Type:
Primary need-based state grant for undergraduate residents.

Award Amount:

- For the **2026–27** academic year, the maximum grant remains **$2,500** for full-time students.
- Part-time students (at least 6 credits) may receive up to **$1,250**.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Maine resident undergraduate enrolled in an eligible degree or certificate program.
- Student Aid Index (SAI) must be **10,000 or below** for priority consideration.
- Must file the FAFSA by the state deadline.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Eligible Maine public and private colleges and universities.

- **Priority Funding:** Funding is limited; if you file after the priority deadline, you are only considered on a first-come, first-served basis if funds remain.

Application Process:

- File the **FAFSA**; no separate MSGP application is required.

Deadline:

- **May 1, 2026** is the priority deadline for the 2026–27 academic year.


#### Maine Free College Scholarship

Type:
Last-dollar scholarship that covers tuition at Maine’s community colleges for recent high school graduates.

Award Amount:

Where It Can Be Used:

- Any of the seven [Maine Community Colleges](https://www.mccs.me.edu/free-college/).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- **Completion Time:** Class of 2026 students must complete their degree within **150%** of the standard time (e.g., 3 years for an associate degree).
- **No Income Limit:** This program is not based on financial need, making it accessible to all qualifying graduates.

## Maryland

### Overview

Maryland has a relatively strong, need-heavy aid system, anchored by the **Howard P. Rawlings Guaranteed Access (GA)** and **Educational Assistance (EA)** Grants and a community college **Promise** program. For low-income students, the combination can cover most or all tuition and sometimes full demonstrated need.

---

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Howard P. Rawlings Guaranteed Access (GA) Grant

Type:
High-need, need-based grant that can function like a full-need scholarship for qualifying students.

Award Amount:

- For the **2026–27** award year, the maximum grant is **$18,000 per year**.
- It is designed to cover **100% of a student’s calculated financial need**. The minimum award is **$100**.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Maryland resident eligible for in-state tuition; U.S. citizen or eligible noncitizen. (Undocumented students use the **MHEC One-App**).
- Must be **under age 26** at the time of first receipt.
- High school diploma or GED (minimum score of 145 per module); must begin college within **6 years** of completion.
- Full-time enrollment (12+ credits) at an eligible Maryland two- or four-year institution.
- **Income Limit:** Initial applicants must have an annual family income at or below **130% of the federal poverty level** (e.g., ~$33,566 for a family of 3).

Where It Can Be Used:

- Approved Maryland public and private colleges and universities.

Application Process:

- File the **FAFSA** or **MHEC One-App** by the priority deadline.

Deadline:

- **March 1, 2026** is the hard priority deadline.
- Any requested supporting documentation is typically due by **August 1, 2026**.


#### Howard P. Rawlings Educational Assistance (EA) Grant

Type:
Broad need-based grant for undergraduates with low-to-moderate income.

Award Amount:

- Maximum annual award is **$3,000**; minimum is **$400**.
- Exact amounts vary based on Student Aid Index (SAI) and the institution's cost of attendance.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Maryland resident attending an eligible in-state institution full-time.
- Must demonstrate financial need as calculated by the Maryland Higher Education Commission (MHEC).
- Recipients who have used the award for two years must complete **30 credits annually** to maintain the full renewal amount.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Maryland public and private colleges and universities.

Application Process:

- No separate application; eligibility is determined automatically via the **FAFSA** or **MHEC One-App**.

Deadline:

- **March 1, 2026** for the FAFSA/MHEC One-App. Supporting docs due **August 1, 2026**.


#### Maryland Community College Promise Scholarship

Type:
Last-dollar scholarship covering tuition and mandatory fees at community colleges.

Award Amount:

- Up to **$5,000 per year** to cover remaining tuition and fees after all other federal and state aid (like Pell or EA Grants) have been applied.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Maryland high school graduate or GED recipient.
- Enrolled in at least **6 credits per semester** (part-time) or **12 credits** (full-time) for credit-bearing programs, or an approved non-credit sequence leading to licensure.
- **GPA Requirement:** High school seniors need a minimum unweighted **2.3 GPA**; current community college students need a **2.5 GPA**.
- **Income Limits:** Not more than **$100,000** for single/single-parent households or **$150,000** for married/two-parent households.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Any Maryland community college. Generally, you must attend the college in your county unless your program isn't offered there.

Application Process:

- Submit the **FAFSA** or **MHEC One-App**.

Deadline:

- **April 1, 2026** for the 2026–27 academic year.

---

### Key Insights for This State

* **The "Safety Net":** For the most financially vulnerable students, the **GA Grant** is one of the most generous in the country, potentially providing up to $18,000 to eliminate the need for student loans.
* **The March 1 Cliff:** Missing the **March 1 deadline** is often fatal for GA and EA Grant eligibility. However, MHEC does offer a "Late FAFSA Appeal" through **July 1** for students with specific extenuating circumstances.
* **Community College "Bridge":** The Promise Scholarship ensures that middle-income families (up to $150k) can still access a tuition-free pathway at the community college level.
* **One-App for All:** Maryland's **MHEC One-App** ensures that undocumented students who meet in-state tuition requirements (via the Maryland Dream Act) have access to the same state grant pools as FAFSA filers.

## Massachusetts

### Overview

- [Generous state](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/massachusetts/) with robust [need-based grants](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/massgrant.asp), last-dollar tuition programs, and strong [merit scholarships](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/massgrant-massgrant-plus) for public institutions.
- Heavy emphasis on low- and moderate-income residents, with [free community college](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/documents/publications/2025/OSFA%202025-2026%20Guidelines/2025-2026%20MASSGrant%20Plus%20Expansion.pdf) and expanded support for [both Pell and non‑Pell students](https://catalog.worcester.edu/undergraduate/financial-aid/grants-waivers-massachusetts-residents/).


### Major State Aid Programs

#### MASSGrant

Type: [Need-based grant](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/massgrant.asp).

Award Amount:

- Typical awards at public institutions roughly [$500–$3,000 per year](https://catalog.worcester.edu/undergraduate/financial-aid/grants-waivers-massachusetts-residents/), varying by [need and institution](https://www.house.mn.gov/hrd/pubs/ss/ssmsgp.pdf).

Eligibility Requirements:

- [Massachusetts resident](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/massgrant.asp) meeting state residency rules; U.S. citizen or eligible noncitizen.

- Enrolled [full-time](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/massgrant.asp) (≥12 credits) in an eligible certificate, associate, or bachelor’s program; no prior bachelor’s degree.
- Demonstrated [financial need](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/massgrant.asp) after considering SAI and other grants/scholarships; must maintain satisfactory academic progress and not be in loan default.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Approved [Massachusetts public universities](https://catalog.worcester.edu/undergraduate/financial-aid/grants-waivers-massachusetts-residents/), community colleges, and independent colleges.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Award level depends on [legislative funding](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/massgrant.asp) and institutional cost of attendance.

Application Process:

- File [FAFSA](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/documents/publications/2025/OSFA%202025-2026%20Guidelines/2025-2026%20MASSGrant%20Guidelines1.pdf) annually (or MASFA if ineligible for FAFSA).

Deadline:

- State FAFSA deadline for 2025–26 listed as [June 30, 2025](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/massgrant.asp); some campuses (e.g., Worcester State) use earlier [priority dates](https://catalog.worcester.edu/undergraduate/financial-aid/grants-waivers-massachusetts-residents/) such as May 1, 2025.


#### MASSGrant Plus & MASSGrant Plus Expansion

Type: Need-based, largely [last-dollar tuition/fee coverage](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/massgrant-massgrant-plus) at public colleges.

Award Amount:

- [Covers remaining in‑state tuition](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/massachusetts/) and mandatory fees at Massachusetts public community colleges and four-year campuses after other grants/scholarships.
- At four-year campuses, may include an additional [book/supplies allowance](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/massachusetts/) of up to about $1,200.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Massachusetts resident [enrolled at least half-time](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/massgrant-plus) at an eligible public community college or four-year campus.
- MASSGrant Plus: generally for Pell‑eligible students; [Plus Expansion](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/documents/publications/2025/OSFA%202025-2026%20Guidelines/2025-2026%20MASSGrant%20Plus%20Expansion.pdf) for non‑Pell students with SAI up to 15,000.
- No prior bachelor’s degree; must meet [satisfactory academic progress](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/massgrant-massgrant-plus) and loan/default rules.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [Massachusetts community colleges](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/massgrant-massgrant-plus) and public four-year campuses (UMass and state universities).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Funding and exact rules [subject to annual appropriations](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/documents/publications/2025/OSFA%202025-2026%20Guidelines/2025-2026%20MASSGrant%20Plus%20Expansion.pdf); awards not guaranteed if funds are insufficient.
- Tuition/fee focused; does not guarantee [coverage of housing](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/massachusetts/) or other living costs.

Application Process:

- Complete [FAFSA (or MASFA)](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/documents/publications/2025/OSFA%202025-2026%20Guidelines/2025-2026%20MASSGrant%20Plus%20Expansion.pdf) and institutional aid process; no separate MASSGrant Plus application.

Deadline:

- Same general [state deadline](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/massgrant.asp) as MASSGrant (FAFSA by June 30, 2025 for 2025–26), but students should follow earlier [campus priority dates](https://catalog.worcester.edu/undergraduate/financial-aid/grants-waivers-massachusetts-residents/).


#### Free Community College (MassEducate & MassReconnect)

Type: [Last-dollar tuition and fee programs](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/massachusetts/) for community college (MassEducate for broader ages; MassReconnect for adults 25+).

Award Amount:

- [Covers in‑state tuition and mandatory fees](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/massachusetts/) at Massachusetts community colleges for eligible students.

Eligibility Requirements (high-level):

- Massachusetts residents enrolling at an in-state community college; [MassReconnect](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/massachusetts/) specifically targets adults 25+ without a prior degree.
- Must complete FAFSA and any college-specific steps; [income rules](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/massachusetts/) are looser for MassEducate but more targeted for MassReconnect.

Where It Can Be Used:

- All [Massachusetts community colleges](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/massachusetts/).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Programs are last-dollar: other [grants and scholarships apply first](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/massachusetts/), then the program covers remaining tuition/fees.
- Availability and specific criteria can [shift as the initiative evolves](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/massachusetts/) and is reauthorized.

Application Process:

- Apply to a Massachusetts community college and [file FAFSA](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/massachusetts/); program is administered through the college financial aid office.

Deadline:

- Follow [college admissions](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/massachusetts/) and FAFSA priority timelines; earlier filing improves chances of full coverage.


#### John and Abigail Adams Scholarship

Type: [Merit-based tuition credit](https://www.doe.mass.edu/scholarships/adams/default.html).

Award Amount:

- [Credit toward resident undergraduate tuition](https://www.mefa.org/article/what-to-know-about-the-john-abigail-adams-scholarship/) for up to 8 semesters at UMass, state universities, or community colleges (e.g., [UMass tuition credits](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/adams.asp) around $1,400–$1,700 per year depending on campus).

Eligibility Requirements:

- Massachusetts public school student with [strong grade 10 MCAS performance](https://studentscholarships.org/scholarship/9030/john-and-abigail-adams-scholarship): Advanced on at least one test, Proficient or Advanced on others, and combined score in top 25% of district.
- Must enroll [full-time](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/adams.asp) in an eligible public college within 6 years of high school graduation; no prior bachelor’s degree.
- U.S. citizen or eligible noncitizen; [must complete FAFSA](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/john-and-abigail-adams-scholarship) and meet SAP and loan/default rules.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [UMass campuses](https://www.mefa.org/article/what-to-know-about-the-john-abigail-adams-scholarship/), Massachusetts state universities, and Massachusetts community colleges.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- [Covers tuition only](https://www.doe.mass.edu/scholarships/adams/default.html); does not cover fees, housing, or other costs.
- [Renewable up to 8 semesters](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/adams.asp) with full-time enrollment and satisfactory progress.

Application Process:

- [No separate scholarship application](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/john-and-abigail-adams-scholarship); eligibility determined by MCAS and notified by the state; FAFSA still required for disbursement.

Deadline:

- Follow [FAFSA and institutional](https://www.doe.mass.edu/scholarships/adams/default.html) financial aid deadlines; scholarship must be activated within the allowed post‑graduation window.


#### Gilbert Matching Student Grant Program

Type: [Need-based grant](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/gilbert.asp) administered through participating institutions.

Award Amount:

- [$200–$2,500](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/documents/publications/2025/OSFA%202025-2026%20Guidelines/2025-2026%20Gilbert%20Grant%20Guidelines.pdf) per academic year, depending on [institutional policies](https://www.collegescholarships.com/scholarships/detail/133052) and funding.

Eligibility Requirements:

- [Massachusetts resident](https://www.collegescholarships.com/scholarships/detail/133052) (student or supporting parent) for at least 12 months prior to academic year; U.S. citizen or eligible noncitizen.
- [Full-time undergraduate](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/gilbert.asp) at a participating Massachusetts independent college or school of nursing; no prior bachelor’s degree.
- Demonstrated [financial need](https://www.collegescholarships.com/scholarships/detail/133052), satisfactory academic progress, and not in default on education loans.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Participating [Massachusetts independent institutions](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/documents/publications/2025/OSFA%202025-2026%20Guidelines/2025-2026%20Gilbert%20Grant%20Guidelines.pdf) and schools of nursing.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- [Institutions must match state funds](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/gilbert.asp); number and size of awards vary by campus and appropriation.

Application Process:

- [File FAFSA (or MASFA)](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/documents/publications/2025/OSFA%202025-2026%20Guidelines/2025-2026%20Gilbert%20Grant%20Guidelines.pdf) and follow the college’s internal Gilbert/need-based aid process.

Deadline:

- [Set by each participating institution](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/gilbert.asp); students should treat FAFSA priority dates as the practical deadline.


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs (Massachusetts)

- Various state-funded [tuition waivers and cash grants](https://catalog.worcester.edu/undergraduate/financial-aid/grants-waivers-massachusetts-residents/) exist (e.g., Cash Grant, tuition waivers for specific populations), usually administered directly by campuses and [layered on top](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/massgrant.asp) of MASSGrant/MASSGrant Plus.


### Key Insights for Massachusetts

- [Very strong aid state](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/massgrant-massgrant-plus), especially for low- and moderate-income residents in community colleges and public universities.
- [Filing FAFSA early is critical](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/massgrant.asp); some campuses use [earlier priority dates](https://catalog.worcester.edu/undergraduate/financial-aid/grants-waivers-massachusetts-residents/) than the statewide June 30 deadline.
- High-achieving students can [significantly reduce tuition](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/adams.asp) at public campuses through the [Adams Scholarship](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/john-and-abigail-adams-scholarship) on top of need-based grants.
- Aid is fragmented across [several overlapping programs](https://catalog.worcester.edu/undergraduate/financial-aid/grants-waivers-massachusetts-residents/)—students should work closely with [campus financial aid](https://www.mass.edu/osfa/programs/massgrant.asp) to ensure they capture MASSGrant, Plus, free CC, and institutional waivers.

### Michigan

### Overview

- [Strong aid state](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/michigan/) with a large statewide “[Michigan Achievement Scholarship](https://www.michigan.gov/mistudentaid/programs/michigan-achievement-scholarship/college-and-university)” plus multiple targeted grants for low-income and Medicaid‑eligible students.
- Mix of need-based, hybrid merit/need, and pathway-specific programs; adult learners also benefit from separate [tuition-free community college initiatives](https://www.michigan.gov/mileap/higher-education/sixty-by-30/initiatives).


### Major State Aid Programs

#### Michigan Achievement Scholarship

Type: Need-based, large foundational state scholarship (quasi last-dollar).

Award Amount:

- Up to [$2,750 per year](https://studentscholarships.org/scholarship/32095/michigan-achievement-scholarship) at community colleges (up to 3 years).
- Up to [$5,500 per year](https://wayne.edu/financial-aid/types/mas) at public universities or baccalaureate programs (up to 5 years).
- Up to [$4,000 per year](https://www.wccan.org/resource/michigan-achievement-scholarship/) at private colleges (up to 5 years).

Eligibility Requirements:

- [Michigan resident](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/MILEAP/2024/09/09/file_attachments/2992453/Michigan%20Achievement%20Scholarship%20Webinar.pdf) (student and, if dependent, parent) since at least July 1 of prior year.
- High school diploma/equivalent earned in Michigan in [2023 or later](https://www.wwcsd.net/downloads/college_and_career_readiness/michigan_achievement_scholarship.pdf); must enroll in college within about 15 months of graduation.
- FAFSA filed by state deadline; [Student Aid Index (SAI) ≤ 30,000](https://www.michigan.gov/mistudentaid/programs/michigan-achievement-scholarship/college-and-university) for 2024–25 (similar thresholds set each year).
- Enrolled full-time in an eligible Michigan postsecondary institution; no prior bachelor’s degree.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Michigan [public universities](https://www.michigan.gov/mistudentaid/programs/michigan-achievement-scholarship/college-and-university), community colleges, and eligible private institutions.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Renewable up to 5 years, contingent on [satisfactory academic progress](https://wayne.edu/financial-aid/types/mas) and continued financial need.
- Functions alongside federal aid; not strictly last-dollar but substantially [reduces net tuition](https://studentscholarships.org/scholarship/32095/michigan-achievement-scholarship).

Application Process:

- [File FAFSA](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/MILEAP/2024/09/09/file_attachments/2992453/Michigan%20Achievement%20Scholarship%20Webinar.pdf); no separate state application—automatic consideration if eligibility criteria are met.

Deadline:

- Students must meet the annual state FAFSA deadline (communicated by MiLEAP; typically in the spring for the upcoming year).


#### Tuition Incentive Program (TIP)

Type: Need-based, [Medicaid-linked grant](https://www.michigan.gov/mistudentaid/programs/tuition-incentive-program) with two phases.

Award Amount:

- Phase I: Covers [standard in-district community college tuition](https://www.cmich.edu/offices-departments/office-scholarships-financial-aid/loans/state-of-michigan-tuition-incentive-plan-(tip)) or lower-level resident tuition at participating public universities, up to about 80 semester credits.
- Phase II: Up to [$500 per semester](https://www.michigan.gov/-/media/Project/Websites/mistudentaid/Publications/Fact-Sheets/TIPFactSheet.pdf?rev=a569ddcfe2d04a8da85b0b4c36bd234f) (or $400 per term), maximum $2,000 total for upper‑division coursework.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Identified by Michigan Department of Health and Human Services as having qualifying [Medicaid coverage for 24 months](https://www.cmich.edu/offices-departments/office-scholarships-financial-aid/loans/state-of-michigan-tuition-incentive-plan-(tip)) within a 36‑month window between ages 9 and high school completion.
- Michigan resident; U.S. citizen/permanent resident/refugee; [high school completion](https://www.michigan.gov/-/media/Project/Websites/mistudentaid/Publications/Fact-Sheets/TIPFactSheet.pdf?rev=a569ddcfe2d04a8da85b0b4c36bd234f) (or equivalent) before age 20 (21 for certain early/middle college students).
- Begin using TIP [within 4 years](https://www.michigan.gov/mistudentaid/hs-counselors/mi-data-sharing-center/tuition-incentive-program) of high school completion; enroll at least half‑time in an eligible certificate, associate, or bachelor’s program.
- File FAFSA and meet satisfactory academic progress.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Phase I: Michigan community colleges and [select public universities](https://www.michigan.gov/mistudentaid/programs/tuition-incentive-program) offering qualifying lower-division programs.
- Phase II: Participating [four-year institutions](https://www.michigan.gov/mistudentaid/hs-counselors/mi-data-sharing-center/tuition-incentive-program) for bachelor’s‑level coursework.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Phase I and Phase II each have [separate credit and time limits](https://www.michigan.gov/-/media/Project/Websites/mistudentaid/Publications/Fact-Sheets/TIPFactSheet.pdf?rev=a569ddcfe2d04a8da85b0b4c36bd234f); awards targeted to Medicaid-exposed students only.

Application Process:

- FAFSA plus any [TIP acceptance/activation steps](https://www.michigan.gov/mistudentaid/programs/tuition-incentive-program) via MiSSG portal; students notified of potential TIP eligibility by the state.

Deadline:

- FAFSA must be filed each year; program must be [activated within 4 years](https://www.michigan.gov/-/media/Project/Websites/mistudentaid/Publications/Fact-Sheets/TIPFactSheet.pdf?rev=a569ddcfe2d04a8da85b0b4c36bd234f) of high school completion and Phase II used within 30 months of finishing Phase I.


#### Michigan Competitive Scholarship (MCS)

Type: [Hybrid merit/need scholarship](https://www.michigan.gov/mistudentaid/programs/michigan-competitive-scholarship).

Award Amount:

- Current appropriation sets awards around [$1,500 per year](https://www.house.mi.gov/hfa/PDF/Alpha/Fiscal_Brief_Michigan_Competitive_Scholarship_Mar2025.pdf), subject to budget and number of recipients.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Michigan resident (and parents, if dependent) since at least July 1 of prior year; U.S. citizen or eligible noncitizen.
- High school diploma/equivalent earned [before 2023](https://scholarshipguidance.com/scholarships/27724/michigan-competitive-scholarship); qualifying [SAT score ≥ 1200](https://www.michigan.gov/-/media/Project/Websites/mistudentaid/Publications/Fact-Sheets/MCSFactSheet.pdf?rev=2d25182d2f9948f38f6febba2bed7f92) (or ACT ≥ 23 for pre‑2017 classes).
- Demonstrated financial need; enrolled at least half‑time in an eligible Michigan institution; no prior bachelor’s degree.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [Participating Michigan public and private institutions](https://www.michigan.gov/mistudentaid/programs/michigan-competitive-scholarship) listed by MI Student Aid.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Renewable up to roughly [10 semesters or 10 years](https://studentscholarships.org/scholarship/10056/michigan-competitive-scholarship) post‑high‑school, whichever comes first, with minimum 2.0 GPA and SAP.

Application Process:

- [File FAFSA](https://www.michigan.gov/-/media/Project/Websites/mistudentaid/Publications/Fact-Sheets/MCSFactSheet.pdf?rev=2d25182d2f9948f38f6febba2bed7f92) by the annual state MCS deadline; school choice managed via MiSSG portal.

Deadline:

- FAFSA receipt by federal processor by the published MCS deadline (varies by year; often [around March–July](https://studentscholarships.org/scholarship/10056/michigan-competitive-scholarship)).


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs (Michigan)

- [Futures for Frontliners](https://www.ncmich.edu/admissions/futures-for-frontliners.html) (now closed to new applicants) provided tuition-free community college to qualifying essential workers; it has [mostly sunset](https://bridgemi.com/talent-education/michigan-is-wrapping-up-a-covid-era-scholarship-for-essential-workers-heres-how-it-went/) but remains relevant for continuing recipients.
- [Michigan Reconnect](https://www.michigan.gov/mileap/higher-education/sixty-by-30/initiatives) (adult-focused tuition-free community college) operates as a separate [last-dollar program](https://www.ncmich.edu/admissions/futures-for-frontliners.html) for residents without degrees.


### Key Insights for Michigan

- [Michigan Achievement plus TIP](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/michigan/) create strong coverage for low-income and Medicaid-linked students, especially at community colleges.
- Merit/need hybrids like MCS favor students with strong test scores; those graduating [2023 or later](https://www.michigan.gov/mistudentaid/programs/michigan-achievement-scholarship/college-and-university) rely more on Michigan Achievement than MCS.
- [Medicaid history is crucial](https://www.michigan.gov/mistudentaid/programs/tuition-incentive-program)—students with long-term Medicaid exposure should check TIP eligibility early in high school.
- Adult and nontraditional students have separate pathways ([Reconnect](https://www.michigan.gov/mileap/higher-education/sixty-by-30/initiatives), legacy Frontliners), making the state relatively favorable across age groups.

## Minnesota

### Overview

- [Strong need-based system](https://onestop.umn.edu/finances/types-financial-aid/grants-and-waivers/minnesota-state-grant) centered on the [Minnesota State Grant](https://www.house.mn.gov/hrd/pubs/ss/ssmsgp.pdf) plus new [last-dollar tuition guarantee](https://system.umn.edu/state-minnesota-programs) (North Star Promise).
- Multiple programs aim to make [public and tribal colleges](https://www.normandale.edu/admissions/paying-for-college/financial-aid/north-star-promise.html) effectively [tuition-free](https://www.metrostate.edu/NorthStar) for low- and moderate-income residents, with [special support](https://system.umn.edu/state-minnesota-programs) for American Indian students.


### Major State Aid Programs

#### Minnesota State Grant

Type: [Need-based grant](https://onestop.umn.edu/finances/types-financial-aid/grants-and-waivers/minnesota-state-grant) (core state program).

Award Amount:

- Annual awards range from about [$100 up to roughly $17,700](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/quick-takes/2025/07/24/minnesota-alters-financial-aid-program-formula) depending on income, family size, and institution type, with [typical awards](https://mlpp.pressbooks.pub/introtouspublicpolicy/back-matter/appendix-a-the-minnesota-state-grant-program/) in the low‑thousands.

Eligibility Requirements:

- [Minnesota resident](https://www.house.mn.gov/hrd/pubs/ss/ssmsgp.pdf); generally a Minnesota high school graduate or equivalent, at least 17 years old.
- Pursuing first undergraduate certificate or degree; [no prior bachelor’s degree](https://onestop.umn.edu/finances/types-financial-aid/grants-and-waivers/minnesota-state-grant).
- Demonstrated financial need via FAFSA/MN Dream Act; [shared-responsibility formula](https://ohe.mn.gov/sites/default/files/2025-09/2025-2026%20MN%20State%20Grant%20Manual_updated%20092425_ADA.pdf) factors in family contribution, Pell, and student share.
- Enrolled at an [eligible Minnesota institution](https://www.house.mn.gov/hrd/pubs/ss/ssmsgp.pdf) (public or private) at least [half‑time](https://onestop.umn.edu/finances/types-financial-aid/grants-and-waivers/minnesota-state-grant); must meet SAP and loan/default requirements.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [Minnesota public universities](https://onestop.umn.edu/finances/types-financial-aid/grants-and-waivers/minnesota-state-grant) and state colleges, private non‑profit colleges, and some other eligible institutions.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Normally [last dollar after Pell](https://mlpp.pressbooks.pub/introtouspublicpolicy/back-matter/appendix-a-the-minnesota-state-grant-program/) and expected family contribution, except for some institutional aid categories.
- [Minimum annual award around $100](https://ohe.mn.gov/sites/default/files/2025-09/2025-2026%20MN%20State%20Grant%20Manual_updated%20092425_ADA.pdf); program parameters can be [reduced in deficit years](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/quick-takes/2025/07/24/minnesota-alters-financial-aid-program-formula).

Application Process:

- Submit [FAFSA or MN Dream Act Application](https://www.house.mn.gov/hrd/pubs/ss/ssmsgp.pdf); no separate state grant form.

Deadline:

- For 2025–26, FAFSA/MN Dream Act generally must be submitted [within 30 days of the start of the term](https://mankato.mnsu.edu/university-life/campus-services/campushub/financial-aid/grants/minnesota-state-grant/) (e.g., Fall 2025 deadline around late September).


#### North Star Promise Scholarship

Type: [Last-dollar tuition and fee program](https://ohe.mn.gov/sites/default/files/Documents/North%20Star%20Promise/NSP_FactSheet_FINAL_Print_ADA.pdf) for public/tribal institutions.

Award Amount:

- [Covers remaining eligible tuition and mandatory fees](https://www.metrostate.edu/NorthStar) after Pell, State Grant, and other scholarships/waivers at participating institutions.

Eligibility Requirements:

- [Minnesota resident](https://system.umn.edu/state-minnesota-programs).
- Family [Adjusted Gross Income below $80,000](https://ohe.mn.gov/sites/default/files/Documents/North%20Star%20Promise/NSP_FactSheet_FINAL_Print_ADA.pdf) as reported on FAFSA or MN Dream Act.
- Enrolled in [at least 1 credit](https://www.normandale.edu/admissions/paying-for-college/financial-aid/north-star-promise.html) in an aid‑eligible undergraduate program at a Minnesota public college/university or tribal college; no prior bachelor’s degree.
- Not in default on loans; must meet SAP.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [All Minnesota State Colleges and Universities](https://www.northlandcollege.edu/about/offices/financial-aid/north-star-promise-scholarship/), all University of Minnesota campuses, and all [tribal colleges](https://system.umn.edu/state-minnesota-programs) in Minnesota.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Last-dollar structure means [living expenses](https://www.normandale.edu/admissions/paying-for-college/financial-aid/north-star-promise.html) and some fees are not fully covered.
- Award sizes and availability can be [limited by appropriations](https://system.umn.edu/state-minnesota-programs); FAFSA timing matters.

Application Process:

- [File FAFSA or MN Dream Act](https://www.northlandcollege.edu/about/offices/financial-aid/north-star-promise-scholarship/); no separate application—institutions automatically identify eligible students.

Deadline:

- Example: University of Minnesota requires [FAFSA on file by June 1, 2026](https://system.umn.edu/state-minnesota-programs) for 2026–27 consideration; other institutions may use similar term-based deadlines.


#### American Indian Scholars Program

Type: [First-dollar tuition and fee waiver](https://system.umn.edu/state-minnesota-programs) for eligible American Indian students.

Award Amount:

- [Covers tuition and required fees](https://system.umn.edu/state-minnesota-programs) at Minnesota State institutions and the University of Minnesota for eligible students.

Eligibility Requirements (high-level):

- [Minnesota resident](https://system.umn.edu/state-minnesota-programs) eligible for resident tuition.
- Enrolled member/citizen of a [federally recognized tribe](https://system.umn.edu/state-minnesota-programs) or Canadian First Nation.
- Enrolled in an undergraduate program; typically no prior bachelor’s degree; must meet SAP.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [Minnesota State system](https://system.umn.edu/state-minnesota-programs) and University of Minnesota campuses (undergraduate level).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Designed as a [first-dollar commitment](https://system.umn.edu/state-minnesota-programs); other grants and scholarships may cover remaining costs beyond tuition/fees.

Application Process & Deadline:

- [File FAFSA or MN Dream Act](https://system.umn.edu/state-minnesota-programs) and provide documentation of tribal citizenship; deadlines align with institutional financial aid calendars.


### Key Insights for Minnesota

- Combination of State Grant plus [North Star Promise](https://ohe.mn.gov/sites/default/files/Documents/North%20Star%20Promise/NSP_FactSheet_FINAL_Print_ADA.pdf) makes Minnesota particularly strong for residents with [family AGI under $80,000](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/quick-takes/2025/07/24/minnesota-alters-financial-aid-program-formula) at public or tribal colleges.
- [FAFSA/Dream Act timing is critical](https://mankato.mnsu.edu/university-life/campus-services/campushub/financial-aid/grants/minnesota-state-grant/) because State Grant uses hard term deadlines and North Star Promise uses yearly cutoffs.
- Private college students get [strong State Grant support](https://mlpp.pressbooks.pub/introtouspublicpolicy/back-matter/appendix-a-the-minnesota-state-grant-program/) but do not benefit from North Star Promise; public/tribal enrollment yields the deepest tuition discounts.
- [American Indian students](https://system.umn.edu/state-minnesota-programs) can often achieve near‑full tuition coverage through the dedicated Scholars Program layered with other aid.

## Mississippi

### Overview

- Moderate aid state with three main grants: one very generous [full-tuition need-based program (HELP)](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/help/) and two broad programs for moderate-need and high-merit students ([MTAG](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/mtag/), [MESG](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/mesg/)).
- Funding is structured so [students generally receive only one grant at a time](https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2022-04/Mississippi%20State%20Grant%20Aid.pdf), with HELP taking precedence for eligible low-income students.


### Major State Aid Programs

#### Mississippi Resident Tuition Assistance Grant (MTAG)

Type: Need-based grant for students who are not already receiving maximum Pell (aimed at [“middle” need](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/SFA_GrantFlyer_2526.pdf)).

Award Amount:

- Up to [$500 per year](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/mtag/) for freshmen and sophomores.
- Up to [$1,000 per year](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/SFA_GrantFlyer_2526.pdf) for juniors and seniors.
- Maximum [8 semesters](https://www.mississippi.gov/Agencies/office-student-financial-aid) (or equivalent).

Eligibility Requirements:

- [Mississippi resident](https://www.mississippi.gov/Agencies/office-student-financial-aid) for at least 1 year before application.
- High school [GPA ≥ 2.5 and ACT ≥ 15](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/mtag/) (superscore accepted) or equivalent credentials for GED/home school.
- Enrolled [full-time](https://www.mississippi.gov/Agencies/office-student-financial-aid) in first certificate, associate, or bachelor’s program at an approved Mississippi institution.
- Eligible for [less than the maximum Federal Pell Grant](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/mtag/); FAFSA required.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Approved [public and private non-profit colleges](https://www.mississippi.gov/Agencies/office-student-financial-aid) and universities in Mississippi.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Students [cannot receive MTAG in the same term as HELP or MESG](https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2022-04/Mississippi%20State%20Grant%20Aid.pdf); they are placed in the program that yields the highest grant (HELP first, then MESG, then MTAG).

Application Process:

- Complete [online state aid application](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/sfa/awards-timeline/) plus FAFSA.

Deadline:

- Application opens October 1; [MTAG application deadline is September 15](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/sfa/awards-timeline/); supporting documents due October 15.


#### Mississippi Eminent Scholars Grant (MESG)

Type: Merit-based scholarship with [some need overlay](https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2022-04/Mississippi%20State%20Grant%20Aid.pdf).

Award Amount:

- Up to [$2,500 per academic year](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/mesg/) (cannot exceed tuition and fees).

Eligibility Requirements:

- [Mississippi resident](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/mesg/) for at least 1 year prior to high school completion.
- High school [GPA ≥ 3.5 and ACT ≥ 29](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/mesg/) (or SAT ≈1350) or National Merit Finalist/Semifinalist.
- Full-time enrollment in first certificate, associate, or bachelor’s program at an approved MS institution.
- College students can [qualify within 3 years of high school](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/mesg/) if they meet GPA and ACT and have 12–18 college credits with GPA ≥3.5.

Where It Can Be Used:

- State-approved [public and private not‑for‑profit](https://www.mississippi.gov/Agencies/office-student-financial-aid) two- and four‑year colleges in Mississippi.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Cannot stack with HELP or MTAG; [HELP supersedes MESG](https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2022-04/Mississippi%20State%20Grant%20Aid.pdf) when both criteria are met.

Application Process & Deadline:

- Same [online application portal](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/sfa/awards-timeline/) and FAFSA; MESG application window runs October 1–September 15, with supporting documents due October 15.


#### Higher Education Legislative Plan for Needy Students (HELP)

Type: Need-based, full-tuition grant for low-income students completing a [specific college-prep curriculum](https://holmescc.edu/financial-aid/state-grants/).

Award Amount:

- Covers [full tuition](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/help/) (up to four years) at approved in-state public and select private institutions, after other gift aid.

Eligibility Requirements:

- [Mississippi resident](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/help/) for at least 1 year prior to application.
- High school [GPA ≥ 2.5 and ACT ≥ 20](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/SFA_GrantFlyer_2526.pdf) (superscore accepted).
- Completion of the [HELP Core Curriculum](https://holmescc.edu/financial-aid/state-grants/), a more rigorous high school course sequence.
- Meet income limitations and demonstrate financial need; [must qualify for at least a partial Pell Grant](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/help/) via FAFSA.
- Enroll [full-time](https://www.mississippi.gov/Agencies/office-student-financial-aid) in first certificate, associate, or bachelor’s program at an approved MS institution.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Approved [Mississippi public and private](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/help/) institutions.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Most generous state grant; [overrides MTAG/MESG](https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2022-04/Mississippi%20State%20Grant%20Aid.pdf) when a student qualifies for multiple programs.
- Limited to [four academic years](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/help/); must maintain full-time enrollment and SAP.

Application Process:

- [Online HELP application](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/sfa/awards-timeline/) plus FAFSA and required curriculum/income documentation.

Deadline:

- [HELP application deadline March 31](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/SFA_GrantFlyer_2526.pdf); supporting documents due April 30 for the upcoming year.


### Key Insights for Mississippi

- Very strong for low-income students who complete the [HELP curriculum](https://holmescc.edu/financial-aid/state-grants/) and meet ACT thresholds (full tuition), but weaker for those who miss those criteria.
- [Moderate aid](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/mtag/) helps middle‑income and high‑achieving students but at [lower dollar amounts](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/mesg/) than HELP.
- Students can [only draw one state grant at a time](https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2022-04/Mississippi%20State%20Grant%20Aid.pdf); planning to qualify for HELP is a high‑impact goal for low‑income students.
- [Deadlines are early and strict](https://www.msfinancialaid.org/sfa/awards-timeline/); missing March 31 (HELP) or September 15 (MTAG/MESG) can eliminate state aid for the year.

## Missouri

### Overview

- [Moderate aid state](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf) with one primary need-based grant (Access Missouri) plus merit (Bright Flight) and “promise-type” A+ Scholarship, and an adult-focused [Fast Track grant](https://dhewd.mo.gov/ppc/grants-scholarships/access-mo).
- Awards are meaningful but not typically tuition‑free, so students often rely on [federal aid and institutional scholarships](https://sfs.mst.edu/financialaid/typesofaid/grants/accessmissourigrant/) as well.


### Major State Aid Programs

#### Access Missouri Financial Assistance Program

Type: [Need-based grant](https://dhewd.mo.gov/ppc/grants-scholarships/access-mo) (primary state program).

Award Amount (2025–26 estimates):

- Public 2‑year: [$500–$1,700 per year](https://dhewd.mo.gov/ppc/grants-scholarships/access-mo).
- Public 4‑year, State Technical College, private, and approved virtual institutions: [$1,750–$3,500 per year](https://dhewd.mo.gov/ppc/grants-scholarships/access-mo).

Eligibility Requirements:

- [Missouri resident](https://dhewd.mo.gov/ppc/grants-scholarships/access-mo) and U.S. citizen or permanent resident.
- Full-time undergraduate at an approved Missouri institution; not pursuing theology/divinity degrees; no prior bachelor’s or [≥150 credit hours](https://accessscholarships.com/scholarship/access-missouri-financial-assistance-program/).
- [Student Aid Index (SAI) ≤ 12,000](https://sfs.mst.edu/financialaid/typesofaid/grants/accessmissourigrant/) (or similar threshold) based on FAFSA.
- [2.5 minimum GPA](https://accessscholarships.com/scholarship/access-missouri-financial-assistance-program/) for renewal; must meet SAP and not be in loan default.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [Missouri public 2‑year and 4‑year institutions](https://dhewd.mo.gov/ppc/grants-scholarships/access-mo), State Technical College, and eligible private/virtual schools.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Award amounts [finalized in August](https://dhewd.mo.gov/ppc/grants-scholarships/access-mo) after funding and number of eligible students are known; amounts may be prorated.

Application Process:

- [File FAFSA](https://dhewd.mo.gov/news/mdhewd-encourages-missourians-file-fafsa-access-missouri-grant-priority-deadline); no separate application—eligibility determined from FAFSA data.

Deadline:

- [Priority FAFSA deadline](https://dhewd.mo.gov/news/mdhewd-encourages-missourians-file-fafsa-access-missouri-grant-priority-deadline) generally early February (e.g., [February 1 or 2](https://sfs.mst.edu/financialaid/typesofaid/grants/accessmissourigrant/) for Access Missouri).


#### Bright Flight (Missouri Higher Education Academic Scholarship)

Type: [Merit-based scholarship](https://dhewd.mo.gov/ppc/grants-scholarships/bright-flight) for top test scorers.

Award Amount:

- Legislatively determined each year; provides a [significant but partial tuition offset](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf) for top ACT/SAT scorers.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Missouri resident and [U.S. citizen or permanent resident](https://dhewd.mo.gov/ppc/grants-scholarships/bright-flight).
- Composite ACT/SAT score in the [top 5% of Missouri test takers](https://dhewd.mo.gov/ppc/grants-scholarships/bright-flight) (with limited awards for top 3rd–5th percentiles depending on funding).
- Enrolled [full-time](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf) at an approved Missouri postsecondary school; continuous enrollment required for renewal.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [Approved Missouri public and private institutions](https://dhewd.mo.gov/ppc/grants-scholarships/bright-flight).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Intended to keep [high-achieving students in-state](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf); award and cutoff scores may change with funding.


#### A+ Scholarship Program

Type: [Merit/“promise-type” program](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf) tied to high school performance for use at mostly 2‑year institutions.

Award Amount:

- Promise‑type funding that can [cover standard tuition](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf) at participating community colleges and some vocational/technical schools, subject to appropriation.

Eligibility Requirements (high-level):

- Graduate from an [A+ designated high school](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf) meeting GPA, attendance, conduct, and tutoring/service requirements.
- Enroll at a participating [Missouri community college](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf) or vocational/technical school; must be a Missouri resident and meet SAP.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [Participating community colleges](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf) and technical schools in Missouri.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- [Award amounts can be adjusted](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf) when state funding is constrained.


#### Fast Track Workforce Incentive Grant

Type: [Need-based, workforce-focused grant](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf) for adults.

Award Amount:

- Need-based funding for [adults entering high‑demand fields](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf); amount varies by tuition and other aid.

Eligibility Requirements (high-level):

- [Missouri resident adult](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf) pursuing programs in high‑demand occupations at approved institutions; income and age requirements apply.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [Approved Missouri institutions](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf) and eligible high‑demand programs.


### Key Insights for Missouri

- [Access Missouri is essential](https://dhewd.mo.gov/ppc/grants-scholarships/access-mo) for low-income full-time students; [missing the February FAFSA priority deadline](https://dhewd.mo.gov/news/mdhewd-encourages-missourians-file-fafsa-access-missouri-grant-priority-deadline) significantly reduces chances of funding.
- Merit‑strong students should [aim for Bright Flight and A+](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf) to stack with federal and institutional aid, especially at in‑state schools.
- Aid rarely makes four-year college tuition‑free; Missouri is [more “moderate help”](https://accessscholarships.com/scholarship/access-missouri-financial-assistance-program/) than “full ride” for most students.
- Adult learners in [high‑demand programs](https://sheeo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/MDHEWD-Financial-Aid.pdf) can access Fast Track funding, but must pay close attention to program-specific requirements.

## Montana

### Overview

- [Moderate aid state](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0200/chapter_0260/part_0060/section_0230/0200-0260-0060-0230.html) with strong [tuition waivers](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/MUS_Honor_Scholarship.html) for top students and a last-dollar [Montana Promise Grant](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Montana%20Promise%20-%20Eligibility%20and%20Procedures%20-%20FINAL%208.19.25.pdf) for 2‑year/tribal colleges.
- Generosity is targeted: high‑achieving or low‑income 2‑year students benefit most; overall [funding caps](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0200/chapter_0260/part_0060/section_0230/0200-0260-0060-0230.html) limit total recipients.


### Major State Aid Programs

#### MUS Honor Scholarship

Type: [Merit-based](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/MUSHS-Fact-Sheet%202024.pdf), four-year [tuition waiver](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/MUS_Honor_Scholarship.html) at Montana University System campuses.

Award Amount:

- Waives undergraduate tuition for up to 4 consecutive years; average value around [$5,000 per year](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/MUS_Honor_Scholarship.html) at a four‑year campus (tuition only).

Eligibility Requirements:

- Montana resident; [2025 high school graduate](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/MUSHS-Fact-Sheet%202024.pdf) from a school accredited by the Montana Board of Public Education.
- Enrolled [full-time](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/MUS_Honor_Scholarship.html) at an eligible MUS campus; GPA and ACT/SAT score thresholds (e.g., [cumulative GPA ≥3.4](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/MUSHS-Fact-Sheet%202024.pdf) and specified rigorous core curriculum).
- Must apply in the year of graduation and meet residency and academic criteria set by Board of Regents.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Eligible [4‑year units](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/MUSHS-Fact-Sheet%202024.pdf) of the Montana University System.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- [Does not cover mandatory or class fees](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/MUS_Honor_Scholarship.html); students must budget for fees and living expenses.
- Limited number of scholarships (up to about [200 annually](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/MUSHS-Fact-Sheet%202024.pdf)), contingent on funding; meeting criteria does not guarantee selection.

Application Process:

- Centralized [MUS Honor Scholarship application](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/MUS_Honor_Scholarship.html); open December 1 with deadlines in March.

Deadline:

- Applications typically due [mid‑March](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/MUSHS-Fact-Sheet%202024.pdf) for the upcoming academic year.


#### Montana Promise Grant

Type: Need-based, [last-dollar grant](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Montana%20Promise%20-%20Eligibility%20and%20Procedures%20-%20FINAL%208.19.25.pdf) for community, tribal, and 2‑year institutions.

Award Amount:

- Per term grant equals the greater of [$75 per enrolled credit](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0200/chapter_0260/part_0060/section_0230/0200-0260-0060-0230.html) or the remaining tuition after other federal, state, and private grants/waivers.
- Total statewide [funding capped](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0200/chapter_0260/part_0060/section_0230/0200-0260-0060-0230.html) at about $2 million per fiscal year.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Montana resident for [at least 12 months](https://believeinme.news/montana-promise-act/) before applying.
- Enrolled at least [half-time](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Montana%20Promise%20-%20Eligibility%20and%20Procedures%20-%20FINAL%208.19.25.pdf) at a Montana community college, tribal college, or 2‑year unit of the Montana University System.
- High school diploma or equivalency; [minimum GPA of about 2.5](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0200/chapter_0260/part_0060/section_0230/0200-0260-0060-0230.html) or equivalent academic ability.
- Must [file FAFSA](https://believeinme.news/montana-promise-act/), accept all available federal and state grants, and not have more than 60 college credits or an associate degree.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Montana [community colleges, tribal colleges](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Montana%20Promise%20-%20Eligibility%20and%20Procedures%20-%20FINAL%208.19.25.pdf), and 2‑year MUS institutions.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Funding is first [limited by the statewide cap](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0200/chapter_0260/part_0060/section_0230/0200-0260-0060-0230.html); Board of Regents may adopt prioritization if funds are insufficient.
- Intended as a [last-dollar tuition grant](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0200/chapter_0260/part_0060/section_0230/0200-0260-0060-0230.html); does not typically cover fees and living costs.

Application Process & Deadline:

- FAFSA plus any [institution-level Promise Grant process](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Montana%20Promise%20-%20Eligibility%20and%20Procedures%20-%20FINAL%208.19.25.pdf); awards made term by term according to Board of Regents policies.


#### 2 Plus 2 Honor Scholarship

Type: Merit-based [tuition waiver](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/2_Plus_2_Honor_Scholarship.html) for associate-degree graduates transferring to a 4‑year campus.

Award Amount:

- Waiver of undergraduate tuition for [up to 4 continuous](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/2_Plus_2_Honor_Scholarship.html) fall/spring semesters at any 4‑year MUS unit.

Eligibility Requirements:

- [Montana resident](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/2_Plus_2_Honor_Scholarship.html).
- Graduate with an associate degree from an eligible 2‑year campus with [cumulative GPA ≥3.4](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/2_Plus_2_Honor_Scholarship.html); must have been enrolled for at least two semesters prior to graduation.
- Accepted to or enrolled at an eligible [4‑year MUS campus](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/2_Plus_2_Honor_Scholarship.html); must begin using scholarship the next regular semester after earning the associate degree.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [4‑year units](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/2_Plus_2_Honor_Scholarship.html) of the Montana University System.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- [Cannot be used with any other MUS tuition waiver](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/2_Plus_2_Honor_Scholarship.html) and does not cover mandatory fees.

Application Process & Deadline:

- [Apply in June](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/2_Plus_2_Honor_Scholarship.html) following associate-degree completion; selection among eligible applicants.


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs (Montana)

- Campus-specific [“promise” or tuition-guarantee programs](https://www.umt.edu/finaid/scholarships/grizzly-promise/) (e.g., [Grizzly Promise](https://www.umt.edu/finaid/scholarships/grizzly-promise/) at the University of Montana) cover tuition for in-state students from families with incomes around $50,000 or less.


### Key Insights for Montana

- Very good for [strong academic performers](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/MUS_Honor_Scholarship.html) and for low-income students at [2‑year and tribal colleges](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Montana%20Promise%20-%20Eligibility%20and%20Procedures%20-%20FINAL%208.19.25.pdf).
- [Funding caps](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0200/chapter_0260/part_0060/section_0230/0200-0260-0060-0230.html) mean eligible students may not always receive Montana Promise if appropriations are tight.
- [Tuition waivers](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/2_Plus_2_Honor_Scholarship.html) generally do not cover fees or living expenses.
- [Timing matters](https://www.mus.edu/Prepare/Pay/Scholarships/MUSHS-Fact-Sheet%202024.pdf): high school and 2‑year students should track specific scholarship windows (March and June deadlines).

## Nebraska

### Overview

- Moderate aid state with one main need-based grant ([Nebraska Opportunity Grant](https://ccpe.nebraska.gov/nebraska-opportunity-grant-nog)) and a strong [tuition guarantee](https://huskerhub.unl.edu/scholarships-financial-aid/scholarships-grants/nebraska-promise/) (Nebraska Promise) for low-income students within the University of Nebraska system.
- Effectiveness is [highest for residents](https://ccpe.nebraska.gov/nebraska-opportunity-grant-nog) with lower incomes attending in-state public institutions.


### Major State Aid Programs

#### Nebraska Opportunity Grant (NOG)

Type: [Need-based grant](https://www.scholarships.com/scholarships/nebraska-opportunity-grant).

Award Amount:

- [Varies by institution](https://ccpe.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/FS_NOG.pdf) and financial need; no single statewide dollar figure, but provides meaningful grant support for high-need students.

Eligibility Requirements:

- [Nebraska resident](https://ccpe.nebraska.gov/nebraska-opportunity-grant-nog) with [significant financial need](https://ccpe.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/FS_NOG.pdf) as measured by Student Aid Index (SAI) via FAFSA; must not have earned a bachelor’s, graduate, or professional degree.
- Enrolled at an [eligible Nebraska postsecondary institution](https://ccpe.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/FS_NOG.pdf) in a degree/credential program.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Nebraska [public and private colleges](https://www.scholarships.com/scholarships/nebraska-opportunity-grant) and universities that participate in NOG.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Awards are made by [institutions out of their NOG allocations](https://ccpe.nebraska.gov/nebraska-opportunity-grant-nog); not all eligible students may receive funding if allocations are exhausted.

Application Process:

- [File FAFSA](https://www.scholarships.com/scholarships/nebraska-opportunity-grant); institutions use FAFSA data to award NOG—often automatically to the highest-need students.

Deadline:

- [Institutional priority FAFSA deadlines](https://ccpe.nebraska.gov/nebraska-opportunity-grant-nog) apply; earlier filing increases likelihood due to limited campus allocations.


#### Nebraska Promise

Type: [Tuition guarantee / last-dollar program](https://huskerhub.unl.edu/scholarships-financial-aid/scholarships-grants/nebraska-promise/) within the University of Nebraska system.

Award Amount:

- Guarantees [coverage of undergraduate tuition](https://huskerhub.unl.edu/scholarships-financial-aid/scholarships-grants/nebraska-promise/) (up to 15 credit hours per semester, 30 per year) at University of Nebraska campuses for qualifying students.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Nebraska resident undergraduate pursuing [first bachelor’s degree](https://huskerhub.unl.edu/scholarships-financial-aid/scholarships-grants/nebraska-promise/) at a University of Nebraska campus (UNK, UNL, UNMC, UNO, or NCTA).
- [Family income ≤ $65,000](https://huskerhub.unl.edu/scholarships-financial-aid/scholarships-grants/nebraska-promise/) and/or Pell Grant eligibility; must be full-time and maintain at least 2.5 GPA.
- Must [file FAFSA](https://huskerhub.unl.edu/scholarships-financial-aid/scholarships-grants/nebraska-promise/) by campus priority deadline each year.

Where It Can Be Used:

- University of Nebraska – [Lincoln, Omaha, Kearney, Medical Center](https://huskerhub.unl.edu/scholarships-financial-aid/scholarships-grants/nebraska-promise/) (undergrad), and Nebraska College of Technical Agriculture.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- **Last-dollar:** other grants and scholarships pay first; [Nebraska Promise fills remaining tuition only](https://huskerhub.unl.edu/scholarships-financial-aid/scholarships-grants/nebraska-promise/).
- [Does not cover fees](https://huskerhub.unl.edu/scholarships-financial-aid/scholarships-grants/nebraska-promise/), housing, or other costs.

Application Process & Deadline:

- Apply for admission to a University of Nebraska campus and [file FAFSA](https://huskerhub.unl.edu/scholarships-financial-aid/scholarships-grants/nebraska-promise/) by that campus’s priority deadline; students are automatically evaluated.


### Key Insights for Nebraska

- NOG extends need-based support across many institutions, but awards [depend heavily on campus funding pools](https://ccpe.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/FS_NOG.pdf).
- [Nebraska Promise](https://huskerhub.unl.edu/scholarships-financial-aid/scholarships-grants/nebraska-promise/) makes in‑state tuition effectively free for many low-income students at NU campuses; FAFSA timing and income documentation are critical.
- Students eyeing NU should prioritize full-time enrollment and [2.5+ GPA](https://huskerhub.unl.edu/scholarships-financial-aid/scholarships-grants/nebraska-promise/) to maintain Nebraska Promise in later years.
- Overall generosity is [solid for low‑income](https://ccpe.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/FS_NOG.pdf) public‑sector students, more modest for others.

## Nevada

### Overview

- Limited-to-moderate aid state focused on community colleges and low-income students via the **Nevada Promise Scholarship** and **Silver State Opportunity Grant (SSOG)**.
- **Governor Guinn Millennium Scholarship** serves as the primary merit-based award for high-achieving high school graduates attending any in-state public institution.
- Students targeting four‑year universities (UNR/UNLV) rely more on the Millennium Scholarship, as the Promise and SSOG programs are primarily geared toward community and state colleges.

---

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Nevada Promise Scholarship
**Type:** Last-dollar scholarship for community college students.

* **Award Amount:** Covers up to **three years** of tuition and mandatory fees at participating Nevada community colleges after other gift aid (Pell, etc.) is applied.
* **Eligibility Requirements:**
    * Nevada resident for tuition purposes.
    * High school diploma from Nevada or a bordering county school that accepts NV residents (earned in **2026** for the upcoming cohort).
    * Enroll in at least **12 credits** per semester in an associate, bachelor’s, or eligible certificate program at a Nevada community college.
    * **Milestones:** Must complete a multi-step process including a mentor meeting, **8 hours of community service**, and mandatory training.
* **Where It Can Be Used:** CSN, TMCC, GBC, and WNC.
* **Application Process & Deadline:**
    * Apply between **August 1 and October 31** of the senior year.
    * FAFSA/Non-Filer Declaration deadline: **April 1, 2026**.
    * Community Service deadline: **May 1, 2026**.
    * Mentor meeting/Financial Aid verification: **July 1, 2026**.

#### Governor Guinn Millennium Scholarship (GGMS)
**Type:** Merit-based scholarship for high school graduates.

* **Award Amount:** Up to a lifetime maximum of **$10,000**.
    * **$80 per credit** at UNR, UNLV, and other eligible 4-year institutions.
    * **$60 per credit** at Nevada State University.
    * **$40 per credit** at NSHE community colleges (lower division).
* **Eligibility Requirements:**
    * Graduate from a Nevada high school with at least a **3.25 GPA** (weighted or unweighted) OR a qualifying test score (**21 ACT** or **1070 SAT**).
    * Complete a specific core curriculum (4 English, 4 Math including Algebra II, 3 Science, 3 Social Science).
    * Must have been a Nevada resident for at least two years of high school.
* **Key Restrictions:** Does not pay for remedial courses (numbered below 100). Must complete at least **12 credits** (6 at community colleges) with a **2.75 semester GPA** to maintain eligibility.

#### Silver State Opportunity Grant (SSOG)
**Type:** Need-based grant for low-income, college-ready students.

* **Award Amount:** Up to **$5,500 per year**; calculated based on a shared-responsibility model that factors in the cost of attendance, family contribution, and federal aid.
* **Eligibility Requirements:**
    * Nevada resident enrolled in at least **12 credits** at an NSHE community college or Nevada State University.
    * Must be "college-ready" (placed into college-level Math and English).
    * Expected Family Contribution (EFC) or SAI must be under a specific threshold (historically **$8,500**).
* **Key Restrictions:** Funding is limited and awarded on a **first-come, first-served basis** based on FAFSA completion date.

---

### Key Insights for Nevada

* **Community College Advantage:** Combining the Nevada Promise with the Millennium Scholarship can result in a "refund" or credit for other expenses, as the Promise covers tuition gaps.
* **Strict Deadlines:** Nevada Promise is famously rigid; missing the **October 31** application or the **March 1** training deadline results in permanent loss of eligibility.
* **Merit vs. Need:** High-achievers should bank on the Millennium Scholarship ($10k cap), while low-income students should file the FAFSA as early as possible (October/November) to secure SSOG funds before they are exhausted.
* **University Costs:** While the Millennium Scholarship helps, it only covers a portion of tuition at UNR and UNLV (approximately **$2,400/year** for 30 credits), leaving a significant gap compared to the full-tuition coverage available at community colleges.

## New Hampshire

### Overview

- **Limited state aid:** New Hampshire consistently ranks as one of the lowest states for direct public investment in student financial aid.
- **Tuition-free pathways:** Affordability is primarily driven by institutional "Promise" programs that target Pell-eligible residents, particularly within the community college and state university systems.
- **High Debt Burden:** Due to sparse state funding, New Hampshire graduates often face some of the highest student loan debt averages in the country.

---

### Major State Aid Programs

#### UNIQUE Scholarship Programs
**Type:** Need-based grants funded by the state's UNIQUE endowment.

* **UNIQUE Annual Allocation (UAAP):** Provides roughly **$2,500 per year** to the neediest students.
* **UNIQUE Endowment Allocation (UEAP):** Awards vary, typically ranging from **$1,000 up to the full cost of education**.
* **Eligibility:**
    * New Hampshire resident and Pell Grant eligible.
    * Must attend a participating, accredited New Hampshire institution.
* **Application:** File the **FAFSA**. Funds are allocated directly to colleges, which then select recipients based on the highest financial need.

#### New Hampshire Governor's Scholarship
**Type:** Merit/Need-based hybrid.

* **Award Amount:**
    * **$2,000 per year:** For Pell-eligible students who graduated high school as a designated **"NH Scholar."**
    * **$1,000 per year:** For other Pell-eligible residents.
* **Eligibility:** Must be a recent high school graduate, a New Hampshire resident, and enrolled full-time at a participating in-state institution.
* **Key Note:** This is a four-year scholarship as long as the student maintains Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP).

#### CCSNH Promise Program
**Type:** Last-dollar gap funding for community college students.

* **Award Amount:** Covers the **remaining gap** for tuition and mandatory fees after all other grants (Pell, UNIQUE, etc.) have been applied.
* **Where It Can Be Used:** All seven campuses within the **Community College System of New Hampshire (CCSNH)**.
* **Eligibility:** Pell-eligible New Hampshire residents enrolled in at least **6 credits** (part-time or full-time).
* **Application:** No separate application; students are automatically considered upon filing the FAFSA and enrolling.

#### Granite Guarantee (UNH & Plymouth State)
**Type:** Last-dollar tuition guarantee for the University System of New Hampshire (USNH).

* **Award Amount:** Guarantees **$0 tuition** by bridging the gap between a student's total federal/state aid and the cost of in-state tuition.
* **Eligibility:**
    * First-time, first-year NH residents who are Pell-eligible.
    * Community college transfer students who are Pell-eligible and have an associate degree.
* **Key Restrictions:** Covers **tuition only**. Students are still responsible for mandatory fees, housing, and food. It is renewable for four years if the student remains Pell-eligible and maintains full-time status.

---

### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

* **New Hampshire Scholars:** High school students who complete a specific rigorous core curriculum (Advanced, STEM, or Arts) become eligible for increased Governor’s Scholarship amounts and application fee waivers at many NH colleges.
* **Scholarships for Orphans of Veterans:** Provides up to **$2,500** for children of New Hampshire residents who died while in service or as a result of service-connected disabilities.
* **NEBHE Tuition Break:** Allows NH residents to attend certain out-of-state New England public colleges at a discount if their desired major is not offered by a New Hampshire public institution.

---

### Key Insights for New Hampshire

* **The "Pell-Or-Nothing" Reality:** Almost all meaningful aid in NH—including the Granite Guarantee and CCSNH Promise—is strictly tied to **Pell Grant eligibility**. Middle-income students who do not qualify for Pell often receive very little state-level assistance.
* **Community College First:** Starting at a CCSNH school is the most reliable way to minimize debt, as the Promise program effectively makes the first two years of tuition free for high-need residents.
* **FAFSA Speed Matters:** Because programs like the UNIQUE scholarship are distributed via fixed pools of money to each college, filing the FAFSA early (ideally by **March 1**) is critical before the institutional funds run out.

## New Jersey

### Overview

- **Top-Tier Support:** New Jersey maintains one of the most robust state-funded aid systems in the nation, combining large-scale need-based grants with specific "promise" programs that create clear pathways to debt-free degrees.
- **2026-2027 Shift:** The state has tightened eligibility for its middle-income "Tier 2 and 3" awards. For 2026, many programs now prioritize students with an Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) of **$0–$65,000** for new awards, while continuing to support higher-income students who were previously enrolled.
- **State-Specific Application:** NJ uses its own financial aid portal (NJFAMS) to manage state aid. Even after filing the FAFSA, students must log in to NJFAMS to complete their "State Record."

---

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Tuition Aid Grant (TAG)
**Type:** Need-based grant (flagship program).

* **Award Amount (2026-2027):**
    * Full-time TAG: Ranges from **$1,280 to $14,404** depending on the type of institution (public vs. private) and student need.
    * Part-time TAG: Available for community college students, ranging from **$320 to $1,097**.
* **Eligibility:**
    * New Jersey resident for at least 12 consecutive months.
    * Seeking a first associate or bachelor’s degree at an approved NJ institution.
    * Must meet the New Jersey Eligibility Index (NJEI) threshold based on financial need.
* **Deadlines:**
    * **Renewal Students:** Must file the FAFSA/NJ Alternative Application by **April 15, 2026**.
    * **New Students:** **September 15, 2026** for the Fall/Spring terms; **February 15, 2027** for Spring-only aid.

#### Educational Opportunity Fund (EOF)
**Type:** Need-based grant + academic/counseling support for educationally and economically disadvantaged students.

* **Award Amount:** Up to **$3,050 per year**, plus intensive tutoring, advising, and a pre-college summer program.
* **Eligibility:**
    * Must demonstrate a history of poverty and a household income within the EOF scale (e.g., for a family of four in 2026-27, the gross income limit is approximately **$64,300**).
    * Must be a first-generation college student or from an "educationally disadvantaged" background.
* **Key Note:** EOF is campus-based; you must apply to the EOF office at your specific college in addition to filing the FAFSA.

#### NJ STARS and NJ STARS II
**Type:** Merit-based tuition programs for top-ranking students.

* **NJ STARS:** Covers **full tuition** at any NJ community college for students who rank in the **top 15%** of their high school class.
* **NJ STARS II:** Provides up to **$2,500 per year** for NJ STARS graduates who earn their associate degree with at least a **3.25 GPA** and transfer to an NJ four-year college.
* **Eligibility:** Must be a New Jersey resident and enroll full-time (at least 12 credits). For NJ STARS II, family income must be less than **$250,000**.

#### Garden State Guarantee (GSG)
**Type:** Last-dollar tuition guarantee for 3rd and 4th-year students at public 4-year institutions.

* **Award Amount:** Covers remaining tuition and mandatory fees after all other aid is applied.
* **2026 Eligibility:**
    * **Tier 1 ($0–$65k AGI):** Guarantees **$0 net price** for tuition and fees.
    * **Tiers 2 & 3 ($65k–$100k AGI):** New awards for this income bracket are restricted in 2026; typically only students who previously received GSG in these tiers will remain eligible.
* **Structure:** Specifically for students who have earned **60–128 credits**.

---

### Key Insights for New Jersey

* **Early Deadlines are Absolute:** Unlike the federal FAFSA, which has a rolling deadline, NJ's **April 15** renewal deadline is strict. Missing it can cost a student over $14,000 in TAG funding.
* **The "State Record" Requirement:** A common pitfall is filing the FAFSA but failing to check **NJFAMS**. The state often requires additional documentation (like tax transcripts or residency proof) that must be uploaded to their specific portal to trigger an award.
* **NJ Dreamers:** New Jersey allows undocumented students to apply for all state aid (TAG, EOF, STARS) through the **NJ Alternative Financial Aid Application**, provided they attended an NJ high school for three years and graduated.

## New Mexico

### Overview

New Mexico is one of the most generous states in the country, with broad “tuition-free” coverage at public colleges through the [Opportunity Scholarship](https://scholarships.unm.edu/Resources/opportunity-scholarship.html) and [Legislative Lottery Scholarships](https://scholarship.unm.edu/Resources/lottery-scholarships.html). Aid is heavily focused on in-state public institutions, with generous last‑dollar coverage and relatively modest academic requirements (2.5 GPA, 6–12+ credits). Need-based grants like the [College Affordability Grant](https://www.nmhu.edu/financial-aid/college-affordability-grant/) fill gaps for low-income students who miss other state programs.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### New Mexico Opportunity Scholarship

Type: [Last-dollar, tuition guarantee](https://www.governor.state.nm.us/opportunity-scholarship/) (need-sensitive but no income cap).

Award Amount:

- Covers up to [100% of tuition and course-specific mandatory fees](https://www.reachhighernm.com/returning-students/) at New Mexico public colleges and universities, generally up to 90 attempted credits for an associate and 160 for a bachelor’s.

Eligibility Requirements:

- New Mexico resident.
- Enrolled in a [credit-bearing certificate, associate, or bachelor’s program](https://global.nmsu.edu/blog/general/new-mexico-opportunity-scholarship-frequently-asked-questions/) at a New Mexico public college or university.
- At least 6 credits per term and generally not more than 18; [2.5 cumulative GPA](https://www.cnm.edu/depts/financial-aid/scholarships/new-mexico-opportunity-scholarship) to maintain eligibility.
- Available to both “recent graduates” (within ~16 months of high school completion) and [“returning learners”](https://scholarships.unm.edu/Resources/opportunity-scholarship.html) (adults re-entering or starting college later).

Where It Can Be Used:

- All [29 New Mexico public colleges and universities](https://hed.nm.gov/financial-aid), including community colleges and four-year institutions.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Covers [tuition and course-specific mandatory fees only](https://www.cnm.edu/depts/financial-aid/scholarships/new-mexico-opportunity-scholarship) (not housing, meals, etc.).
- Continues up to credit caps if student maintains 2.5 GPA, enrollment minimums, and other institutional Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP) standards.
- [No income limit and no separate application](https://losalamosreporter.com/2025/10/22/new-mexicos-opportunity-scholarship-receives-national-recognition-for-free-college-tuition-policy/) make it unusually inclusive compared to other states.

Application Process:

- No separate application; students are awarded automatically once the institution confirms eligibility.
- FAFSA is not required, but strongly encouraged for stacking federal aid.

Deadline:

- No fixed program deadline, but students should enroll as early as possible and [complete FAFSA early](https://hed.nm.gov/financial-aid) each year to maximize combined aid.


#### New Mexico Legislative Lottery Scholarship

Type: [Merit-leaning, last-dollar tuition scholarship](https://scholarship.unm.edu/Resources/lottery-scholarships.html) for recent high school graduates.

Award Amount:

- Covers up to 100% of [base tuition](https://www.cnm.edu/depts/financial-aid/scholarships/nm-legislative-lottery-scholarship) (exact percentage set by the legislature annually) at New Mexico public colleges and universities.

Eligibility Requirements:

- New Mexico resident who [graduated from a New Mexico high school](https://www.nmhu.edu/financial-aid/new-mexico-legislative-lottery-scholarship/) or earned an approved equivalency.
- Must enroll full time at a New Mexico public institution within about [15–16 months of high school graduation](https://essayhub.com/study/new-mexico-legislative-lottery-scholarship) or equivalency (qualifying term).
- Qualifying term: complete at least 12 credits at a two‑year college or 15 credits at a four‑year college with at least a 2.5 GPA.
- To retain: typically 2.5 GPA and 30 credits per academic year.

Where It Can Be Used:

- New Mexico [public community colleges and four‑year universities](https://www.collegeraptor.com/paying-for-college/articles/financial-aid/new-mexico-college-grants/).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Award starts in the [second semester](https://essayhub.com/study/new-mexico-legislative-lottery-scholarship) after the qualifying term.
- Generally available for up to 7–8 regular semesters (plus limited summers) of undergraduate study if continuous full-time enrollment and GPA requirements are met.
- [No separate application](https://web.ped.nm.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Lottery-Brochure-2021.pdf); entitlement-style if criteria are met.

Application Process:

- No separate scholarship application; institutions automatically determine eligibility based on enrollment and academic performance.
- FAFSA not required but encouraged.

Deadline:

- Functional deadline is enrolling full time within the 15–16 month post‑graduation window and completing the qualifying term successfully.


#### College Affordability Grant

Type: [Need-based grant](https://accessscholarships.com/scholarship/college-affordability-grant/) for students who do not qualify for other state grants/scholarships (beyond Pell).

Award Amount:

- Recent rule changes increased the [maximum allowable amount](https://www.nmlegis.gov/handouts/ALESC%20062619%20Item%2011%20A.3%20-%20PROPOSED%20RULE%20-%20NPRM%20College%20Affordability%20Grant%205.7.23.pdf) from roughly 1,000 to up to about 1,500 per semester; check your college for current caps.
- Total eligibility typically up to eight consecutive semesters, subject to funding and Pell eligibility.

Eligibility Requirements:

- New Mexico resident undergraduate at a public college or university (or tribal college) enrolled at least [half time (6+ credits)](https://www.nmhu.edu/financial-aid/college-affordability-grant/).
- Demonstrated financial need via FAFSA.
- Must not be receiving other state grants or scholarships (other than Pell).

Where It Can Be Used:

- New Mexico public colleges and universities, and eligible tribal colleges.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Designed specifically for needy students who are excluded from other state grant/scholarship programs.
- Funding is limited and awards are prorated for less than full‑time enrollment.

Application Process:

- Complete FAFSA and any institutional forms; awards administered by campus financial aid offices.

Deadline:

- Institutions commonly use priority FAFSA deadlines in early spring (e.g., March 1 to file FAFSA and mid‑April to complete the aid file).


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

- **New Mexico Student Incentive Grant** – [Need-based grant](https://www.nmt.edu/finaid/stateaid.php) for resident undergraduates with substantial financial need at New Mexico public or tribal colleges; awards commonly reported up to around 2,500 per year.
- Targeted programs like **Grow Your Own Teachers** provide additional aid for [paraeducators and school employees](https://www.collegeraptor.com/paying-for-college/articles/financial-aid/new-mexico-college-grants/) entering teacher preparation programs.


### Key Insights for New Mexico

- New Mexico is one of the strongest states for tuition coverage at public colleges due to the [combined Opportunity and Lottery Scholarships](https://www.governor.state.nm.us/opportunity-scholarship/).
- Many awards are automatic with no separate state application, but full‑time or half‑time enrollment and a 2.5 GPA are critical thresholds.
- The College Affordability Grant is important for low‑income students who slip through gaps in other state aid – [especially part‑time or nontraditional students](https://accessscholarships.com/scholarship/college-affordability-grant/).
- Funding caps and rules (e.g., credit hour limits) mean students should avoid unnecessary credits that could eat into scholarship eligibility.
- Applying early for FAFSA and coordinating with the financial aid office is key to [stacking Pell, state scholarships, and institutional aid](https://www.collegeraptor.com/paying-for-college/articles/financial-aid/new-mexico-college-grants/) efficiently.

## New York

### Overview

New York offers a relatively generous, complex aid system with a large need‑based grant ([TAP](https://hesc.ny.gov/find-aid/nys-grants-scholarships/tuition-assistance-program-tap)), “tuition‑free” SUNY/CUNY options ([Excelsior](https://www.excelsior.edu/costs-and-financing/financial-aid/new-york/)), and new promise‑style programs for adult learners. Aid skews toward in‑state study and has a mix of strict income caps and broader need-based formulas, with [multiple niche programs](https://hesc.ny.gov/about/news-releases/student-update-may-2025) layered on top.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Tuition Assistance Program (TAP)

Type: [Need-based grant](https://hesc.ny.gov/find-aid/nys-grants-scholarships/tuition-assistance-program-tap).

Award Amount:

- For recent years, awards range roughly from 1,000 to 5,665 per year depending on income, tuition, dependency status, and when you first received TAP.
- Many institutional examples cite [maximum awards around 5,165–5,665](https://www.excelsior.edu/costs-and-financing/financial-aid/new-york/) at approved New York institutions.

Eligibility Requirements:

- New York State resident studying at an [approved NYS postsecondary institution](https://hesc.ny.gov/find-aid/nys-grants-scholarships/tuition-assistance-program-tap).
- Matriculated in an approved program and in good academic standing, with at least a [C average by the fourth semester](https://www.excelsior.edu/costs-and-financing/financial-aid/new-york/) of payment.
- Full-time (typically 12+ degree‑applicable credits per term) for standard TAP; separate provisions exist for part-time TAP.
- Must meet NYS income limits; limits vary by dependency and family status.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Approved NYS public and private colleges and universities (SUNY, CUNY, and many privates).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- [Limited number of payments](https://hesc.ny.gov/find-aid/nys-grants-scholarships/tuition-assistance-program-tap) (generally up to 3 years for associate, 4 for bachelor’s, 5 for approved five‑year programs).
- Award amounts are sensitive to income and tuition; students at low‑tuition community colleges may receive lower dollar awards.

Application Process:

- Complete FAFSA, then the [NYS TAP application](https://hesc.ny.gov/about/news-releases/student-update-may-2025) (often linked directly from FAFSA confirmation).

Deadline:

- TAP application deadlines are typically [June 30 of the academic year](https://hesc.ny.gov/about/news-releases/student-update-may-2025) (e.g., 2024–25 TAP application closing June 30, 2025).


#### Excelsior Scholarship

Type: [Last-dollar “tuition-free” scholarship](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W4dL9NuBA-0) for SUNY/CUNY under an income cap.

Award Amount:

- Covers [remaining tuition at SUNY or CUNY](https://hesc.ny.gov/about/news-releases/student-update-may-2025) after other grants/scholarships, effectively allowing eligible students to attend tuition‑free.

Eligibility Requirements:

- New York State resident whose [adjusted combined federal household income](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W4dL9NuBA-0) is at or below 125,000.
- Pursuing an undergraduate degree at a SUNY or CUNY campus.
- Enrolled full time (12+ credits per term in the program of study) and completing at least [30 degree‑applicable credits per year](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W4dL9NuBA-0).
- Must agree to [live and work in New York](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W4dL9NuBA-0) for the same number of years as receiving the scholarship; otherwise the scholarship converts to a loan.

Where It Can Be Used:

- [SUNY and CUNY](https://hesc.ny.gov/about/news-releases/student-update-may-2025) colleges and universities.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Last‑dollar: federal and state grants (e.g., Pell, TAP) are applied first; Excelsior fills remaining tuition only.
- Strict enrollment and credit‑completion pacing; falling below 30 credits per year can jeopardize eligibility.

Application Process:

- FAFSA + TAP application plus a [separate Excelsior Scholarship application](https://hesc.ny.gov/about/news-releases/student-update-may-2025) through HESC during open periods.

Deadline:

- Application windows open periodically (often spring–summer); students must watch HESC announcements.


#### Enhanced Tuition Awards (ETA)

Type: [Tuition-matching scholarship](https://hesc.ny.gov/about/news-releases/student-update-may-2025) for students at participating private colleges in New York.

Award Amount:

- Helps cover private college tuition, often via a combination of state funds and institutional matching; exact amounts vary by school.

Eligibility Requirements:

- New York resident attending a [participating private college](https://hesc.ny.gov/about/news-releases/student-update-may-2025) in NYS.
- Similar income limits and full‑time enrollment expectations as Excelsior.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Participating NYS private colleges.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Last‑dollar style relative to other grants; participation is limited to colleges that opt into the program.

Application Process:

- FAFSA, TAP, and a separate ETA application through HESC when available.

Deadline:

- Follows HESC-announced timelines, often in the same general window as Excelsior.


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

- **New York State Opportunity Promise Scholarship** – [New 2025–26 program](https://ticas.org/new-york/2025-legislative-session/) covering tuition, fees, books, and supplies for adult learners (ages 25–55) without a prior degree at SUNY/CUNY community colleges, typically in high‑demand fields.
- **NYS Math and Science Teaching Incentive** and **Masters in Education Teacher Incentive Scholarships** – Cover [full tuition at SUNY/CUNY](https://hesc.ny.gov/about/news-releases/student-update-may-2025) or eligible private colleges for students preparing to teach math, science, or education in New York.


### Key Insights for New York

- TAP is the backbone of need‑based aid; almost all in‑state undergraduates at NY colleges should complete [FAFSA and TAP applications early](https://hesc.ny.gov/about/news-releases/student-update-may-2025).
- Excelsior is attractive but rigid—students must plan carefully to hit [30 credits per year](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W4dL9NuBA-0) and understand the post‑graduation residency requirement.
- Adult learners now have [better coverage](https://ticas.org/new-york/2025-legislative-session/) through emerging “Promise”-style programs like the Opportunity Promise Scholarship.
- Private college students should ask about ETA and [institutional matching](https://hesc.ny.gov/about/news-releases/student-update-may-2025) to bring private tuition closer to public-sector prices.
- Many NY programs are layered and last‑dollar; [reading award letters closely](https://www.excelsior.edu/costs-and-financing/financial-aid/new-york/) is crucial to see which dollars actually reduce out‑of‑pocket tuition versus fees and living costs.

## North Carolina

### Overview

North Carolina is relatively generous, combining a large need-based [Next NC Scholarship](https://www.cfnc.org/pay-for-college/apply-for-financial-aid/next-nc-scholarship/) with aggressive tuition reduction policies like [NC Promise](https://www.northcarolina.edu/impact/affordability-efficiency/nc-promise/). Aid is heavily need-focused for families under about 80,000 in income, with clear minimum awards at both community colleges and UNC campuses.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Next NC Scholarship

Type: [Hybrid need-based/guaranteed minimum award](https://www.cfnc.org/pay-for-college/next-nc-scholarship/) (combines Pell and state funds).

Award Amount:

- Guarantees [at least 5,000 per year](https://www.cfnc.org/pay-for-college/apply-for-financial-aid/next-nc-scholarship/) in combined federal and state aid for eligible students at UNC System universities.
- Guarantees [at least 3,000 per year](https://www.cfnc.org/pay-for-college/next-nc-scholarship/) in combined federal and state aid for eligible students at North Carolina community colleges.

Eligibility Requirements:

- North Carolina resident from a household with adjusted gross income (AGI) of [80,000 or less](https://www.cfnc.org/pay-for-college/apply-for-financial-aid/next-nc-scholarship/) and a Student Aid Index (SAI) of 7,500 or less.
- Must meet Pell-eligibility criteria and Title IV aid requirements.
- Enrolled at least half time (6+ credits) in an eligible program at a UNC institution or NC community college.
- Maintain institutional Satisfactory Academic Progress; not in default on any state loan or grant.

Where It Can Be Used:

- All [16 UNC System universities](https://www.northcarolina.edu/impact/affordability-efficiency/nc-promise/) and North Carolina community colleges.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- The scholarship is a packaging framework—[Pell is counted](https://www.cfnc.org/pay-for-college/next-nc-scholarship/) as part of the guaranteed minimum.
- State grant semester limits (6 FTE for two‑year, 10 for four‑year, 12 for five‑year programs) apply.

Application Process:

- FAFSA only; no separate state application.

Deadline:

- [Priority FAFSA deadlines](https://www.cfnc.org/pay-for-college/next-nc-scholarship/): June 1 for UNC System universities and August 15 for community colleges; after that, funding is first‑come, first‑served.


#### NC Promise Tuition Plan

Type: State-funded [tuition reduction program](https://affordableeducation.org) (tuition guarantee).

Award Amount:

- Reduces in‑state undergraduate tuition to [500 per semester](https://www.northcarolina.edu/impact/affordability-efficiency/nc-promise/) at participating UNC institutions.
- Nonresident tuition has been set at 2,500 per semester for cohorts prior to 2026, rising to 3,500 for new students beginning 2026.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Enrollment as an undergraduate at one of the [NC Promise institutions](https://affordableeducation.org) (Elizabeth City State University, Fayetteville State University, UNC Pembroke, and Western Carolina University).
- Residency requirements determine in‑state vs out‑of‑state rate.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Elizabeth City State University, Fayetteville State University, UNC Pembroke, Western Carolina University.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Applies to [tuition only](https://affordableeducation.org); standard fees and living expenses still apply.
- Students are automatically charged the lower tuition; financial aid is based on the reduced cost.

Application Process:

- No separate application; benefit is built into tuition charges.

Deadline:

- Follows institutional admission and FAFSA timelines; no separate program deadline.


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

- **North Carolina Longleaf Commitment Grant** – Covered [tuition and fees for recent high‑school graduates](https://www.nccommunitycolleges.edu/students/paying-for-college/options-for-paying-for-college/scholarships/north-carolina-longleaf-commitment-grant/) at NC community colleges; scheduled to end after Spring 2025.


### Key Insights for North Carolina

- For families under 80,000 AGI, the Next NC Scholarship can [substantially reduce net costs](https://www.cfnc.org/pay-for-college/apply-for-financial-aid/next-nc-scholarship/)—students should prioritize meeting FAFSA priority dates.
- NC Promise makes specific UNC campuses among the [lowest‑tuition four‑year options](https://www.northcarolina.edu/impact/affordability-efficiency/nc-promise/) in the country.
- North Carolina’s affordability strategy blends price controls with targeted need-based aid, which is especially favorable to in‑state students.
- Policy changes (e.g., the [phasing out of Longleaf](https://www.nccommunitycolleges.edu/students/paying-for-college/options-for-paying-for-college/scholarships/north-carolina-longleaf-commitment-grant/)) mean students should always confirm current programs with CFNC.
- About [60% of NC college students](https://affordableeducation.org) receive some form of scholarship or grant aid, underscoring the importance of applying.

## North Dakota

### Overview

North Dakota offers a modest but diversified package: a primary need-based [State Grant](https://ndus.edu/paying-for-college/state-grant) plus several merit and targeted scholarships, including the Scholars, Academic, CTE, and Native American programs. Aid levels are moderate compared to tuition, so students often rely on [institutional aid](https://uj.smartcatalogiq.com/en/2025-2026/2025-2026-undergraduate-catalog/academic-information-and-policies/payment-and-financial-aid/scholarships-and-grants/north-dakota-state-scholarships-and-grants) and federal programs to bridge the gap.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### North Dakota State Grant (Student Financial Assistance Program)

Type: [Need-based grant](https://ndus.edu/paying-for-college/state-grant).

Award Amount:

- Up to [1,375 per semester](https://uj.smartcatalogiq.com/en/2025-2026/2025-2026-undergraduate-catalog/academic-information-and-policies/payment-and-financial-aid/scholarships-and-grants/north-dakota-state-scholarships-and-grants) or 917 per quarter for full-time enrollment; prorated for part-time students.
- Limited to eight semesters or twelve quarters of full‑time equivalent enrollment, or until a bachelor’s degree is earned.

Eligibility Requirements:

- North Dakota resident who is a first-time undergraduate pursuing an eligible program at a [qualifying ND institution](https://ndus.edu/paying-for-college/state-grant).
- Enrolled at least quarter‑time.
- Must have graduated from an eligible ND or bordering-state high school, a supervised home education program, or have a ND-issued GED.

Where It Can Be Used:

- North Dakota public, private, and tribal institutions with programs of at least two years in length.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- [Funding is limited](https://ndus.edu/paying-for-college/state-grant); students are urged to file FAFSA as soon as it opens since awards are subject to available funds.

Application Process:

- FAFSA is the application; no separate state form.

Deadline:

- “As soon as possible” after FAFSA opens to ensure consideration before funds are exhausted.


#### North Dakota Scholars Program

Type: [Merit-based scholarship](https://uj.smartcatalogiq.com/en/2025-2026/2025-2026-undergraduate-catalog/academic-information-and-policies/payment-and-financial-aid/scholarships-and-grants/north-dakota-state-scholarships-and-grants).

Award Amount:

- Scholarship at private institutions equals the average tuition at a ND University System research university.
- Renewable for up to three additional years (total of four years) with a 3.50 cumulative GPA.

Eligibility Requirements:

- North Dakota residents selected as Scholars based largely on [high ACT scores](https://uj.smartcatalogiq.com/en/2025-2026/2025-2026-undergraduate-catalog/academic-information-and-policies/payment-and-financial-aid/scholarships-and-grants/north-dakota-state-scholarships-and-grants) and strong academic records.
- Full-time enrollment at an accredited ND institution.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Accredited North Dakota higher education institutions (public and private).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Highly competitive; targeted at high-achieving students.

Application Process:

- Administered by the ND University System; selection is based on test scores and academic performance.

Deadline:

- Varies by NDUS selection cycles; students should inquire early in their senior year.


#### ND Academic & Career and Technical Education (CTE) Scholarships

Type: [Merit-based](https://uj.smartcatalogiq.com/en/2025-2026/2025-2026-undergraduate-catalog/academic-information-and-policies/payment-and-financial-aid/scholarships-and-grants/north-dakota-state-scholarships-and-grants) high school curriculum and achievement scholarships.

Award Amount:

- Each scholarship provides [750 per semester](https://uj.smartcatalogiq.com/en/2025-2026/2025-2026-undergraduate-catalog/academic-information-and-policies/payment-and-financial-aid/scholarships-and-grants/north-dakota-state-scholarships-and-grants) based on full-time enrollment at an accredited ND institution.

Eligibility Requirements:

- ND high school seniors completing a rigorous academic or CTE course sequence and meeting GPA/ACT criteria.
- Enrollment as a full-time student in an accredited ND college.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Accredited ND higher education institutions.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Renewable if students maintain eligibility, typically including GPA and credit-completion standards.

Application Process:

- Students apply through [NDUS-administered processes](https://uj.smartcatalogiq.com/en/2025-2026/2025-2026-undergraduate-catalog/academic-information-and-policies/payment-and-financial-aid/scholarships-and-grants/north-dakota-state-scholarships-and-grants); eligibility verified using high school transcripts and test scores.

Deadline:

- High school seniors should complete applications during senior year.


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

- **North Dakota Native American Scholarship** – Supports Native American students with [demonstrated ability and unmet need](https://uj.smartcatalogiq.com/en/2025-2026/2025-2026-undergraduate-catalog/academic-information-and-policies/payment-and-financial-aid/scholarships-and-grants/north-dakota-state-scholarships-and-grants).
- Multiple smaller scholarships via groups like [North Dakota United Foundation](https://ndunited.org/foundation/scholarships-and-grants) for members and dependents.


### Key Insights for North Dakota

- The State Grant is the central need-based program; applying early via FAFSA is crucial because [funds are limited](https://ndus.edu/paying-for-college/state-grant).
- Merit programs significantly improve affordability for high achievers but require [rigorous high school preparation](https://uj.smartcatalogiq.com/en/2025-2026/2025-2026-undergraduate-catalog/academic-information-and-policies/payment-and-financial-aid/scholarships-and-grants/north-dakota-state-scholarships-and-grants).
- Aid is generally moderate relative to total cost, so students should pay close attention to [state-specific funding decisions](https://bnd.nd.gov/education-funding/be-confident-with-your-decisions/scholarships-and-grants/) and private scholarships.
- Native American students have additional [state-level scholarship options](https://uj.smartcatalogiq.com/en/2025-2026/2025-2026-undergraduate-catalog/academic-information-and-policies/payment-and-financial-aid/scholarships-and-grants/north-dakota-state-scholarships-and-grants) that can be combined with federal and tribal aid.
- Students planning to attend out of state should not expect ND state aid to travel; most programs require [in‑state institutions](https://ndus.edu/paying-for-college/state-grant).

## Ohio

### Overview

Ohio offers a substantial need-based grant, the [Ohio College Opportunity Grant (OCOG)](https://www.csuohio.edu/financial-aid/ohio-college-opportunity-grant-ocog), as its primary statewide aid. Recent [budget increases](https://collegenowgc.org/state-budget-includes-increase-to-ohios-college-opportunity-grant-and-new-merit-scholarship/) have expanded eligibility, making the system strongly need-focused. For many students, OCOG is the key state resource to stack with Pell and institutional grants.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Ohio College Opportunity Grant (OCOG)

Type: [Need-based grant](https://www.cedarville.edu/offices/financial-aid/undergraduate/student-aid-2025-26/info/2025/ohio-college-opportunity-grant).

Award Amount:

- For 2024–25, full-time students at private institutions can receive [5,000 annually](https://www.cedarville.edu/offices/financial-aid/undergraduate/student-aid-2025-26/info/2025/ohio-college-opportunity-grant) (2,500 per semester), with prorated amounts for part-time enrollment.
- Awards are [tuition and general-fee specific](https://www.capital.edu/admission-aid/office-of-financial-aid/types-of-aid/ohio-college-opportunity-grant/) and may vary by sector, including public, private non‑profit, or private for‑profit institutions.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Ohio resident undergraduate with high financial need.
- A [Student Aid Index (SAI) of 3,750 or less](https://www.cedarville.edu/offices/financial-aid/undergraduate/student-aid-2025-26/info/2025/ohio-college-opportunity-grant) and a maximum household income of 96,000.
- Enrollment in an eligible Ohio public university main campus or private institution; eligibility for [community college and regional campuses](https://www.news5cleveland.com/news/state/ohio-senate-restores-some-college-student-grant-funding-in-proposed-operating-budget) has been restored in recent budget actions.
- Limited to 10 full‑time semesters of eligibility.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Eligible Ohio public university main campuses, many private non‑profit colleges, and [recently restored community college](https://collegenowgc.org/state-budget-includes-increase-to-ohios-college-opportunity-grant-and-new-merit-scholarship/) and regional campuses.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Students already receiving 100% tuition/fee coverage from other waivers or aid [may not receive OCOG](https://www.capital.edu/admission-aid/office-of-financial-aid/types-of-aid/ohio-college-opportunity-grant/) due to its fee-specific nature.
- Award levels and eligible sectors are subject to [state appropriations and policy changes](https://www.news5cleveland.com/news/state/ohio-senate-restores-some-college-student-grant-funding-in-proposed-operating-budget) each year.

Application Process:

- FAFSA is the only application; it must be received by [October 1 each year](https://www.cedarville.edu/offices/financial-aid/undergraduate/student-aid-2025-26/info/2025/ohio-college-opportunity-grant) for priority consideration.

Deadline:

- State guidance emphasizes filing the FAFSA by [early fall](https://www.csuohio.edu/financial-aid/ohio-college-opportunity-grant-ocog) to ensure consideration before funds are exhausted.


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

- Recent biennial budgets created a [Governor’s Merit Scholarship](https://collegenowgc.org/state-budget-includes-increase-to-ohios-college-opportunity-grant-and-new-merit-scholarship/) and significantly increased total OCOG appropriations to expand access for more families.
- Targeted programs like [Choose Ohio First](https://www.capital.edu/admission-aid/office-of-financial-aid/types-of-aid/ohio-college-opportunity-grant/) provide additional merit and workforce scholarships for students entering STEM fields.


### Key Insights for Ohio

- OCOG is the primary resource for [low‑SAI and lower‑income students](https://www.csuohio.edu/financial-aid/ohio-college-opportunity-grant-ocog), who should prioritize early FAFSA filing to lock in eligibility.
- Because OCOG is [tuition and fee‑specific](https://www.capital.edu/admission-aid/office-of-financial-aid/types-of-aid/ohio-college-opportunity-grant/), it pairs best with institutions where tuition is not already fully covered by other waivers.
- [Restored eligibility](https://www.news5cleveland.com/news/state/ohio-senate-restores-some-college-student-grant-funding-in-proposed-operating-budget) for community colleges and regional campuses makes two‑year pathways significantly more affordable than in prior cycles.
- Students should monitor [each budget cycle](https://collegenowgc.org/state-budget-includes-increase-to-ohios-college-opportunity-grant-and-new-merit-scholarship/) as eligibility rules and dollar amounts can shift with state appropriations.

## Oklahoma

### Overview

Oklahoma has a strong commitment to college access via [Oklahoma’s Promise](https://okpromise.org/benefits.shtml) (a major tuition scholarship for middle- and lower-income families) plus a broad need-based grant known as [OTAG](https://www.okcollegestart.org/Financial_Aid_Planning/Oklahoma_Grants/Oklahoma_Tuition_Aid_Grant.aspx). Aid is relatively generous for in-state students who commit early in high school and file FAFSA early each year.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Oklahoma’s Promise

Type: [Early-commitment, last-dollar tuition scholarship](https://okpromise.org/benefits.shtml) with income and academic requirements.

Award Amount:

- Pays tuition at an [Oklahoma public two‑year college or four‑year university](https://langston.edu/oklahoma-promise/) for up to five years (or up to about 129 credit hours), and covers at least a portion of tuition at [accredited Oklahoma private colleges](https://okhighered.org/2025/12/dec-31-deadline-fast-approaching-for-oklahomas-promise-scholarship-program/) and some public career tech programs.

Eligibility Requirements (High School Phase):

- Oklahoma resident student in [8th–12th grade](https://okpromise.org/resources.shtml) (or homeschool ages 13–15) who applies by the program’s grade-level deadline.
- Family income at application [generally must not exceed 60,000 to 80,000](https://okhighered.org/2025/12/dec-31-deadline-fast-approaching-for-oklahomas-promise-scholarship-program/) depending on the number of dependent children; some special provisions apply for adopted children and legal guardianship.
- Must complete a [required 15‑unit college‑prep curriculum](https://okpromise.org/benefits.shtml) and meet conduct standards.

Eligibility Requirements (College Phase):

- Oklahoma resident attending an eligible Oklahoma public college/university, private college, or certain career technology programs.
- Must meet ongoing conduct, GPA, and income rules; students with [annual family income above 100,000](https://www.okcu.edu/paying-for-ocu/scholarships-and-assistance/state-grants-scholarships) based on current FAFSA are ineligible for that year.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Oklahoma public two‑year and four‑year institutions; participating Oklahoma private colleges; eligible public career tech programs.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Covers [tuition only](https://okhighered.org/2025/12/dec-31-deadline-fast-approaching-for-oklahomas-promise-scholarship-program/), not fees, books, supplies, or room and board.
- Lifetime cap around [129 credit hours](https://www.okcu.edu/paying-for-ocu/scholarships-and-assistance/state-grants-scholarships) (with exceptions for longer programs).
- Program has been expanded by [recent legislation (House Bill 1727)](https://okpromise.org/resources.shtml) to reach more families and includes extended application deadlines.

Application Process:

- Online [Oklahoma’s Promise application](https://okhighered.org/2025/12/dec-31-deadline-fast-approaching-for-oklahomas-promise-scholarship-program/) while in grades 8–12; paper forms also available.
- FAFSA required each year during college for continued eligibility.

Deadline:

- For 12th graders, the application deadline has been extended to [December 31, 2025](https://okhighered.org/2025/12/dec-31-deadline-fast-approaching-for-oklahomas-promise-scholarship-program/); younger students should apply as early as possible in grades 8–11.


#### Oklahoma Tuition Aid Grant (OTAG)

Type: [Need-based grant](https://www.okcollegestart.org/Financial_Aid_Planning/Oklahoma_Grants/Oklahoma_Tuition_Aid_Grant.aspx).

Award Amount:

- Award amounts vary annually based on state funding; examples at some colleges show typical awards around [1,000–1,300 per academic year](https://www.collegexpress.com/scholarships/oklahoma-tuition-aid-grant-program-otag/5002436/) for full-time students.
- Available for both [full-time and part-time](https://bulldog.swosu.edu/student-services/financial-aid/grants/otag.php) undergraduates.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Oklahoma resident attending an eligible [Oklahoma college, university, or career technology center](https://rose.edu/affordability/financial-aid/types-of-aid/oklahoma-tuition-aid-grant-otag/).
- Demonstrated financial need based on FAFSA; eligibility measured using the [Student Aid Index (SAI)](https://www.collegescholarships.com/scholarships/detail/86871) or Expected Family Contribution metrics.
- Pell-eligible or Pell‑like need is typically required.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Eligible Oklahoma [public and private colleges/universities](https://bulldog.swosu.edu/student-services/financial-aid/grants/otag.php) and participating career technology centers.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- State funding is limited and OTAG commonly receives more eligible applications than it can fund, making [early FAFSA filing critical](https://rose.edu/affordability/financial-aid/types-of-aid/oklahoma-tuition-aid-grant-otag/).
- Awards are reduced or rescinded if total aid exceeds [cost of attendance](https://bulldog.swosu.edu/student-services/financial-aid/grants/otag.php).

Application Process:

- FAFSA is the application; [no separate OTAG form](https://www.okcollegestart.org/Financial_Aid_Planning/Oklahoma_Grants/Oklahoma_Tuition_Aid_Grant.aspx) for most students.

Deadline:

- Students are strongly advised to [file FAFSA as soon as possible](https://www.okcollegestart.org/Financial_Aid_Planning/Oklahoma_Grants/Oklahoma_Tuition_Aid_Grant.aspx) because awards are first‑come, first‑served until funds are depleted.


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

- **Oklahoma Tuition Equalization Grant** is another state grant targeted to Oklahoma residents at private colleges; students should ask private college financial aid offices about it.
- Numerous [campus-based augmentations](https://okpromise.org/benefits.shtml) exist for Oklahoma’s Promise students, varying by institution.


### Key Insights for Oklahoma

- Oklahoma’s Promise is one of the stronger [early‑commitment tuition programs](https://okhighered.org/2025/12/dec-31-deadline-fast-approaching-for-oklahomas-promise-scholarship-program/) nationally; students should apply as early as middle school.
- OTAG is highly time-sensitive—late FAFSA filers are often [shut out despite eligibility](https://rose.edu/affordability/financial-aid/types-of-aid/oklahoma-tuition-aid-grant-otag/).
- Oklahoma’s structure favors students who stay in state and attend public institutions, where [Promise covers full tuition](https://okpromise.org/benefits.shtml) and OTAG can help close remaining gaps.
- [Undocumented students](https://www.collegexpress.com/scholarships/oklahoma-tuition-aid-grant-program-otag/5002436/) may still qualify for OTAG under specific conditions, which is unusual among states.
- Managing credit loads to stay within the [129‑credit cap](https://okpromise.org/benefits.shtml) helps avoid losing coverage before finishing a degree.

## Oregon

### Overview

Oregon is relatively strong on need-based aid, anchored by the [Oregon Opportunity Grant](https://www.oregonstudentaid.gov/grants/oregon-opportunity-grant/) and a robust community-college [Promise program](https://oregonstudentaid.gov/grants/oregon-promise-grant/). Programs are first‑come, first‑served and highly sensitive to FAFSA/ORSAA timing and credit limits.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Oregon Opportunity Grant (OOG)

Type: [Need-based grant](https://www.corban.edu/scholarships/oregon-opportunity-grant/); Oregon’s largest state-funded aid program.

Award Amount:

- For 2025–26, the maximum award at four-year colleges is cited around [7,524–7,800 per year](https://collegeoptions.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Oregon-2025-2026-CCO-FA-Presentation-09-06-2024.pdf), with lower ranges at community colleges depending on sector and SAI.
- Awards are [prorated](https://www.oregonstudentaid.gov/grants/oregon-opportunity-grant/) based on enrollment level (half-time, three-quarter-time, full-time).

Eligibility Requirements:

- Oregon resident, U.S. citizen/eligible noncitizen or qualifying under Oregon’s [tuition-equity laws](https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/displayDivisionRules.action?selectedDivision=2426).
- Pursuing first associate or bachelor’s degree at an eligible Oregon public or [nonprofit private institution](https://www.corban.edu/scholarships/oregon-opportunity-grant/); enrolled at least half time (6+ credits).
- Demonstrated financial need per HECC’s shared-responsibility model.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Oregon community colleges, public universities, Oregon Health & Science University, and [eligible nonprofit private colleges](https://www.oregonstudentaid.gov/grants/oregon-opportunity-grant/).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Available for up to [four years](https://www.oregonstudentaid.gov/grants/oregon-opportunity-grant/) (12 quarters or eight semesters) of full-time enrollment.
- Awards are [first‑come, first‑served](https://www.corban.edu/scholarships/oregon-opportunity-grant/) until funds are depleted; early filing is critical.
- The program is sensitive to policy changes; award amounts and [SAI cutoffs](https://collegeoptions.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Oregon-2025-2026-CCO-FA-Presentation-09-06-2024.pdf) can shift by year.

Application Process:

- FAFSA or Oregon Student Aid Application (ORSAA) required each year; no separate OOG application.

Deadline:

- Students are urged to submit FAFSA/ORSAA as soon as applications open; many advisors recommend a [March 1 target](https://collegeoptions.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Oregon-2025-2026-CCO-FA-Presentation-09-06-2024.pdf).


#### Oregon Promise Grant

Type: [Last-dollar](https://cocc.edu/departments/financial-aid/types-of-aid/grants/oregon-promise) community-college tuition grant.

Award Amount:

- For 2025–26, eligible full-time students are guaranteed a minimum award of around [2,202 per year](https://cocc.edu/departments/financial-aid/types-of-aid/grants/files/or_prom_gen_factsheet.pdf) and up to about 4,584.
- Award covers most average community-college tuition after other grants are applied; paid proportionally by enrollment level.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Recent Oregon [high school graduate or GED recipient](https://oregonstudentaid.gov/grants/oregon-promise-grant/) (must apply during senior year or immediately after GED).
- Minimum high school [GPA of 2.0](https://cocc.edu/departments/financial-aid/types-of-aid/grants/oregon-promise) or GED score of 145.
- Oregon resident for at least 12 months prior to enrollment.
- Enrollment in a certificate or degree program at an Oregon community college [within six months](https://oregonstudentaid.gov/grants/oregon-promise-grant/) of graduation/GED.
- Must complete Oregon Promise application and FAFSA/ORSAA, and accept all federal/state grant aid.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Any [Oregon community college](https://www.oregon.gov/highered/strategy-research/Documents/Legislative-Budget-Resources/Leg-2025/HECC-OOG-and-ORPromise-Overview-2025.pdf).

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Limited to students with fewer than [90 attempted college credits](https://cocc.edu/departments/financial-aid/types-of-aid/grants/files/or_prom_gen_factsheet.pdf).
- Students must maintain at least [half‑time continuous enrollment](https://www.oregon.gov/highered/access/Documents/OSAC/Oregon%20Promise%20General%20Fact%20Sheet.pdf) and meet SAP requirements.
- Subject to an [SAI limit](https://collegeoptions.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Oregon-2025-2026-CCO-FA-Presentation-09-06-2024.pdf) (recently around 25,000) and strict application windows.

Application Process:

- Oregon Promise application via [OSAC Student Portal](https://oregonstudentaid.gov/grants/oregon-promise-grant/) plus FAFSA/ORSAA.

Deadline:

- Application tied to graduation term; most students must apply by [June 1 for fall entry](https://collegeoptions.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Oregon-2025-2026-CCO-FA-Presentation-09-06-2024.pdf).


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

- Sector-specific programs and [targeted scholarships](https://www.oregon.gov/highered/strategy-research/Documents/Legislative-Budget-Resources/Leg-2025/HECC-OOG-and-ORPromise-Overview-2025.pdf) are administered through the Oregon Student Aid site and OSAC scholarship portal.


### Key Insights for Oregon

- Oregon is strong for residents who attend in-state institutions and [file FAFSA/ORSAA early](https://www.corban.edu/scholarships/oregon-opportunity-grant/).
- Oregon Promise is particularly powerful for recent graduates who start at a community college quickly; [missing the six‑month window](https://oregonstudentaid.gov/grants/oregon-promise-grant/) can forfeit eligibility permanently.
- Both OOG and Oregon Promise are sensitive to SAI and credit caps—[staying under 90 attempted credits](https://cocc.edu/departments/financial-aid/types-of-aid/grants/files/or_prom_gen_factsheet.pdf) for Oregon Promise is crucial.
- [Theology or divinity degrees](https://www.corban.edu/scholarships/oregon-opportunity-grant/) are not eligible for OOG, which can matter at certain private institutions.

## Pennsylvania

### Overview

Pennsylvania’s flagship state aid is the [PA State Grant](https://sdlancaster.org/news/2025-26-pa-state-grant-application-is-live-heres-what-students-need-to-know/), a substantial need-based program administered by PHEAA, supplemented by merit-like programs such as Ready to Succeed (RTSS). Awards are meaningful but competitive, with [strict deadlines](https://drexel.edu/drexelcentral/about/news/2025/March/pa-grant-program-application-for-2025-summer-term) and usage limits.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Pennsylvania State Grant (PA State Grant / PASG)

Type: [Need-based grant](https://sfs.temple.edu/financial-aid-types/grants/pennsylvania-state-grant-pasg).

Award Amount:

- Maximum grant for 2025–26 is around [5,260 for full-time students](https://sfs.temple.edu/financial-aid-types/grants/pennsylvania-state-grant-pasg); part-time awards are prorated.
- Some outreach materials describe [potential awards “up to 5,000”](https://sdlancaster.org/news/2025-26-pa-state-grant-application-is-live-heres-what-students-need-to-know/) for a given year; exact amounts depend on sector, enrollment, and appropriations.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Pennsylvania resident attending an eligible postsecondary institution, generally in Pennsylvania; limited portability for some out-of-state programs.
- Demonstrated financial need via FAFSA and [PHEAA’s formula](https://sfs.temple.edu/financial-aid-types/grants/pennsylvania-state-grant-pasg).
- Enrollment at least half time (with differences between full- and part-time rules) and pursuing an approved program.
- Limited to [eight full-time equivalent semesters](https://sfs.temple.edu/financial-aid-types/grants/pennsylvania-state-grant-pasg).

Where It Can Be Used:

- Eligible Pennsylvania colleges and universities; some approved out‑of‑state programs at reduced levels.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- [Separate State Grant Form (SGF)](https://sdlancaster.org/news/2025-26-pa-state-grant-application-is-live-heres-what-students-need-to-know/) often required in addition to FAFSA; PHEAA notifies students via email or mail.
- [Summer grants](https://drexel.edu/drexelcentral/about/news/2025/March/pa-grant-program-application-for-2025-summer-term) count toward the eight‑semester limit.
- [Delays in state budgets](https://www.psu.edu/costs-aid/types-of-aid/grants/pennsylvania-grants-pheaa) can temporarily pause awarding activities, though grants resume once budgets are enacted.

Application Process:

- File FAFSA, then complete the [PA State Grant Form (SGF)](https://drexel.edu/drexelcentral/about/news/2025/March/pa-grant-program-application-for-2025-summer-term) via PHEAA’s Account Access portal when prompted.

Deadline:

- FAFSA and SGF priority deadlines vary by year, but communications for 2025–26 [emphasize prompt completion](https://sdlancaster.org/news/2025-26-pa-state-grant-application-is-live-heres-what-students-need-to-know/) once invited.
- Summer State Grant applications have specific deadlines, such as [August 15](https://drexel.edu/drexelcentral/about/news/2025/March/pa-grant-program-application-for-2025-summer-term) for the summer term.


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

- **Ready to Succeed Scholarship (RTSS)** – Merit-based supplement for [high-achieving, middle-income students](https://www.psu.edu/costs-aid/types-of-aid/grants/pennsylvania-grants-pheaa), administered by PHEAA alongside PA State Grants.
- Additional [PHEAA-managed niche programs](https://www.psu.edu/costs-aid/types-of-aid/grants/pennsylvania-grants-pheaa) (e.g., targeted industry programs, loan forgiveness for certain careers) provide extra aid for teaching, nursing, and high-demand fields.


### Key Insights for Pennsylvania

- PA State Grant is substantial and can [approach in‑state community-college tuition levels](https://sdlancaster.org/news/2025-26-pa-state-grant-application-is-live-heres-what-students-need-to-know/); FAFSA plus SGF completion is essential.
- Funding is sensitive to [state budget timelines](https://www.psu.edu/costs-aid/types-of-aid/grants/pennsylvania-grants-pheaa); students should monitor PHEAA messages closely during budget years.
- [Eight‑semester caps](https://sfs.temple.edu/financial-aid-types/grants/pennsylvania-state-grant-pasg) mean prolonged part‑time enrollment or frequent major changes can reduce total grant eligibility.
- Summer grants are valuable but [consume semester eligibility](https://drexel.edu/drexelcentral/about/news/2025/March/pa-grant-program-application-for-2025-summer-term), so they should be used strategically to accelerate graduation.

## Rhode Island

### Overview

Rhode Island has a relatively modest statewide grant program but a very notable “Promise” last‑dollar scholarship at the [Community College of Rhode Island (CCRI)](https://www.ccri.edu/ripromise/), recently complemented by a similar [Hope Scholarship](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2023/09/26/metro/rhode-island-colleges-free-tuition-program-is-here/) at Rhode Island College. Aid is good for students willing to start at CCRI or follow structured in‑state pathways, but basic [need-based grants](https://blog.fu.loans/financial-aid-and-student-loans-in-rhode-island/) are smaller than in some neighboring states.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Rhode Island Promise Scholarship (CCRI)

Type: [Last-dollar, tuition-free](https://www.appily.com/scholarships/rhode-island-promise-program) community college scholarship.

Eligibility Requirements:

Where It Can Be Used:

- [Community College of Rhode Island (CCRI)](https://riopc.edu/policies/rhode-island-promise-scholarship-program-policies-and-procedures/) only.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Last‑dollar: other grants and scholarships are applied first; Promise covers [remaining tuition and mandatory fees](https://www.appily.com/scholarships/rhode-island-promise-program) only.

Application Process:

- Apply and be admitted to CCRI, complete FAFSA and financial aid file, and submit final transcripts; [no separate application](https://www.ccri.edu/ripromise/) beyond institutional steps.

Deadline:

- CCRI encourages students to complete financial aid by [April 1](https://www.ccri.edu/ripromise/faqs.html) to maximize aid.


#### Rhode Island State Grant

Type: [Need-based grant](https://www.collegescholarships.org/grants/states/rhode-island.htm).

Award Amount:

- Typically between about [250 and 700 per year](https://blog.fu.loans/financial-aid-and-student-loans-in-rhode-island/) depending on financial need.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Rhode Island resident.
- Demonstrated financial need via FAFSA.
- Enrolled at least part time in an [eligible institution](https://www.collegescholarships.org/grants/states/rhode-island.htm).

Where It Can Be Used:

- Eligible in-state institutions and some [out-of-state institutions](https://www.collegescholarships.org/grants/states/rhode-island.htm), subject to program rules.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Grant is supplemental and relatively small; intended to fill part of remaining need.

Application Process:

- FAFSA only; listing Rhode Island as state of residence prompts automatic consideration.

Deadline:

- FAFSA deadline around [March 1](https://www.collegescholarships.org/grants/states/rhode-island.htm) for maximum consideration.


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

- **Rhode Island College “Hope Scholarship”** – A [last‑dollar program](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2023/09/26/metro/rhode-island-colleges-free-tuition-program-is-here/) giving free tuition for junior and senior years at Rhode Island College for eligible residents with at least 60 credits and a 2.5 GPA.

### Key Insights for Rhode Island

- FAFSA remains the gateway; missing [priority timeframes](https://web.uri.edu/financial-aid/grants-and-scholarships/) can reduce available aid.
- Students transferring from CCRI to RIC or URI should [plan carefully](https://www.ccri.edu/ripromise/) so benefits line up with credit pace and major requirements.

## South Carolina

### Overview

South Carolina has one of the most robust merit scholarship portfolios in the country (LIFE, Palmetto Fellows, HOPE) plus need-based and lottery-funded tuition assistance, especially at two-year colleges. The system heavily rewards strong high‑school performance and timely FAFSA filing, with multiple programs funded by the state lottery.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### LIFE Scholarship (Legislative Incentive for Future Excellence)

Type: Broad merit-based scholarship.

Award Amount:

- **Four-year institutions:** Up to **$5,000** per year ($4,700 for tuition + $300 for books).
- **Two-year institutions:** Up to **$5,000** per year (recently increased at some technical colleges to match the university rate).
- **Enhancements:** Eligible STEM, Education, or Accounting majors can receive an additional **$2,500** per year starting their sophomore year, totaling **$7,500** annually.

Eligibility Requirements:

- SC resident and U.S. citizen/eligible noncitizen.
- **Freshmen at 4-year schools:** Must meet 2 of 3 criteria: 3.0+ high school GPA, 1100+ SAT (or 22+ ACT), or top 30% of graduating class.
- **Freshmen at 2-year schools:** 3.0+ high school GPA is the sole requirement.
- **Renewal:** Must maintain a 3.0 "LIFE GPA" and earn an average of 30 credit hours per academic year.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Eligible SC public and independent colleges, universities, and technical colleges.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Cannot be combined with Palmetto Fellows or HOPE.
- Total aid (LIFE + other grants) cannot exceed the cost of attendance.

#### Palmetto Fellows Scholarship

Type: Premier high-merit scholarship.

Award Amount:

- **First year:** Up to **$6,700**.
- **Sophomore, Junior, Senior years:** Up to **$7,500** per year.
- **Enhancements:** Eligible STEM majors can receive an additional **$2,500** Enhancement, raising the total to **$10,000** per year in later years.

Eligibility Requirements:

- Top-tier academic thresholds (e.g., 1200+ SAT/25+ ACT, 3.5+ GPA, and top 6% of class rank; or 1400+ SAT/31+ ACT and 4.0+ GPA).
- Full-time enrollment at an eligible four-year SC institution.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Eligible four-year colleges and universities in South Carolina.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- Most competitive merit award; students are nominated through their high school during early- or late-award cycles.
- Requires 3.0 institutional GPA and 30 credits per year for renewal.

#### South Carolina Need-Based Grant (SCNBG)

Type: Need-based grant.

Award Amount:

- For **2026–27**, the maximum grant at independent colleges has been set at **$5,000**.
- At public institutions, awards typically reach up to **$3,500** per year for full-time students and up to **$1,250** for part-time students.

Eligibility Requirements:

- SC resident demonstrating significant financial need via FAFSA (typically Pell-eligible).
- Enrolled at an eligible public or independent SC institution.
- Maintain a 2.0 GPA and complete 24 credit hours per year for renewal.

Where It Can Be Used:

- Eligible SC public and independent colleges and universities.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- **First-come, first-served:** Funds are often exhausted quickly; early FAFSA filing is essential.

#### Lottery Tuition Assistance (LTA)

Type: Lottery-funded, supplemental tuition grant for two-year students.

Award Amount:

- Awarded on a per-credit-hour basis; for **2026–27**, levels are approximately **$80–$100 per credit hour** depending on total state funding.

Eligibility Requirements:

- SC resident enrolled in at least 6 credit hours in a degree, diploma, or certificate program at a two-year institution.
- Must complete FAFSA (or a FAFSA waiver if eligible).

Where It Can Be Used:

- SC technical colleges and eligible public two-year institutions.

Key Restrictions / Notes:

- **Last-dollar:** Applied only after Federal Pell and state need-based grants are used.
- Cannot be received if the student is receiving the LIFE Scholarship.

### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

* **SC HOPE Scholarship:** A **$2,800** one-year scholarship for freshmen at four-year schools who have a 3.0 GPA but do not meet the SAT/class rank requirements for LIFE.
* **SC WINS (Workforce and Industrial Needs Scholarship):** Supplements LTA at technical colleges for students in high-demand fields (e.g., Healthcare, Mechatronics, IT). Provides **$100 per credit hour**, up to **$2,500** per year.
* **SC National Guard CAP:** Provides up to **$5,500** per year for eligible Guard members at SC institutions.

### Key Insights for South Carolina

* **Merit is King:** South Carolina offers some of the most accessible large-scale merit awards in the US. Even students who don't qualify for the top Palmetto award can often secure **$5,000** through LIFE with a solid GPA.
* **STEM Bonus:** The **$2,500 Enhancement** makes STEM fields particularly lucrative for LIFE and Palmetto scholars, but you must complete 14 hours of math/science credits in your freshman year to unlock it.
* **Technical College Advantage:** Between LTA, SC WINS, and Need-Based grants, many technical college students can attend tuition-free.
* **The "First-Come" Rule:** While merit awards are guaranteed for those who qualify, the **Need-Based Grant** is budget-dependent. Filing your FAFSA as soon as it opens in October is the only way to ensure you don't miss out on those funds.

## South Dakota

### Overview

South Dakota offers a [moderate but targeted mix](https://doe.sd.gov/scholarships/) of merit, workforce, and need-based aid, with especially strong support for technical programs and [in‑state retention](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/south-dakota/).
Aid leans merit-heavy at four-year schools (Opportunity Scholarship) but adds [last‑dollar and need-based components](https://www.freedomscholarshipsd.com) through Build Dakota and the Freedom Scholarship.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### South Dakota Opportunity Scholarship (SDOS)

- **Type:** Merit-based
- **Award Amount:** [Up to \$7,500 total](https://ourdakotadreams.com/k12-students/opportunity-scholarship/) over four years (typically split over eight semesters).
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - South Dakota resident graduating from an [eligible SD high school](https://www.scholarships.com/scholarships/south-dakota-opportunity-scholarship).
    - Meets a prescribed [rigorous high school curriculum](https://ourdakotadreams.com/k12-students/opportunity-scholarship/) and minimum 3.0 GPA.
    - [ACT composite of at least 24](https://www.scholarships.com/scholarships/south-dakota-opportunity-scholarship) (or qualifying SAT equivalent) unless using higher ACT-only pathway.
    - Must enroll at an eligible SD institution within a [specified time after graduation](https://ourdakotadreams.com/k12-students/opportunity-scholarship/) (generally within one year).
    - Typically full-time undergraduate enrollment required.
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - [South Dakota public universities](https://ourdakotadreams.com/k12-students/opportunity-scholarship/).
    - [South Dakota technical colleges](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/south-dakota/).
    - Eligible [in-state private colleges](https://www.scholarships.com/scholarships/south-dakota-opportunity-scholarship) accredited in the region.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Not competitive; any student meeting criteria and applying on time is likely to receive it as long as the program is funded.
    - Renewal requires maintaining at least a [3.0 cumulative college GPA](https://www.scholarships.com/scholarships/south-dakota-opportunity-scholarship) and completing a set number of credits per year.
    - Covers tuition only (not room/board) up to the state-set maximum.
- **Application Process:**
    - Complete the SD Opportunity Scholarship application via the [Our Dakota Dreams/Board of Regents portal](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/south-dakota/).
    - FAFSA often recommended to coordinate with other aid, though SDOS itself is merit-based.
- **Deadline:**
    - For fall 2026 entry, application deadline is listed as [September 1, 2026](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/other/south-dakota-scholarships/); similar early‑September deadlines are typical each year.


#### Build Dakota Scholarship (Technical Colleges)

- **Type:** Last-dollar / workforce, merit + program-based
- **Award Amount:** [Full ride for approved technical programs](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/other/south-dakota-scholarships/) (tuition, fees, books, tools).
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - South Dakota resident or sometimes nonresident in [select employer-sponsored tracks](https://doe.sd.gov/scholarships/) (details vary by year/program).
    - Accepted into an [eligible high‑need program](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/other/south-dakota-scholarships/) at a participating SD technical college.
    - Typically full-time enrollment; must meet program-specific academic standards.
    - [Commit to working in South Dakota](https://doe.sd.gov/scholarships/) in the field after graduation (service agreement).
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - Participating [South Dakota technical colleges](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/south-dakota/) in approved high‑need programs only.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - True full‑cost coverage but only for designated programs; capacity is [limited and competitive](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/other/south-dakota-scholarships/).
    - Service commitment in SD workforce; leaving the state or field early can [convert the scholarship](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/south-dakota/) to a repayable obligation.
    - Campus/program-level selection; awards not guaranteed even if minimum criteria are met.
- **Application Process:**
    - Apply to a [participating technical college](https://doe.sd.gov/scholarships/) and an eligible program.
    - Complete the [Build Dakota application](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/other/south-dakota-scholarships/) and supply any required employer/industry partner documentation.
    - FAFSA strongly encouraged because Build Dakota is “last dollar” after other grants/scholarships.
- **Deadline:**
    - Recent cycle dates run roughly [January 1–March 31](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/other/south-dakota-scholarships/); exact deadlines posted annually by program and campus.


#### South Dakota Freedom Scholarship

- **Type:** Need-based / gap-closing, often last‑dollar style
- **Award Amount:** Varies by institution; designed to close [remaining unmet financial need](https://www.freedomscholarshipsd.com) after other aid, often “thousands” per student.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - [South Dakota resident](https://www.freedomscholarshipsd.com) attending a participating SD college or university.
    - Demonstrated financial need based on [FAFSA and institutional criteria](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/south-dakota/).
    - [Commitment to live and work](https://www.freedomscholarshipsd.com) in South Dakota after graduation (length varies by institution/award).
    - Enrollment at least half-time in a bachelor’s program at a partner institution.
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - Participating [public and private SD colleges](https://www.freedomscholarshipsd.com) and universities across the state.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - [Campus-selected](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/south-dakota/); students are not applying to the state directly but are chosen by their institution from eligible applicants.
    - Designed to [reduce borrowing](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/other/south-dakota-scholarships/) and stop-outs due to small remaining balances.
    - Recipients are expected to [remain in SD](https://www.freedomscholarshipsd.com) for a set period; leaving early may trigger payback terms.
- **Application Process:**
    - File FAFSA by [institutional priority dates](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/south-dakota/) and enroll at a participating campus.
    - Meet any additional documentation requests from your financial aid office; selection is internal.
- **Deadline:**
    - No single statewide date; students should follow each [institution’s FAFSA](https://www.freedomscholarshipsd.com) and scholarship priority deadlines.


#### Dakota Corps Scholarship (service-based)

- **Type:** Service-based, tuition scholarship for critical-need careers
- **Award Amount:** Can cover up to [full tuition](https://doe.sd.gov/scholarships/) at eligible South Dakota institutions for approved critical-need fields.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - South Dakota high school graduate or resident planning to enter a [designated critical-need occupation](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/south-dakota/) in SD (e.g., teaching, nursing, other high-demand fields).
    - Strong academic record; competitive selection.
    - Full-time enrollment in an approved program at a participating SD institution.
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - Eligible South Dakota [public and private colleges](https://doe.sd.gov/scholarships/) and universities offering approved programs.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Requires a [service commitment](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/south-dakota/) to work in the critical field in South Dakota for a specified period after graduation.
    - Limited number of awards; [highly competitive](https://doe.sd.gov/scholarships/) and not guaranteed.
- **Application Process:**
    - Apply through the [South Dakota Board of Regents/DOE](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/south-dakota/) scholarship process for Dakota Corps, including essays and recommendations.
- **Deadline:**
    - [Annual deadlines](https://doe.sd.gov/scholarships/) set by the Board of Regents; typically fall–winter for the following academic year.


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs (South Dakota)

- State-level [need-based grant programs](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/other/south-dakota-scholarships/) exist with awards typically in the \$500–\$2,000 range, often using philanthropic matching and institutional selection to close small remaining gaps.
- Additional [niche scholarships](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/south-dakota/) (e.g., agriculture, 4‑H, industry groups) are common but are usually private rather than state-funded.


### Key Insights for This State

- South Dakota is [strongest for high-achieving students](https://doe.sd.gov/scholarships/) and technical-college pathways via SDOS and Build Dakota; need-only support is more modest but [growing through the Freedom Scholarship](https://www.freedomscholarshipsd.com).
- Many flagship programs [require students to stay](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/south-dakota/), study, and later work in South Dakota, making them best for students already committed to remaining in-state.
- Deadlines can be early ([Jan–Mar for Build Dakota](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/other/south-dakota-scholarships/), early fall for SDOS), so students who wait on the FAFSA or applications can easily miss major awards.
- Funds like SDOS are not competitive but do require [rigorous high school preparation](https://ourdakotadreams.com/k12-students/opportunity-scholarship/); missing coursework or GPA thresholds is a common disqualifier.
- Overall, SD is [moderate for traditional need-based aid](https://doe.sd.gov/scholarships/) but strong if you fit merit/workforce criteria and plan to stay in the state.

## Tennessee

### Overview

Tennessee is one of the [most generous aid states](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/), combining large lottery-funded merit (HOPE), robust last-dollar community college programs (Promise/Reconnect), and a [significant need-based grant](https://www.collegefortn.org/tennessee-financial-aid/) (TSAA). The landscape is merit-heavy at four-year schools but unusually strong for [tuition-free two-year options](https://www.grantforward.com/sponsor/detail/tennessee-student-assistance-corporation-20539) and adult learners.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Tennessee HOPE Scholarship (TELS Family)

- **Type:** Merit-based lottery scholarship
- **Award Amount:** [Roughly \$2,250–\$2,850 per semester](https://www.collegeraptor.com/paying-for-college/articles/financial-aid/tennessee-college-grants/) at four-year schools, and up to about \$1,600 per semester at two-year institutions, with higher amounts for juniors/seniors.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Tennessee resident; graduate from an [eligible TN high school](https://www.collegefortn.org/tennessee-financial-aid/).
    - Enter college within [16 months of high school graduation](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/) at an eligible TN institution.
    - Minimum [21 ACT or 1060 SAT](https://www.collegeraptor.com/paying-for-college/articles/financial-aid/tennessee-college-grants/) OR 3.0+ high school GPA.
    - Typically full-time enrollment in an undergraduate degree program.
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - [Tennessee public universities](https://www.collegeraptor.com/paying-for-college/articles/financial-aid/tennessee-college-grants/) and community colleges.
    - Eligible [Tennessee private colleges](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/).
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Renewal requires [minimum college GPA and progression benchmarks](https://www.collegeraptor.com/paying-for-college/articles/financial-aid/tennessee-college-grants/) (checked at 24, 48, 72 hours etc.).
    - Supplements like the [General Assembly Merit Scholarship](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/) (GAMS) and Aspire Award stack for high-achieving or lower-income HOPE recipients.
- **Application Process:**
    - [Complete FAFSA](https://www.collegefortn.org/tennessee-financial-aid/) (or FAFSA-equivalent) by state deadlines; this serves as the HOPE application.
    - No separate HOPE form for most students; [eligibility is determined automatically](https://www.tnstate.edu/financial_aid/grants.aspx) by TSAC.
- **Deadline:**
    - Students should file FAFSA as early as possible; state guidance commonly cites an [April 15 priority date](https://www.collegefortn.org/tennessee-financial-aid/) for lottery and TSAA consideration.


#### Tennessee Promise

- **Type:** Last-dollar tuition/fee program for recent high school graduates at community colleges/TCATs
- **Award Amount:** [Covers remaining tuition and mandatory fees](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/) at eligible community colleges and Tennessee Colleges of Applied Technology (TCATs) after other state/federal grants.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - [Tennessee resident high school senior](https://www.collegefortn.org/tennessee-financial-aid/) (or equivalent) enrolling as a first-time college student in an eligible program.
    - Attend mandatory meetings and complete [required community service hours](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/).
    - Enroll full-time at an eligible community college or TCAT.
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - [Tennessee community colleges](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/) and TCATs.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - [Last-dollar](https://www.collegefortn.org/tennessee-financial-aid/): pays only after Pell, HOPE, TSAA and other state aid are applied.
    - Limited to approved associate-degree and certificate programs; does not [directly cover four-year tuition](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/).
    - Continuous enrollment and completion of annual community service are required to renew.
- **Application Process:**
    - Complete both [FAFSA and a separate Tennessee Promise application](https://www.collegefortn.org/tennessee-financial-aid/) via the TSAC/CollegeforTN portal.
- **Deadline:**
    - Application and FAFSA [deadlines fall in the student’s senior year](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/); exact dates change annually.


#### Tennessee Reconnect Grant

- **Type:** Last-dollar adult tuition program
- **Award Amount:** Covers remaining tuition and mandatory fees at [eligible community colleges for independent-adult students](https://www.collegefortn.org/tennessee-financial-aid/) after other aid.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Classified as [“independent” on FAFSA](https://www.collegefortn.org/tennessee-financial-aid/) (typically age 24+ or meeting other criteria).
    - Tennessee resident [without a prior bachelor’s degree](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/).
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - [Eligible Tennessee community colleges](https://www.collegefortn.org/tennessee-financial-aid/); some public two-year institutions.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Last-dollar: requires [use of Pell and other grants](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/) first.
    - Student must maintain satisfactory academic progress and continuous enrollment.
- **Application Process:**
    - [File FAFSA each year](https://www.collegefortn.org/tennessee-financial-aid/) and complete a Tennessee Reconnect application via state portals.
- **Deadline:**
    - Rolling, but students should [complete steps](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/) ahead of institutional priority dates.


#### Tennessee Student Assistance Award (TSAA)

- **Type:** Need-based state grant
- **Award Amount:** [Up to about \$2,000 annually](https://www.tnstate.edu/financial_aid/grants.aspx) at public institutions; up to about \$4,000 at eligible privates.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Tennessee resident undergraduate [pursuing first bachelor’s degree](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/).
    - Demonstrated financial need via FAFSA; awards [targeted to lower-income students](https://www.tnstate.edu/financial_aid/grants.aspx).
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - [Tennessee public universities](https://www.tnstate.edu/financial_aid/grants.aspx), community colleges, and eligible private colleges.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - [First-come, first-served](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/) with a limited appropriation — many late filers are waitlisted.
    - Stacks with HOPE and Promise, but does not cover full cost of attendance alone.
- **Application Process:**
    - [FAFSA is the only application](https://www.tnstate.edu/financial_aid/grants.aspx); aid is administered by TSAC through institutions.
- **Deadline:**
    - [Priority consideration](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/) when FAFSA is filed by around April 15.


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs (Tennessee)

- [Wilder-Naifeh Technical Skills Grant](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/) offers up to about \$2,000 per year for students at TCATs, regardless of income.
- [General Assembly Merit Scholarship](https://www.collegeraptor.com/paying-for-college/articles/financial-aid/tennessee-college-grants/) (GAMS) and Aspire Award provide extra merit or need-based supplements on top of HOPE.
- Helping Heroes and other special-purpose scholarships target veterans and National Guard.


### Key Insights for This State

- Tennessee is “aid-rich,” especially for students starting at [community colleges or TCATs](https://www.collegefortn.org/tennessee-financial-aid/), which can make two-year programs nearly tuition-free.
- HOPE plus TSAA plus institutional aid can significantly reduce four-year costs, but [HOPE is merit-centric](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/tennessee/); students just below thresholds lose a large benefit.
- TSAA and some TSAC programs are strictly [first‑come‑first‑served](https://www.tnstate.edu/financial_aid/grants.aspx) — filing FAFSA well before April 15 is critical.
- [Adult learners are unusually well served](https://www.collegefortn.org/tennessee-financial-aid/) compared with other states thanks to Tennessee Reconnect.

## Texas

### Overview

Texas offers solid but competitive [need-based state grants](https://www.uh.edu/financial/undergraduate/types-aid/grants/index.php) centered on the TEXAS Grant, along with campus-administered state-funded grants like the [Texas Public Educational Grant](https://www.uhd.edu/financial/types/financial-types-grants.aspx). Awards can be substantial at public universities, but state funds are [limited and strongly tied](https://reportcenter.highered.texas.gov/agency-publication/guidelines-manuals/toward-excellence-access-success-texas-fy-2026-program-guidelines/) to early FAFSA/TASFA completion and [institutional priorities](https://www.uta.edu/administration/fao/grants/texas-grants).

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Toward EXcellence, Access, and Success (TEXAS) Grant

- **Type:** Need-based, with academic readiness requirements
- **Award Amount:** At many universities, the [maximum annual award is about \$5,000](https://www.uhd.edu/financial/types/financial-types-grants.aspx) for 2026–27, though exact amounts vary by institution and year.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Texas resident attending an [eligible Texas public university](https://reportcenter.highered.texas.gov/agency-publication/guidelines-manuals/toward-excellence-access-success-texas-fy-2026-program-guidelines/) or health-related institution.
    - Demonstrated high financial need; [2025–26 guidance](https://www.uta.edu/administration/fao/grants/texas-grants) gives priority to students with a Student Aid Index (SAI) ≤ 6,514.
    - First undergraduate degree; no prior bachelor’s.
    - Meet one of several [“pathways”](https://www.hhloans.com/types-of-financial-aid/toward-excellence-access-and-success-grant-program-texas-grant/) (e.g., high school graduation pathway with Recommended/Advanced program, top third of class, or dual credit/AP).
    - Enroll at least [three-quarter time](https://www.uta.edu/administration/fao/grants/texas-grants) (full award at 12+ credits; 75% at 9–11).
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - Texas [public universities](https://reportcenter.highered.texas.gov/agency-publication/guidelines-manuals/toward-excellence-access-success-texas-fy-2026-program-guidelines/) and eligible health-related institutions.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Funding is limited and awarded on a [first-come, first-served basis](https://www.uta.edu/administration/fao/grants/texas-grants), with priority for new students who meet early deadlines.
    - [Renewal requires](https://reportcenter.highered.texas.gov/agency-publication/guidelines-manuals/toward-excellence-access-success-texas-fy-2026-program-guidelines/) meeting ongoing need tests and minimum college GPA benchmarks.
- **Application Process:**
    - [FAFSA (or TASFA](https://onestop.txst.edu/financial-aid/learn-more/types/grants.html) for certain noncitizen residents) is the only application; no separate state form.
- **Deadline:**
    - State [priority deadline for FAFSA/TASFA](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/list/by-state/texas/) is January 15 for maximum consideration.


#### Texas Public Educational Grant (TPEG)

- **Type:** Need-based institutional/state grant
- **Award Amount:** [Varies by institution](https://www.uh.edu/financial/undergraduate/types-aid/grants/index.php); commonly a few hundred to several thousand dollars.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Texas resident or, at some institutions, [nonresident with financial need](https://www.uh.edu/financial/undergraduate/types-aid/grants/index.php), as determined by FAFSA/TASFA.
    - Enrolled at least half-time in an eligible program.
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - [Public colleges and universities](https://mycatalog.txstate.edu/undergraduate/general-information/financial-aid-scholarships/) in Texas that participate in TPEG.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Funding pools are limited; many schools state that [TPEG is first-come, first-served](https://www.uh.edu/financial/undergraduate/types-aid/grants/index.php) until funds are exhausted.
- **Application Process:**
    - File FAFSA/TASFA; the [institution automatically considers](https://www.uh.edu/financial/undergraduate/types-aid/grants/index.php) eligible students.
- **Deadline:**
    - Follow [institutional priority deadlines](https://www.uhd.edu/financial/types/financial-types-grants.aspx), often aligned with the January 15 state date.


#### TEXAS Transfer and Related State Grants (Campus-Administered)

- **Type:** Need-based / transfer-focused state grants
- **Award Amount:** For some schools, up to [several thousand dollars per year](https://www.uhd.edu/financial/types/financial-types-grants.aspx).
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Texas resident [transferring between eligible Texas institutions](https://www.uhd.edu/financial/types/financial-types-grants.aspx), often from a two-year to a four-year college.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Availability and naming can vary by institution; these are genuine [state-funded programs](https://mycatalog.txstate.edu/undergraduate/general-information/financial-aid-scholarships/) but highly campus-specific.
- **Deadline:**
    - Typically follow the [January 15 state priority deadline](https://www.uhd.edu/financial/types/financial-types-grants.aspx).


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs (Texas)

- Some campuses reference specialized state grants for [teacher preparation](https://mycatalog.txstate.edu/undergraduate/general-information/financial-aid-scholarships/) or high-demand fields, but availability and rules differ widely.
- Additional state-supported aid may appear as [“Texas State Tuition Grant,”](https://mycatalog.txstate.edu/undergraduate/general-information/financial-aid-scholarships/) but these operate as institutional programs built on state funds.


### Key Insights for This State

- The [TEXAS Grant is the flagship program](https://onestop.txst.edu/financial-aid/learn-more/types/grants.html) and can significantly reduce tuition for high-need students — but it is competitive and tied to early filing.
- Texas leans more on [campus-administered grants](https://mycatalog.txstate.edu/undergraduate/general-information/financial-aid-scholarships/) than on multiple named statewide scholarships.
- State [priority date of January 15](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/list/by-state/texas/) is crucial; missing it often means losing out on limited-pool funds.
- [Academic readiness](https://reportcenter.highered.texas.gov/agency-publication/guidelines-manuals/toward-excellence-access-success-texas-fy-2026-program-guidelines/) matters for TEXAS Grant initial eligibility, not just financial need.

## Utah

### Overview

Utah operates a growing portfolio of state scholarships, anchored by the [Opportunity Scholarship](https://budget.utah.gov/the-story-on-scholarships/) (merit) and Utah Promise Program (need-based), with additional campus‑level “Promise” initiatives. Aid is moderate but improving, blending merit and last‑dollar designs, with especially notable [tuition-coverage programs](https://dailyutahchronicle.com/2025/11/05/new-financial-aid-initiative-makes-higher-education-more-accessible/) for [low- and middle-income students](https://www.aau.edu/u-cover-tuition-eligible-utah-students-families-earning-less-100000).

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Opportunity Scholarship (Utah System of Higher Education)

- **Type:** Merit-based statewide scholarship
- **Award Amount:** In recent reporting it is the largest state scholarship program, [averaging around \$4,176](https://budget.utah.gov/the-story-on-scholarships/) per recipient annually.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Utah resident high school graduate meeting [statutory academic criteria](https://budget.utah.gov/the-story-on-scholarships/) (rigorous high school coursework and minimum GPA/test benchmarks).
    - Enrolled in an eligible Utah public college/university; some private partners may participate.
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - Institutions within the [Utah System of Higher Education (USHE)](https://budget.utah.gov/the-story-on-scholarships/) that participate in the program.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Merit-based; not targeted explicitly by income, though many recipients also receive need-based aid.
- **Application Process:**
    - State-level application through [USHE/Utah Board of Higher Education](https://budget.utah.gov/the-story-on-scholarships/), plus institutional admission.
- **Deadline:**
    - Annual deadlines set by USHE; typically late fall–early spring for the upcoming academic year.


#### Utah Promise Program (Statewide, USHE)

- **Type:** Need-based, last-dollar scholarship program
- **Award Amount:** Average award reported [around \$1,791](https://budget.utah.gov/the-story-on-scholarships/), structured as a last‑dollar grant after other aid.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Utah resident undergraduate student with [demonstrated financial need](https://budget.utah.gov/the-story-on-scholarships/); priority for lower-income students.
    - Enrolled at a participating Utah public institution.
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - Participating public colleges and universities in [Utah’s USHE system](https://budget.utah.gov/the-story-on-scholarships/).
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Last-dollar design: covers [remaining tuition/fee gaps](https://budget.utah.gov/the-story-on-scholarships/) after federal and other grants/scholarships.
- **Application Process:**
    - File FAFSA and institutional aid applications; institutions award Utah Promise based on state guidelines.


#### University of Utah “Utah Promise Scholarship” (Institutional but High-Impact)

- **Type:** Last-dollar tuition guarantee (merit + need hybrid) at a flagship campus
- **Award Amount:** Guarantees [full coverage of tuition and mandatory fees](https://financialaid.utah.edu/types-of-aid/scholarships/freshman/utah-promise-scholarship.php) for up to four years of full-time enrollment.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - First-time, first-year undergraduate admitted to the [University of Utah](https://financialaid.utah.edu/types-of-aid/scholarships/freshman/utah-promise-scholarship.php) for fall 2026.
    - Minimum [3.50 unweighted high school GPA](https://dailyutahchronicle.com/2025/11/05/new-financial-aid-initiative-makes-higher-education-more-accessible/).
    - Family income and assets each [under \$100,000 per year](https://www.aau.edu/u-cover-tuition-eligible-utah-students-families-earning-less-100000) as defined by federal aid guidelines.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - [Last-dollar](https://financialaid.utah.edu/types-of-aid/scholarships/freshman/utah-promise-scholarship.php): scholarship amount varies depending on other grants; if other aid fully covers tuition, the award may be \$0 but still counts toward the eight-semester limit.
    - Does not generally cover course fees, e‑book fees, or most Learning Abroad programs.
- **Application Process:**
    - Apply for admission to the University of Utah by the [Dec. 1 priority deadline](https://dailyutahchronicle.com/2025/11/05/new-financial-aid-initiative-makes-higher-education-more-accessible/).
    - [File FAFSA](https://financialaid.utah.edu/types-of-aid/scholarships/freshman/utah-promise-scholarship.php) by Feb. 1 and complete any verification requirements.


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs (Utah)

- Utah funds several smaller programs like the [T.H. Bell Education Scholarship](https://budget.utah.gov/the-story-on-scholarships/) for future teachers and Talent Development Grants.
- Veterans Tuition Gap Program and public-safety-related scholarships support targeted populations with larger per-student awards.


### Key Insights for This State

- Utah’s [Opportunity Scholarship is large and merit-focused](https://budget.utah.gov/the-story-on-scholarships/), so academically strong students can get significant help even at moderate income levels.
- The statewide Utah Promise Program and the University of Utah’s initiative push the system toward [last-dollar coverage](https://www.aau.edu/u-cover-tuition-eligible-utah-students-families-earning-less-100000) for low- and middle-income families.
- Many programs require early FAFSA filing; missing those dates can [invalidate scholarship eligibility](https://financialaid.utah.edu/types-of-aid/scholarships/freshman/utah-promise-scholarship.php) even if you otherwise qualify.
- Aid is becoming [more favorable for low-income and first-generation students](https://dailyutahchronicle.com/2025/11/05/new-financial-aid-initiative-makes-higher-education-more-accessible/) than in the past.

## Vermont

### Overview

Vermont is strong for need-based and tuition-guarantee programs, especially for low- and moderate-income residents through the [Vermont Grant](https://ljfo.vermont.gov/assets/Uploads/WScott-GilesVSAC-FY26-Budget-Presentation2-21-2025.pdf) and [802Opportunity](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/vermont/). Aid is heavily coordinated through [VSAC (Vermont Student Assistance Corporation)](https://www.vsac.org/pay/student-aid-options/scholarships), which provides a [unified scholarship system](https://scholarshipbuddytexas.com/scholarships/21672/vermont-student-assistance-corporation-scholarships) and a one-stop application for state grants.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Vermont Grant (VSAC)

- **Type:** Need-based grant for undergraduate study
- **Award Amount:** Varies by need, cost of attendance, and timing; awards can be [substantial for lower-income students](https://ljfo.vermont.gov/assets/Uploads/WScott-GilesVSAC-FY26-Budget-Presentation2-21-2025.pdf).
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - [Vermont resident](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/vermont/) enrolled full- or part-time in an undergraduate degree program (in-state or out-of-state).
    - Demonstrated financial need requires [FAFSA and VSAC grant application](https://ljfo.vermont.gov/assets/Uploads/WScott-GilesVSAC-FY26-Budget-Presentation2-21-2025.pdf).
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - Vermont colleges and universities, as well as [eligible out-of-state institutions](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/vermont/) (grant is partially portable).
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Rolling review and effectively [first-come while funds last](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/vermont/); early application is explicitly urged.
- **Application Process:**
    - File FAFSA and complete the [Vermont Grant application via VSAC](https://www.vsac.org/pay/student-aid-options/scholarships).

#### 802Opportunity – Free Community College of Vermont (CCV) Tuition

- **Type:** Last-dollar tuition-free program at CCV
- **Award Amount:** Covers CCV tuition and the standard admin fee.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Vermont resident with family adjusted gross [income ≤ \$100,000](https://ljfo.vermont.gov/assets/Uploads/WScott-GilesVSAC-FY26-Budget-Presentation2-21-2025.pdf).
    - No prior bachelor’s degree and enrolled at CCV in an [eligible program](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/vermont/).
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Last-dollar: requires [completion of FAFSA and VT Grant application](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/vermont/); applied after other grants.

#### VSAC-Assisted Scholarships (Unified Scholarship Application)

- **Type:** Mixed merit/need scholarships, administered through a single application
- **Award Amount:** VSAC manages [150+ scholarships](https://www.vsac.org/pay/student-aid-options/scholarships), many around \$1,000–\$5,000; some reach higher amounts with roughly [150 awards available in a cycle](https://scholarshipbuddytexas.com/scholarships/21672/vermont-student-assistance-corporation-scholarships).
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Generally [Vermont residents](https://scholarshipbuddytexas.com/scholarships/21672/vermont-student-assistance-corporation-scholarships) planning college or training in 2026–27.
- **Application Process:**
    - Create a [MyVSAC account](https://www.vsac.org/pay/student-aid-options/scholarships) and complete the [Unified Scholarship Application](https://scholarshipbuddytexas.com/scholarships/21672/vermont-student-assistance-corporation-scholarships) online.
- **Deadline:**
    - For 2026–27, the deadline is [February 11, 2026](https://scholarshipbuddytexas.com/scholarships/21672/vermont-student-assistance-corporation-scholarships).

#### Vermont National Guard Tuition Benefit Program

- **Type:** Military tuition benefit, often up to full tuition
- **Award Amount:** Up to [full tuition at Vermont State University](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/vermont/) and other participating institutions.
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - [Vermont State University](https://vermontstate.edu/admission/financial-aid/types-of-financial-aid/scholarships/), UVM, and other participating Vermont colleges.

### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs (Vermont)

- [Vermont State University’s “Free Tuition Guarantee”](https://vermontstate.edu/admission/financial-aid/types-of-financial-aid/scholarships/) offers tuition-free attendance for Vermont residents with AGI ≤ \$50,000 enrolling in degree programs.
- The [“Freedom & Unity” program](https://vermontstate.edu/admission/financial-aid/types-of-financial-aid/scholarships/) provides tuition-free VTSU attendance for Vermont students with family income ≤ \$65,000.

### Key Insights for This State

- Vermont is notably strong for low- and moderate-income students through the [Vermont Grant and VTSU tuition guarantees](https://vermontstate.edu/admission/financial-aid/types-of-financial-aid/scholarships/).
- VSAC centralizes most aid, making its [grant and scholarship portals](https://www.vsac.org/pay/student-aid-options/scholarships) essential for applicants.
- [802Opportunity](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/vermont/) effectively makes CCV tuition-free for Vermonters under the \$100,000 AGI threshold.

## Virginia

### Overview

Virginia offers a mix of non-need-based private-college grants (VTAG) and targeted transfer/sector programs, with additional state need-based aid [administered through institutions](https://www.schev.edu/financial-aid/financial-aid/undergraduate-and-graduate-financial-aid-programs). Aid is moderate overall, stronger for students attending [in-state private colleges](https://cicv.org/learn-more/vtag/) and for community-college graduates transferring to four-year schools.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Virginia Tuition Assistance Grant (VTAG)

- **Type:** Non-need-based tuition grant for private colleges
- **Award Amount:** For 2025–26, undergraduate Virginia residents who apply by September 15 [receive \$5,250](https://cicv.org/learn-more/vtag/) for the academic year; graduate health-professions students receive \$5,000.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Virginia resident as defined by [state domicile rules](https://cicv.org/learn-more/vtag/).
    - Full-time enrollment in an [eligible private, nonprofit Virginia college](https://www.schev.edu/financial-aid/financial-aid/undergraduate-and-graduate-financial-aid-programs) or university in an approved degree program.
    - Not enrolled in theology or religious vocational programs.
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - Participating [Virginia private four-year institutions](https://cicv.org/learn-more/vtag/) listed by the Council of Independent Colleges in Virginia (CICV) and SCHEV.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - **Non-need-based:** income [does not affect eligibility](https://cicv.org/learn-more/vtag/), though students must still meet residency and program criteria.
    - Limited to tuition; does not cover fees, room, or board.
- **Application Process:**
    - Submit [VTAG application](https://cicv.org/learn-more/vtag/) to the financial aid office of the participating institution.
- **Deadline:**
    - **September 15** for full-year consideration; late applications may receive reduced or no funding depending on appropriation.

#### Two-Year College Transfer Grant

- **Type:** Hybrid merit/need transfer grant
- **Award Amount:** [Up to \$1,000 per year](https://www.schev.edu/financial-aid/financial-aid/undergraduate-and-graduate-financial-aid-programs), with an additional \$1,000 for students in select high-demand programs.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Virginia resident [enrolled full-time](https://www.schev.edu/financial-aid/financial-aid/undergraduate-and-graduate-financial-aid-programs) at a participating four-year public or private institution.
    - Must transfer within one year of receiving an [associate degree](https://www.schev.edu/financial-aid/financial-aid/undergraduate-and-graduate-financial-aid-programs) from a Virginia two-year public college.
    - Minimum **3.0 GPA** on the associate degree.
    - Expected Family Contribution (EFC) at or [below 12,000](https://www.schev.edu/financial-aid/financial-aid/undergraduate-and-graduate-financial-aid-programs) (or equivalent index).
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Designed specifically for community-college transfer students; not available to native four-year entrants.

### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs (Virginia)

- SCHEV also oversees other [undergraduate and graduate financial aid programs](https://www.vpcc.edu/admissions/financial-aid/grant-programs/) including need-based awards for public institutions.
- [Military and survivor benefits](https://www.scholarships.com/financial-aid/college-scholarships/scholarship-directory/residence-state/virginia) such as the Virginia Military Survivors & Dependents Education Program offer significant assistance for qualifying families.

### Key Insights for This State

- **VTAG** is a standout benefit for residents choosing private colleges, effectively discounting tuition by [over \$5,000 per year](https://cicv.org/learn-more/vtag/) without income testing.
- Community-college students who complete an associate degree with a strong GPA can leverage the [Transfer Grant](https://www.schev.edu/financial-aid/financial-aid/undergraduate-and-graduate-financial-aid-programs) to reduce costs.
- Much of the state’s true need-based aid for public universities is channeled through [institutional grants](https://www.vpcc.edu/admissions/financial-aid/grant-programs/), so outcomes vary by campus.
- Deadlines (especially VTAG’s **September 15** cutoff) are strict; missing them may result in losing an entire year of eligibility.

## Washington

### Overview

Washington is one of the [most generous need-based aid states](https://www.gradetograd.com/blog/washington-college-grant-2025-guide), anchored by the Washington College Grant plus the College Bound Scholarship and a [strong STEM scholarship](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/washington/) (WSOS). Aid is heavily income-tested, with [full-tuition coverage](https://wsac.wa.gov/wcg) at community and technical colleges for the lowest-income students and substantial support at four-year schools.

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Washington College Grant (WCG)

- **Type:** Need-based grant
- **Award Amount:** For low-income students, can [cover 100% of tuition](https://wsac.wa.gov/wcg) at Washington community and technical colleges and [around \$10,000–\$12,000 per year](https://www.gradetograd.com/blog/washington-college-grant-2025-guide) at four-year institutions; partial awards scale down with income.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - [Washington resident](https://wsac.wa.gov/wcg).
    - Enrolled in an [eligible Washington college](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/washington/), technical program, or registered apprenticeship.
    - Income-based eligibility tied to [Median Family Income (MFI)](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/washington/): families of four earning up to about \$78,500 can receive a full award.
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - Washington [public universities and community/technical colleges](https://wsac.wa.gov/wcg).
    - Many [private nonprofit institutions](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/washington/) and some apprenticeship programs.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - [Fiscal changes from 2025–26](https://financialaid.wsu.edu/2025/06/05/2025-26-changes-to-washington-state-aid/) tightened maximum-award targeting but broadened eligibility for some income bands.
    - Replaces and expands the prior State Need Grant; widely viewed as [one of the most generous](https://www.gradetograd.com/blog/washington-college-grant-2025-guide) state grants nationally.
- **Application Process:**
    - File FAFSA or, for undocumented students, [WASFA (Washington Application for State Financial Aid)](https://wsac.wa.gov/wcg).
- **Deadline:**
    - Students are [urged to file FAFSA/WASFA](https://financialaid.wsu.edu/2025/06/05/2025-26-changes-to-washington-state-aid/) as early as possible because some sectors have limited funds.


#### College Bound Scholarship

- **Type:** Last-dollar tuition and fee scholarship for low-income students who sign up in middle school
- **Award Amount:** Combined with WCG, [covers tuition (at public rates)](https://wsac.wa.gov/college-bound), some fees, and a small book allowance; maximum award tables list [institution-specific caps](https://wsac.wa.gov/college-bound).
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - [Sign the College Bound pledge](https://wsac.wa.gov/college-bound) in middle school (by grade 8).
    - Graduate from a [Washington high school](https://financialaid.wsu.edu/2025/06/05/2025-26-changes-to-washington-state-aid/) or earn a Washington GED.
    - Enroll in a college within a limited window; [recent policy changes](https://financialaid.wsu.edu/2025/06/05/2025-26-changes-to-washington-state-aid/) extended the maximum timeframe to six years.
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - Washington [public two- and four-year institutions](https://wsac.wa.gov/college-bound) and eligible private colleges.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - **Last-dollar** relative to WCG—[College Bound fills remaining tuition/fee gaps](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/washington/).
- **Application Process:**
    - Initial [pledge/registration in middle school](https://wsac.wa.gov/college-bound) through schools/WSAC.
    - Later, file FAFSA/WASFA and list Washington institutions.


#### Washington State Opportunity Scholarship (WSOS)

- **Type:** Merit/need hybrid, STEM and health workforce scholarship
- **Award Amount:** Multi-year awards can reach [several thousand dollars per year](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/washington/) toward tuition and living expenses.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Washington resident pursuing [eligible STEM/health-care degrees](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/washington/).
- **Application Process:**
    - [Separate WSOS application](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/washington/) in addition to FAFSA/WASFA.


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs (Washington)

- [Passport to Careers](https://financialaid.wsu.edu/2025/06/05/2025-26-changes-to-washington-state-aid/) supports current and former foster youth and unaccompanied homeless youth.
- [State Work Study](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/washington/) and National Guard Grants provide additional service-linked aid.


### Key Insights for This State

- Washington is among the [best states for low-income students](https://www.gradetograd.com/blog/washington-college-grant-2025-guide); WCG plus College Bound can [virtually eliminate tuition](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/washington/) at many institutions.
- [WASFA makes state aid accessible](https://wsac.wa.gov/wcg) to many students who cannot file FAFSA.
- [College Bound requires action](https://wsac.wa.gov/college-bound) as early as middle school; families who miss the pledge window cannot retroactively qualify.

## West Virginia

### Overview

West Virginia combines a [strong merit scholarship](https://www.weirtondailytimes.com/news/local-news/2026/02/deadlines-approaching-for-promise-scholarship-west-virginia-higher-education-grant-program/) (PROMISE) with a solid need-based grant (Higher Education Grant) and a [last-dollar community/technical college program](https://cfwvconnect.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Financial-Aid-Fact-Sheet-2025-2026.pdf) (WV Invests).
Aid is [generous relative to tuition levels](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/west-virginia/), but heavily deadline-driven, especially for [PROMISE](https://www.cfwv.com/financial-aid/promise-scholarship/apply-now/).

### Major State Aid Programs

#### PROMISE Scholarship

- **Type:** Merit-based scholarship
- **Award Amount:** [Up to \$5,500 per year](https://www.weirtondailytimes.com/news/local-news/2026/02/deadlines-approaching-for-promise-scholarship-west-virginia-higher-education-grant-program/) toward tuition and mandatory fees at eligible institutions.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - [West Virginia resident](https://wvctcs.edu/deadlines-approaching-for-promise-scholarship-west-virginia-higher-education-grant-program/) high school senior.
    - Minimum [3.0 overall and 3.0 core GPA](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/west-virginia/) in high school.
    - [ACT: at least 21 composite](https://www.weirtondailytimes.com/news/local-news/2026/02/deadlines-approaching-for-promise-scholarship-west-virginia-higher-education-grant-program/) or SAT: 1080 total with specific sub-score minimums.
    - Enroll full-time at an [eligible West Virginia institution](https://www.cfwv.com/financial-aid/promise-scholarship/apply-now/).
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - [West Virginia public colleges](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/west-virginia/) and universities, and eligible in-state independent colleges.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Students have [until June SAT/July ACT dates](https://www.weirtondailytimes.com/news/local-news/2026/02/deadlines-approaching-for-promise-scholarship-west-virginia-higher-education-grant-program/) to secure qualifying test scores for a given cohort.
    - [Late applications](https://www.cfwv.com/financial-aid/promise-scholarship/apply-now/) are accepted but awards are contingent on available funding.
- **Application Process:**
    - Submit the PROMISE Scholarship application via the [West Virginia Student Aid Management](https://www.cfwv.com/financial-aid/promise-scholarship/apply-now/) (WVSAM) portal.
    - File the FAFSA by the [March 1 deadline](https://www.weirtondailytimes.com/news/local-news/2026/02/deadlines-approaching-for-promise-scholarship-west-virginia-higher-education-grant-program/).
- **Deadline:**
    - For 2026–27, PROMISE application and FAFSA [were due March 1, 2026](https://cfwvconnect.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Financial-Aid-Fact-Sheet-2025-2026.pdf).


#### West Virginia Higher Education Grant Program (HEGP)

- **Type:** Need-based state grant
- **Award Amount:** [Up to \$3,400 per year](https://wvctcs.edu/deadlines-approaching-for-promise-scholarship-west-virginia-higher-education-grant-program/) at eligible in-state institutions.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - West Virginia resident undergraduate student with [financial need](https://wvctcs.edu/deadlines-approaching-for-promise-scholarship-west-virginia-higher-education-grant-program/).
    - Enrolled [at least half-time](https://cfwvconnect.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Financial-Aid-Fact-Sheet-2025-2026.pdf) in an eligible institution.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - [Priority given](https://cfwvconnect.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Financial-Aid-Fact-Sheet-2025-2026.pdf) to students who file FAFSA by April 15.
- **Deadline:**
    - [FAFSA priority deadline April 15](https://www.weirtondailytimes.com/news/local-news/2026/02/deadlines-approaching-for-promise-scholarship-west-virginia-higher-education-grant-program/) for the upcoming academic year.


#### WV Invests Grant

- **Type:** Last-dollar grant for community and technical college programs
- **Award Amount:** [Covers tuition and mandatory fees](https://cfwvconnect.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Financial-Aid-Fact-Sheet-2025-2026.pdf) for eligible programs at participating community and technical colleges.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - [West Virginia resident](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/west-virginia/) enrolled at least six credit hours.
    - Must [pass a drug screening](https://cfwvconnect.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Financial-Aid-Fact-Sheet-2025-2026.pdf) and perform at least two hours of community service each term.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Last-dollar: applies only [after other federal and state grants](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/west-virginia/).
- **Deadline:**
    - [Priority consideration](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/west-virginia/) for students who complete steps by April 15.


### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs (West Virginia)

- [HEAPS Grant and HEAPS Workforce](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/west-virginia/) provide grants for part-time and workforce training students.
- [Underwood-Smith Teaching Scholars](https://scholarshipsandgrants.us/state/west-virginia/) provide substantial stipends in exchange for teaching commitments in-state.


### Key Insights for This State

- PROMISE plus HEGP can cover a large share of tuition, making WV [relatively affordable](https://www.weirtondailytimes.com/news/local-news/2026/02/deadlines-approaching-for-promise-scholarship-west-virginia-higher-education-grant-program/) for academically strong students.
- WV Invests is especially attractive for technical degrees, but the [drug screening and community service](https://cfwvconnect.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Financial-Aid-Fact-Sheet-2025-2026.pdf) requirements are non-negotiable.
- [March 1 and April 15](https://www.weirtondailytimes.com/news/local-news/2026/02/deadlines-approaching-for-promise-scholarship-west-virginia-higher-education-grant-program/) are critical deadlines for maximum funding consideration.

## Wisconsin

### Overview

Wisconsin provides a broad set of grants and scholarships across sectors, including the **Wisconsin Grant** (need-based), **Talent Incentive Program**, **Academic and Technical Excellence Scholarships**, and multiple tuition-promise initiatives. It is particularly favorable for in-state students attending **UW System** or technical colleges, especially those from low-income families or with strong academics.

---

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Wisconsin Grant (UW, Technical, Private, Tribal)

- **Type:** Need-based grant across sectors.
- **Award Amount:** Maximum levels vary by sector and year; awards are set annually by the **Higher Educational Aids Board (HEAB)** and are renewable up to **10 semesters** for many students.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Wisconsin resident undergraduate with financial need.
    - Enrolled at least half-time at a participating UW institution, **Wisconsin Technical College System (WTCS)** college, eligible private nonprofit, or tribal college.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Award formulas differ significantly by sector (UW vs. Technical vs. Private vs. Tribal).
- **Deadline:**
    - Follow institutional priority FAFSA deadlines. Earlier filing is critical as funds are often allocated on a first-come, first-served basis.

#### Talent Incentive Program (TIP) Grant

- **Type:** Need-based, targeted at the most financially needy and educationally disadvantaged students.
- **Award Amount:** Typically ranges from **\$600 to \$1,800** per year.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Wisconsin resident undergraduate with exceptional financial need and a disadvantaged background.
    - **First-time freshmen** are typically nominated for the grant by school financial aid offices or **Wisconsin Educational Opportunity Programs (WEOP)** counselors.
- **Key Restrictions / Notes:**
    - Eligibility is limited to **10 semesters**. Continuing students must continue to show financial need.
- **Application Process:**
    - Requires FAFSA; often involves a nomination from financial aid staff or WEOP.

#### Academic Excellence Scholarship (AES)

- **Type:** Merit-based.
- **Award Amount:** **\$2,250 per year** (half funded by the state, half matched by the institution) for up to 8 semesters.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Awarded to Wisconsin high school seniors with the **highest GPA** in each public and private high school.
    - **New for 2026:** Under **Act 80**, high schools with 1–499 students now receive **one guaranteed scholarship** (replacing the old "pool" system for small schools).
    - Homeschooled students are now eligible with an **ACT score of 33+** or **SAT score of 1500+**.
- **Deadline:**
    - High schools must submit nominations; for 2026, nominations closed **March 13, 2026**.

#### Technical Excellence Scholarship (TES)

- **Type:** Merit-based for technical education proficiency.
- **Award Amount:** Up to **\$2,250 per year** for up to 6 semesters at WTCS institutions.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Awarded to seniors who demonstrate the highest level of proficiency in technical education subjects (e.g., DECA, FFA, SkillsUSA involvement).
- **Deadline:**
    - For 2026, typical application deadlines to high school counseling offices were in **mid-February**.

#### Wisconsin Tuition Promise (UW System, excluding UW–Madison)

- **Type:** Last-dollar tuition and fee guarantee.
- **Award Amount:** Covers the "gap" in tuition and segregated fees after federal and state grants are applied.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Wisconsin resident; first-time bachelor's or associate degree student (includes transfers).
    - Family **Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) ≤ \$55,000**.
    - Must be enrolled full-time (12+ credits).
- **Where It Can Be Used:**
    - Participating UW System campuses (excluding Madison).

---

### Lesser-Known or Supplemental Programs

* **Bucky’s Tuition Promise (UW–Madison):** A flagship guarantee for incoming freshmen/transfers with a family **AGI ≤ \$65,000**.
* **Bucky’s Pell Pathway:** An expansion that guarantees to meet the **full financial need** (including housing and books) for all Federal Pell Grant-eligible students at UW–Madison.
* **Indian Student Assistance Grant:** For residents who are at least 25% Native American; requires a separate HEAB application.
* **Hearing & Visually Handicapped Student Grant:** Need-based aid for students with severe hearing or visual impairments.

---

### Key Insights for 2026

* **FAFSA Delays:** For the 2026–27 cycle, UW–Madison and other institutions warned of a **temporary processing delay** for FAFSAs submitted after April 26, 2026, due to federal updates.
* **Expanded Access:** Recent legislative changes have made the **Academic Excellence Scholarship** much easier for students in small rural high schools and homeschooled environments to access.
* **Income Thresholds:** Always check the specific AGI limit for your target campus; while the system-wide Promise uses **\$55,000**, UW–Madison's threshold is higher at **\$65,000**.

**Expert Guide Note:** Are you looking for aid specific to a four-year university or a technical college, as the application processes and scholarship matches (like AES vs. TES) vary between them?

## Wyoming

### Overview

Wyoming’s aid system is anchored by the **Hathaway Scholarship**, a robust merit-based program with a need-based component funded by a state endowment. In **February 2026**, Governor Mark Gordon signed **Senate File 47** into law, significantly increasing award amounts for the first time in several years to help offset tuition inflation. Additionally, the state has recently expanded support for adult learners through the **Wyoming’s Tomorrow Scholarship**.

---

### Major State Aid Programs

#### Hathaway Scholarship Program

- **Type:** Merit-based with a need-based supplement.
- **2026–27 Award Amounts:** Effective July 1, 2026, awards for students enrolled in 12+ credits have increased as follows:
    - **Honors Level:** **\$2,360 per semester** (up from \$1,680).
    - **Performance Level:** **\$1,770 per semester** (up from \$1,260).
    - **Opportunity/Provisional Levels:** **\$1,180 per semester** (up from \$840).
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - **Merit Tiers:** Determined by a combination of high school GPA (2.50 to 3.50+) and ACT scores (17 to 25+).
    - **Curriculum:** Students must complete the **Hathaway Success Curriculum** (specific years of math, science, and foreign language).
    - **Residency:** Must be a Wyoming resident and initiate the scholarship within four years of high school graduation (or by age 21 for homeschoolers).
- **Need-Based Supplement:**
    - For students with "unmet need" (calculated after other aid and a \$2,000 student commitment), the Hathaway Need Grant has increased to a maximum of **\$2,120 per year** (up from \$1,575).
- **Where It Can Be Used:** University of Wyoming and Wyoming's seven public community colleges.

#### Wyoming’s Tomorrow Scholarship

- **Type:** Need-based scholarship for adult learners.
- **Award Amount:** Up to **\$1,800 per semester** (\$3,600 per year) for a lifetime maximum of \$7,200.
- **Eligibility Requirements:**
    - Must be a Wyoming resident **aged 24 or older**.
    - Must file the FAFSA and demonstrate unmet financial need.
    - Must register with the Wyoming Department of Workforce Services (**hire.wyo.gov**).
- **Key Restrictions:** Cannot be used simultaneously with a Hathaway Scholarship.

#### Wyoming Works

- **Type:** Grant for career and technical education (CTE).
- **Award Amount:** Up to **\$1,680 annually** (Standard Grant) or **\$3,360 annually** (Critical Grant for high-demand fields).
- **Eligibility Requirements:** Adult students in approved certificate or degree programs at community colleges who do not qualify for Hathaway.

---

### Key Insights for 2026

* **Restored Value:** The **2026 legislative increases** (roughly 40% for merit tiers) were designed to bring the Hathaway Honors award closer to covering a majority of tuition and fees at the University of Wyoming, which is approximately **\$2,700 per semester** for residents in 2026–27.
* **Success Curriculum is Strict:** Even high-achieving students can lose eligibility if they miss a single required "Success Curriculum" course in high school; there is no retroactive "fix" after graduation.
* **The "Six-Year Clock":** Once you initiate your Hathaway Scholarship, you have **six years** to use your eight semesters of eligibility. 
* **Adult Learner Focus:** With the full implementation of **Wyoming’s Tomorrow**, the state has significantly shifted its focus toward helping adults retrain or complete degrees they started years prior.
